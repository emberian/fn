## 2. What was plainly over-counted, and what was not

**Fixed: the history counted H twice.** The history budget charges a held row its payload octets
plus 320 per membership (`books/store-budget.lisp` `fn-sbud-row-octets`), and it commits at most H of
them. The figure charged the arena at H payload octets and, separately, the memberships at
H / 320. No store the budget admits reaches both. `books/heap-store-figure.lisp` now has:

- `fn-heap-store-history-octets` = the empty arena + 2 H + one page pointer.
- `fn-heap-arena-octets-slope`: an octet of payload costs at most 2 in the arena, plus one pointer
  at a page boundary. It also holds for a cache bound that does not grow with USED, which is
  arena-offheap-3's.
- KEYSTONE `fn-heap-store-history-holds-payload-and-memberships`: USED + 320 M <= H implies
  arena(USED) + 640 M <= the history bound.

The store keystones now take the budget's joint hypothesis in place of two independent ones:
`fn-heap-store-figure-holds-every-store`, `fn-heap-decide-admits-every-store-the-profile-admits`,
`fn-heap-operation-decide-holds-the-store` and `...-at-the-open-trigger`. The teeth moved to the
costliest store the budget admits: H / 320 memberships and 128 octets of payload. That witness is
tight: the figure less 1 MiB fails. The saving is H: 8 MiB on the small preset and 1 TiB on the
default.

Examined and **not** plainly over-counted. These are the ones the proposal has to decide:

- **Records, 2 T x 12 KiB: the largest term, and shape-dependent.** Two curves on the d5ab87aec
  developer-heap image, with the store at T 131,072 and H 512 MiB, 2 KiB articles, and live heap
  measured after a full collection:
  - 28-character Message-IDs: 5.85 KB a post, which is about 3.4 KB a record over the 2,443
    stored octets.
  - 250-character Message-IDs and 900-octet Subjects: 29.4 KB a post while posting and about
    17 KB after a reopen.

  So 12 KiB is 2 to 3 times the need for short headers and **below** the need for long ones. The
  constant is not a bound. Its proper replacement is a charge per record against the budget (P2
  below), not a smaller constant.
- **The image outside the heap (the whole core file, 204 MiB).** The core's dynamic content
  (118 MiB mapped) sits inside the dynamic space and is also inside the file, so it looks counted
  twice. Its mappings on hbox, SBCL 2.6.8, with an 800 MB dynamic space: read-only 38 MiB,
  fixed-object 60 MiB reserved, text 160 MiB reserved. VmSize outside the dynamic space is about
  308 MiB. So the term under-counts virtual memory by about 104 MiB and over-counts resident
  memory, where about 80 MiB of the file is resident. It belongs to the metric split, not to a fix.
- **Threads, 30 x 5 MiB.** 16 control-client threads are a policy cap
  (`*fn-nctrl-max-active-clients*`); 11 threads were measured idle. The reservation has to hold
  the cap, so this is a policy question: 4 control clients would give 18 threads and 90 MiB.
- **Octet buffers, 2 x (3 H + a segment).** The reader's buffer holds the checkpoint file at open
  and is released after the load (`fnn-octets-release`). The publication buffer grows to the
  largest step. Bounding the step, and observing the checkpoint's size, would lower this, but it
  needs a host bound proved first.
- **Open transient beside the in-flight request.** Summing them is a phase over-count only if the
  open's garbage were known to be collected before serving. It is not: see finding F5.

## 3. The admission model: the options and the number each reaches (model; measure at convergence)

P0 is today's worst-case reservation, after this lane:

- Small preset: init 1,179 MiB, launcher 770 to 825 MB. Unmet.
- About 104 MiB of virtual memory is not modelled at all: the text and fixed-object reservations.
- It scales as 24 KiB a transaction slot plus about 11 x H: SYNTH_1M is 54 GB.

P1 keeps the worst case but charges records to the budget, as memberships already are. Each record
is charged its state against H: a fixed part plus its identifiers' octets, from the long-header
curve. The state term then becomes about 2 x H (the heap-per-charge ratio) instead of 2 T x 12 KiB.

- Small preset: 1,179 - 384 + 16 = about 811 MiB.
- SYNTH_1M: about 29.5 GB.
- It is still a reservation, and still far from 256 MiB, because of the image, the threads, the
  buffers at 6 H and the chunk lists.

P2 is the credit model (GPT-6's review, and ember's F8 split). It is implemented and proved here as
a ledger (PRF-380). No host line calls it yet, so PRF-380 cites no events: its theorems are certified but
not yet a claim about the running server. Admission is by credit acquired before
allocation:

    M_base + M_cache + sum_i (U_i + R_i) + E_completion + E_runtime <= B

`books/memory-credits.lisp` provides these operations:

- `fn-mcr-acquire` refuses by name (`memory-budget-exhausted`) exactly when the credit does not
  fit.
- `fn-mcr-grow` is never refused within the reserve (reserve to finish), and the total is
  unchanged.
- `fn-mcr-retain` moves the retained octets to the cache and returns the rest.
- `fn-mcr-evict` returns cache credit only for octets actually evicted.
- `fn-mcr-overdraw` draws only from the separately funded completion reserve.

KEYSTONE `fn-mcr-transitions-keep-funded`. Teeth: a 256 MiB ledger admits 47 article credits and
refuses the 48th by name.

The terms map onto the model's breakdown like this:

- **M_base** is the image resident set, the thread runtime actually used and the collector's
  headroom. Today's image has 118 MiB of dynamic content plus about 80 MiB of resident file
  pages; image-floor's stripping took the load requirement to 74 MiB.
- **Credits** for requests are zero-copy-commit's article slots (PRF-377): 32 at 1.52 MiB is
  48.5 MiB at the small preset. The same credit follows the buffer from ingress through the queue
  to the commit.
- **M_cache** is the retained state actually held, charged per record (P1's charge) and
  reclaimable only on eviction.
- **E_completion** is the open or recovery reserve, and **E_runtime** the SBCL runtime's own
  reserve.

Under P2 a 1,000-post small store charges what it holds: about 7 MiB of state at short headers
(measured), plus the base and the credits. Whether that is under 256 MiB accountable and 128 MiB
working set is the convergence measurement. The model says the base alone is about 200 MiB with
today's image and about 120 MiB with image-floor's stripping. So P2 reaches the target only with
the stripped image and a small collector headroom: the 8 MiB served trigger rather than the 64 MiB
cap.

**What the page store removes** (the history off-heap):

- The history term (payload), and the records term to the extent the columns move off the heap.
- The open's suffix vectors, and the per-record build if the index is paged.
- The octet buffers: page writes instead of a whole-file buffer.

What is left is base plus credits plus cache, which is P2's shape. Under P0 the page store would
shrink SYNTH_1M from 54 GB to roughly the records term. Under P2 it turns the retained state into
cache credit.

**F8 metric (adopted):**

- The old reserved verdict stays recorded UNMET: 1,179 MiB. It cannot be met, because the image
  and threads alone are 354 MiB.
- Virtual address space: today 1,179 MiB plus about 104 MiB of unmodelled reservation.
- Accountable physical memory (anonymous, plus attributable resident file pages, plus kernel and
  socket memory, cgroup-style): target 256 MiB under the named small profile and a named
  active-request mix.
- Working set: target at most 128 MiB.
- **Measure at convergence.** None of these three is claimed here.

## 4. Findings

- **F1 (soundness, open).** A 100,000 x 2 KiB store (T 131,072, H 512 MiB; 428 MB on disk:
  checkpoint 144 MB and journal 268 MB; checkpoint sequence 44,908, suffix 19,082) did **not
  reopen**:
  - at 4 GiB, "Heap exhausted during garbage collection";
  - at the launcher's own figure, 9,444 MB (MemoryMax 14G), the same `FN-SRS-DECODE` backtrace.

  The live state before the reopen was 926 MB. So the figure does not hold this store's open, or
  the open has an unbounded path, either the heap or a deep `fn-srs-decode` recursion. To
  reproduce, run `tools/fundamentals/f8_curve.py` with its default points on the developer-heap
  image; that takes about 15 minutes. **Blocks any claim that the figure holds every store
  (PRF-198) at T 131,072.** Owner: the open/recover lanes.
- **F2.** Per-record state depends on the header shape: 3.4 KB to 27 KB a record. The 12 KiB
  constant is under the need for long headers (section 2).
- **F3.** The posted state exceeds the reopened state: 1,083 MB against 780 MB for the same 25,000
  long-header records. The posting path retains about 12 KB a record more than a reopen.
- **F4.** Garbage before a full collection reached 181 to 218 MB, more than the collector's room
  of 2 x 64 MiB. Older generations are not in the collector term.
- **F5.** The launcher's figure grows with what is on disk (780 -> 823 MB over 1,000 posts,
  because the open is sized by observation), while init's figure is the full store's.
