# Q11: why expiry cannot return transaction capacity, and the decision it needs (lane expiry, 2026-09-28)

## The finding

A store's transaction budget T (`max_transactions`, the profile) is compared with
`fn-sbud-used` (books/store-budget.lisp), the number of committed records the
Store state holds. Content reclamation (`store reclaim`, and expiry through it,
RET-008) rewrites an article record's payload to its tombstone and keeps the
record: its Message-ID (so a re-offer is refused, never resurrected, D25), its
group numbers (never reused), its sequence and txid. `fn-rclp-events-keep-the-length`
(books/store-reclaim-pack.lisp): the rewritten history has exactly as many
records as the history. So reclaim frees bytes (the history bound H, disk) and
never transactions; a store at T records stays at T.

T is not an accounting error to be fixed in place. It bounds what every record
costs whether or not its payload remains: the open's replay (books/store-replay-bound.lisp),
the heap figure (T x the per-record term, books/heap-store-figure.lisp) and the
checkpoint's generation space. A tombstone record still costs a record there.

## What would return capacity

Only removing records: history compaction H (specs/storage.md, "History classes
and lifetimes"): replace old records by a summary that keeps every future
decision. Two facts make H a decision, not an implementation detail:

1. A summary that keeps every accepted Message-ID grows with every article ever
   accepted, so it only moves the ceiling. Bounded state under indefinite
   operation means FORGETTING old Message-IDs.
2. Forgetting a Message-ID changes a D25 verdict: a later article reusing it is
   accepted as new instead of `:duplicate`/`:conflict`. The standard defence
   (INN's `/remember/` window with its `artcutoff`) refuses any article whose
   own date is older than the remember window, so a genuine re-offer of a
   forgotten article is refused as too old; what remains lost is a FRESH-dated
   article reusing a forgotten Message-ID (a conflict D25 would have recorded).

This is D13 ("When may history and evidence be forgotten?"), which is open:
recommendation "Preserve duplicate history initially; add proved pruning with an
explicit old-input/resurrection policy"; alternative "Finite age/epoch windows
from v1". It also needs a store-core change: the file kernel requires record
sequences contiguous from 0 (books/store-files.lisp `fn-sf-record-listp`, 802
dependents), so a history with forgotten records needs a summary base (or an
epoch with a sequence mapping for consumer positions, which are Store-event
prefixes).

## The recovery path today

`store export`, a fresh `init` with a larger `--max-transactions` (bounded by the
machine's memory: the heap figure), `store import` (docs/operator.md, store
settings).

## For ember (D13)

- (a) Keep D13's recommendation as is: a count-full store is full until
  reinstalled with a larger T; expiry frees disk and bytes only. Nothing to build.
- (b) An operator `remember DAYS` window (INN's): expired-and-reclaimed records
  older than the window are forgotten into a summary (per-group high waters,
  the allocation frontier), and every ingress refuses an article dated before the
  window (RFC 5537 section 3.6 lets a relayer refuse old articles). Stated loss:
  a fresh-dated article reusing a forgotten Message-ID is accepted as new.
  Default: no window (a). Cost: the sequence-base change in the kernel and the
  summary in the checkpoint; a lane of its own after the store-representation
  rows (A1-A7), which own those books.
