## What changed

- `books/memory-credits.lisp` (PRF-380): two transitions the served path
  needs. `fn-mcr-resize` sets an operation's credit: shrinking is never
  refused (reserve to finish), growing is admitted exactly when it fits
  beside everything funded and is otherwise refused
  `:memory-budget-exhausted` by name with the ledger unchanged
  (`fn-mcr-resize-refuses-exactly-past-the-budget`). `fn-mcr-move` hands
  credit from one operation to another with the funded total unchanged
  (`fn-mcr-total-of-move`). KEYSTONE `fn-mcr-resize-and-move-keep-funded`.
- `books/owner-credits.lisp` (new): the served path's use of one ledger a
  run.
  - Keys: `(:conn . ID)` for a connection's body mid-article and its queued
    submissions (one reserve each), `:open` for what the committer took,
    `:sealed` for the batch in flight.
  - Subject: `fn-mca-read-span`, which `host/owner-host.lisp`
    `fn-owner-chunk-span-at` calls in place of `fn-oas-read-span`. It is
    that read, with ID's credit resized to what the read left it needing.
    Past the budget the read is refused by name: a POST 440 at the command,
    else 400 and close with the read not run.
  - The commit: `fn-owner-take` (`fn-mca-take`), `fn-owner-shed-outcome`
    (`fn-mca-untake`), and in `host/native/owner.lisp` the seal after
    `fnn-log-seal-open-batch`, COMPLETE after the barrier, the stop, and the
    settle after a synchronous write or a START that kept no member.
  - The close: `fn-owner-close`, `fn-owner-fault` (`fn-mca-close`).
  - The budget: `fn-owner-connection-budget` installs `fn-mca-initial`.
- Keystones: `fn-mca-read-span-keeps-funded`,
  `fn-mca-read-span-covers-the-connection`,
  `fn-mca-read-span-within-the-credit-unfolds`,
  `fn-mca-read-span-never-blocks-what-is-held` (reserve to finish),
  `fn-mca-close-keeps-what-the-commit-owns`,
  `fn-mca-commit-steps-keep-funded` with `fn-mca-take-and-seal-keep-the-total`
  and `fn-mca-seal-and-batch-done-move-the-batch`, and
  `fn-mca-initial-funds-exactly-the-articles`. The last one says the budget
  is `fn-heap-figure-octets`, the open's terms are the completion reserve,
  the collector's room is the runtime reserve, and exactly
  `fn-heap-articles-octets` is free.

## The defect the credits close (SCN-194)

The native commit pipeline (`host/native/owner.lisp`
`fnn-owner-commit-pipeline`) feeds each member's outcome to the owner in
START, before the batch's barrier. The syncer thread then runs the
fdatasync with the owner released. So while the barrier runs:

- the owner's queue is empty;
- its in-flight field is nil;
- the poster's connection is out of article mode, or already closed if the
  client hung up or timed out.

`fn-oas-held` therefore counted zero, and the slots admitted a new article
body beside a batch the syncer still owned. The same happened at a stall:
members told uncertain are closed, the bytes may still land, and the slots
were free again.

Under credits the batch holds `:sealed` until COMPLETE, and a close releases
only `(:conn . ID)`. The ACL2 witness is `tests/acl2/owner-credits-tests.lisp`,
section "THE COMMIT'S STEPS": a mutant close that also releases `:sealed`
admits the next POST, and the real close refuses it. The native case is
`tests/test_native_credits.py`
`test_the_batch_in_flight_keeps_its_credit_past_the_close`.

Also found: `fn-oas-held` adds `(len (fn-own-inflight o))`, but the in-flight
field is one submission record, so this adds 4 or 6 where it means 1. That
book belongs to lane admission-gap and was reported there.

## Teeth (tests/acl2/owner-credits-tests.lisp, prefix mcat-)

- Positive witness for the read keystones: room for one reserve, a POST
  offered 340. The read is `fn-oas-read-span`'s, and connection 0 then holds
  exactly one reserve.
- The credit refuses where the slots would not: 32 slots and room one octet
  short. MUTATION: `fn-oas-read-span` offers 340, and taking it would
  over-commit. The host's call answers the memory 440 and leaves the ledger
  unchanged.
- Hypothesis removals:
  - An unfunded ledger stays unfunded.
  - Reserve to finish: a body line with no room reads on unchanged. Without
    the held credit, the refused read closes the connection 400.
  - Coverage: a queued submission the credit does not cover leaves the
    credit short of the need.
- The commit's steps: take, seal, close, COMPLETE, untake, stop, and a
  control submission, with the conservation facts.
- The initial ledger on the production core:
  - small preset: 50,855,936 octets free, 32 reserves admitted and not 33;
  - A = 4 MiB: 1 reserve admitted and not 2.

## Not claimed (open)

- A frame theorem that one connection's read leaves every other
  connection's buffers as they were. Lane admission-gap is proving it. With
  it, per-connection coverage becomes an invariant of the whole served
  machine; this lane proves coverage per read, for the connection read.
- A served-state witness of the shut-read tier. That tier is reached when a
  peer's IHAVE or TAKETHIS article completes within one read, so its refused
  read still queues it. The witness needs an owner fixture with a peer
  connection; the tier's output is witnessed directly.
- Cache credit and eviction on the served path. No cache is charged: a
  committed article is held by the base's state term at the profile's bounds
  (`fn-heap-store-history-holds-payload-and-memberships`). Retain and evict
  get a host subject when the state leaves the base (P1/P2 of the
  f8-reservation record). Overdraw has no host caller.
- The accountable-memory and working-set figures under a named request mix.
  Measure at convergence: the 256 MiB accountable target with the stripped
  image. What to measure: VmRSS and anonymous memory with 32 posters at the
  small preset mid-article, and with a stalled barrier.
