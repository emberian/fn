# K5 live, and the 90-second accept refusal (lane `w11/feed-k5`, 2026-09-21)

Two things this lane was asked to do: diagnose why node B closed every
connection at accept for 90 s after a peer restart, and then run K5 --
`kill -9` a sender mid-offer and show the article resolving by RE-OFFER with
the receiver holding exactly one copy. This record says what was measured.
Nothing in it is a proof and none of it is a flight-readiness claim.

## 1. The accept refusal: a connection leak, not a bound

**The cause: the owner never released a connection the BOOK told it to
close.** `fn-served-closingp` is true of the effects after QUIT's `205`.
`tools/run_owner.py` set `conn.closing`, armed the socket for write, stopped
reading it and never called `drop()`. So the socket stayed open, the
selector spun on it, and -- the part that matters -- the entry stayed in
`fn-own-conns` for the lifetime of the process. `fn-own-open` refuses when
`(len conns) >= max-conns` and the host closed the new socket with no
greeting, no `ACCEPT-FAULT` and no log line. N QUIT-terminated connections
cost N permanent slots; at the bound the node refuses everything, for ever,
while `kill -0` says it is alive.

### The measurement

`/private/tmp/.../accept_probe.py`, kept as the shape rather than as a
committed tool: one owner from this worktree at `--max-connections 4`, a
client that greets, sends `ARTICLE`, then QUITs and closes exactly as
`tools/deploy_gate.py`'s `Conn.close` does, and after each client a read of
`(fn-owner-connections state)` through the owner's own control channel.

So it is not close handling in general and it is not the bound being

every one greeted, and `connections` is empty after each.

### Why this is the 90 seconds of `HANDOFF-w11-twonode-feed`

The gate's driver QUITs -- `tools/deploy_gate.py` `Conn.close()` sends
`QUIT` and reads the `205` -- and `feed.py wait` opens a fresh connection
every 0.5 s for its whole deadline. A node started at
`--max-connections 32` therefore walls at poll 33, which is 16.5 s into a
90 s deadline. **Gate `0e5a7f8` recorded exactly that: "step 87 aborted at
16.5 s of a 90 s deadline".** Every one of the 180 polls after it read
`server closed the connection`, the tap in front of B recorded no COMMAND
(the gate greps the tap for command and status lines, and a connection
closed before its greeting produces neither), and node B's log said nothing
because the refusal prints nothing.

It was never a fault inside `accept_nntp`, which is why no `ACCEPT-FAULT`
was ever going to appear. Raising the default from 8 to 32 in wave 11 did
not fix it; it moved the wall from the fourth probe to the thirty-third.

### What changed

`tools/run_owner.py`: a connection whose reply has left the socket and
whose effects said `closing` is dropped -- socket closed, selector
unregistered, `fn-owner-close` called, which is what releases the slot; the
refusal at the bound prints `ACCEPT-REFUSED <peer> : the owner holds N of M
connections`, because a server that refuses everything must be able to say
why; a failure between the owner's open and the selector registration
releases the ACL2 connection instead of leaking it; and a selector
registration with no connection behind it is unregistered instead of spun
on.

## 2. A lost connection now requeues, per peer

`feed_drop` told ACL2 only `(:feed-conn peer nil)`, which stops selection
and resolves nothing: the in-flight entry stayed `:sent` until the process
restarted. `fn-own-feed-lost` (`books/owner.lisp`) applies `fn-feed-lost` to
ONE peer through `fn-own-feed-lost-one` (`books/owner-feed.lisp`): the
in-flight entry returns to `:queued` with its attempt counted and a backoff,
and the connection is forgotten. `fn-own-feed-lost-one-touches-no-other-peer`
is the theorem that says it is not `fn-own-feed-restart-all`, whose settle of
another peer's genuinely in-flight entry is the second transfer K5 forbids.

The record it authorizes is `(:feed-outcome peer msgid attempt 400)` and not
the `(:feed-restart peer)` the handoff's packet proposed. That is the record
a `400` on the wire already writes, and `fn-feed-apply-record` replays it as
`fn-feed-queue-requeue-inflight`, so the journal's enumeration stays closed
and a replay reaches the state the live machine reached. A `:feed-restart`
record would not: `fn-feed-restart` RETIRES the attempt where `fn-feed-lost`
counts it.

The host reports an event and a time. The requeue decision is `fn-feed-lost`'s.

## 4. The third blocker, which only the first two made visible

**Gate `15ac399`** (`python3 tools/twonode_gate.py HEAD --host persvati`,
invoked from this worktree; record
`planning/evidence/twonode-15ac399-2026-09-21.md`, 129 steps, 1 failed, 1
not exercised).

The 90 s of silence is gone. Where gate `0ec08bb` recorded 180 polls of
`server closed the connection`, this run records
`status=430 no article with that message-id attempts=179`: node B answered
every poll, correctly, for the whole deadline. And what that uncovered is
that **node A never offered the article at all**. A posted
`<fed-restart@example.invalid>` with B down (`240 article received OK`), was
killed with `kill -9`, restarted, logged `FEED b replayed 8` -- and the tap
in front of B recorded no session from A for the rest of the run.

**Cause: the feed enqueue was never journaled.** `specs/peering.md` §3.3
writes `(:feed-enqueue peer msgid tick)` before the entry is `:queued`.
`fn-own-feed-durable-records` existed and had **no caller**: `fn-own-outcome`
and `fn-own-transit-outcome` fold `fn-own-feed-durable` into the new owner,
`host/owner-host.lisp` installed the served effects and not the feed
records, and `tools/run_owner.py` never flushed them. The queue lived in
memory and nowhere else until its first offer, so an article accepted while
a peer was unreachable did not survive the process that accepted it. The
predecessor lane measured the shape without naming it: four sealed records
after a crossing, "kind 06 restart, 02 offer, 03 sent, 04 outcome" -- no 01.

`fn-own-outcome-records` and `fn-own-transit-outcome-records`
(`books/owner.lisp`) state the condition under which the records are owed --
the same connection, in-flight submission and `:durable` completion the
transition itself uses -- and the host reads them off the owner before the
outcome moves it, installs them and flushes them in `drain`, before the loop
writes the 240 to the poster.

**Measured on the laptop.** One node, one peer record naming `127.0.0.1:1`
where nothing listens. POST answers `240 article received OK`;
`<store>/feed/b.fnfd` is 135 octets and names the Message-ID. `kill -9`,
restart: 184 octets, and its frames in order are

| offset | kind | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 6 | `:feed-restart b`, the first start |
| 53 | **1** | **`:feed-enqueue b <enq-probe@example.invalid>`** |
| 139 | 6 | `:feed-restart b`, after the kill |

Kind 1 had never been on disk.

## 5. K5 ran

**Gate `2c27ef6`** (`python3 tools/twonode_gate.py HEAD --host persvati`,
invoked from this worktree; record
`planning/evidence/twonode-2c27ef6-2026-09-21.md`, 130 steps, 2 failed, 1
not exercised; persvati, Ubuntu 25.10, Linux 6.17.0-40-generic x86_64, 24
cores, python3 3.13.7).

The scenario: node B is stopped; node A accepts `<fed-restart@example.invalid>`
by POST, so the entry is queued and undeliverable; **node A is killed with
`kill -9`** -- no shutdown path runs; both nodes restart on their pinned
ports; the gate polls node B for 90 s.

| row | value |
| --- | --- |
| A's POST while B was down | `240 article received OK` |
| A's FNFD journal names the queued article, before the kill | **1** |
| B receives it after A's restart | **`220 0 <fed-restart@example.invalid> article follows`, attempts=1** |
| the octets B serves against the octets A serves | **identical=True** |
| **B's own store, before the kill and after** | **`fn.letters` held 6 articles and holds 7** |
| the wire in front of B, across the kill | `MODE STREAM; CHECK <fed-restart@example.invalid>; TAKETHIS <fed-restart@example.invalid>` |
| offers / article blocks / accepted transfers / refused-as-duplicate | **1 / 1 / 1 / 0** |
| node A's restart log | `FEED b replayed 11` |

**A sender killed mid-offer resolved the article by RE-OFFERING it, and the
receiver holds exactly one copy.** The offer is a `CHECK` -- never a blind
`TAKETHIS` -- which is what `fn-feed-restart` guarantees and what §3.3 says
lets the peer's own history absorb a retransmission. The "exactly one copy"
is a COUNT in the receiver, not a reread: a reread cannot tell one copy from
two, because the store would refuse the second and the reread would look
identical either way.

The negative the keystone claims is asserted and holds: **one article block
on the wire and one accepted transfer**, not two.

### Two things this run reports honestly rather than as passes

- **`article-blocks` was miscounted on this run.** The first version of the
  teeth counted `S< 238` as an article block. A `238` is the CHECK answer --
  permission to send -- so one correct transfer read as two and the run
  raised a duplicate-transfer gap against itself. The counter now counts
  `C> TAKETHIS` (RFC 4644 §2.5: the article always follows) and `S< 335`
  (RFC 3977 §6.3.2: the go-ahead), which is one per article block. The
  underlying measurement is unchanged: one TAKETHIS, one `239`.
- **`node A is unchanged by node B's death` failed**, and the defect is the
  gate's bookkeeping, not the node's. Node A ACCEPTED `<fed-restart@…>` by
  POST and holds it; the scenario never recorded that, so the later step
  classed it as an article only B ever accepted and asserted A does not hold
  it. That assertion was false the moment the feed started working. Fixed by
  recording the acceptance where the cut scenario already does.

The other failure, `feed: <alpha@a.example.invalid> reread on B`, is the
predecessor's two open rows unchanged: the second offer in one session draws
`335`/`238` rather than `435`/`438`, and `CAPABILITIES` names neither
`IHAVE` nor `STREAMING`. Both are in §6 below and in `specs/peering.md`.

## 7. One hazard, paid for here

`self.deploy = "$HOME/fn-deploy/<rev>"`, with no tree in it, while the gate
LOCK path at `:590` is `<GATE_ROOT>/<tree>-<rev>`. Two gates at the same

out from under each other whatever `--tree` says. A `v0_matrix.py` run and a
