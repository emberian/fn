# The native cut campaign on the 1a9dd747 image pair — 2026-09-24

This is the first run of the native cut campaign on any image since
`da5fd8cb`, and the first on an image that carries the 32 KiB repair
(plan §2.1 P2, "the cut campaign has run in no image since the 32 KiB repair").
It is a runtime record. It certifies nothing and proves no theorem. The ACL2
differential against the byte model (`tests/test_native_crash_model.py`) did
not run.

**Result in one line.** The operator campaign has 40 cut observations (20
cuts × {served owner, `store`/`recover` entry}), and all 40 agree with the
table, byte-identical over two runs. The image-gated test passed 10 of 10, and
every production refusal held. The NNTP-wire probe added in this lane found
three P2 defects at the wire: **W1**, an uncertain outcome is delivered as a
closed connection with no reply; **W2**, an EIO at a `finish-*` cut reports a
durable article as `441 ... refused`; **W3**, a store refusal carries no
reason. None of the 20 cuts can be selected on the production image, by
design. The kill cuts ran on the developer twin built from the same source.

## Per cut: the operator campaign

Each cut is taken twice, each time over a fresh store seeded with one prior
article through `operator CFG post`. **served**: an `operator CFG run` owner
with `FN_NATIVE_POST_FAULT=<cut>:kill` (or `FN_NATIVE_RECOVERY_FAULT` for a
recovery cut, over a `.stage-` orphan) takes the candidate through `operator
CFG post`. **store/recover**: `store ROOT post` (or `operator CFG recover`)
with the selector. Then `operator CFG recover`, `store ROOT inspect`, a
restarted owner's NNTP `ARTICLE` of both articles, and a resubmission through
the entry that submitted. A PASS requires all of the following:

- the table's candidate column holds;
- the prior's transaction bytes at death equal the seeded bytes;
- recovery exits 0, changes nothing in `transactions/` and leaves `staging/`
  empty;
- the prior rereads equal to its seeded octets by both readers, and the
  readers agree;
- a surviving candidate rereads as its reference by both readers, and the
  resubmission answers duplicate with no new transaction;
- an absent candidate gives `inspect` exit 1 and NNTP `430`, and the
  resubmission adds exactly one transaction.

A served post row also requires the owner to be ready, the client to exit
**3** (UNCERTAIN) and the owner to exit −9. A served recovery row requires
the owner to die −9 before `LISTENING`.

## Per cut at the NNTP wire (P2's bar)

Plan P2 is stated at the wire: `240` only after the completion was consumed,
`441` naming its reason, uncertain as `441 ... do not repost`. The operator
campaign does not observe the wire, since it posts through the control socket.
The probe takes each post cut on the same served owner with
`FN_NATIVE_POST_FAULT=<cut>:kill` and again with `:eio`, sends the candidate
by NNTP `POST`, and records the reply octets. It then stops the owner by
pid, runs `operator recover`, rereads through `inspect` and `ARTICLE`, and

### W1: an uncertain outcome is never delivered to the NNTP client

Nine EIO rows fail this way: `frontier-replaced`, `-attempted`, `-durable`,
`-reserved`, `record-linked`, `-attempted`, `-durable`, `-completing` and
`-staging-cleaned`.

- **Expected bytes:** `441 posting failed; the outcome is uncertain, do not repost\r\n`.
- **Observed bytes:** none. The client read EOF right after sending the
  article, and the `QUIT` that followed got EOF too. The owner exited 3.
- **Owner log** (`probe-work/frontier-replaced-eio/owner-1.err`):
  `uncertain post path=served connection=0 message-id=<candidate@campaign.invalid> …`,
  then `owner connection-local fault; service continues: send-reply: [Errno 32] Broken pipe`,
  then `uncertain operator run`.

Cause, from the source: `fnn-owner-chunk`'s uncertain branch
(`host/native/owner.lisp:1376-1377`) calls `fnn-owner-stop-service-locked`,
which shuts down **every** client socket, this one included (`:541-544`,
`socket-shutdown … :direction :io`). It then returns the reply, and
`:send-reply` (`:1512-1516`) writes the ACL2-computed uncertain line into the
socket it has just shut down. The fence runs before the reply.

The article's fate was consistent with the table: absent at the frontier
cuts, present and identical at the record cuts. The client, though, was told
nothing, so by RFC 3977 it cannot tell this from a network drop. The
operator entry gets 3 at the same cuts because its client itself turns a
closed control socket into UNCERTAIN. This was not seen before because no
campaign observed the NNTP wire under an EIO. `test_native_owner`'s
`test_two_client_uncertainty_fences_before_later_mutation` asserts only the
owner's exit 3 and the second session's closure. It never asserts the first
session's reply.

### W2: an EIO at a finish cut reports a durable article as refused

- **Rows:** `finish-consumed:eio` and `finish-durable:eio`.
- **Expected:** `240 article received OK` or `441 posting failed; the outcome
  is uncertain, do not repost`. The transaction was already published and
  linked.
- **Observed:** `441 posting failed; the article was refused\r\n`, owner exit
  0 (`accepted operator run`), owner log `refused post path=served …`.
- **Store:** `00000000000000000001.txn` present before recovery. After
  recovery it rereads `2ef233fa…` by `inspect` and by `ARTICLE` (`220 0
  <candidate@campaign.invalid>`), the payload's injected form.

This is a three-outcome collapse: a durable article is reported refused.
In `fnn-finish` (`host/native/io.lisp:1599-1615`), `(fnn-at store
:finish-consumed)` sits after `completion-pending` is cleared, and `(fnn-at
store :finish-durable)` after the fence is lifted. Both are outside the
function's own `handler-case`. So the `fnn-os-error` they raise reaches
`fnn-owner-attempt`'s catch-all `((or fnn-store-error fnn-os-error) ()
:refused)` (`host/native/owner.lisp:744`; `fnn-owner-attempt-transit` has the
same clause at `:819`). That clause decides the outcome in host code, where
the one-owner rule wants ACL2 to decide it.

Qualification: no system call sits at either `fnn-at` in this source, so
only the injection raises an EIO there today. What the row shows is the
mapping, which reports any OS error raised after publication as refused. The
kill at the same two cuts passes.

### W3: a store refusal names no reason

- **Rows:** `frontier-staged-durable:eio` and `record-staged-durable:eio`
  (the candidate is absent, so refusal is the right outcome), and every repost
  of a stored article.
- **Observed:** `441 posting failed; the article was refused\r\n`.
- **Expected under P2:** a `441` that names its reason, as the injection
  refusals do (`441 posting failed; From is required`).

`fn-nntp-post-outcome` (`books/nntp-post.lisp:280-281`) maps every store
`:refused` to that one line, whether the cause is an I/O failure before
publication, a duplicate of an identical article, or a conflict. The operator
entry tells the duplicate apart (`DUPLICATE`, exit 0). RFC 3977 §6.3.1 allows
`441` for all three. Naming the reason is the stronger fn guarantee P2 states.

## What this shows for P2 and P10

- **P2, cut half: holds on this pair.** After a kill at every one of the 20
  cuts, through both entries and at the wire, the acknowledged prior rereads
  byte-identical by both readers. So does every candidate that survived. No
  `240` preceded any death, and after an accepted `240` a SIGKILL of the
  production owner rereads the article byte-identical.
- **P2, wire half: fails.** Uncertain is not `441 ... do not repost`; the
  connection closes with no reply (W1). A durable article can be reported
  refused (W2). Store refusals carry no reason (W3).
- **The operator CLI keeps the three outcomes distinct:** 0 accepted or
  duplicate, 1 refused, 3 uncertain, 5 usage, −9 death, in every row.
- **P10:** every cut taken has a `fn-bs-*-program` coordinate, and the
  campaign passes at all 20 cuts, but **on the developer image**. The
  production image refuses every cut selector at start (27 of 27, exit 5,
  store unchanged). It was exercised only by SIGKILL at two command
  boundaries: after a `240`, and mid-article before any program ran. Those
  are the final and initial states of the write, and neither is a named cut.
  "Passes on the production image" is therefore true only as "passes on the
  developer image built from the same source and certified book set as the
  production image". The two cores differ (`12ece6cc…` vs `9b38ba45…`).
  Five model sites have no host cut; see above.

## What this does not show

- There was no ACL2 differential, so the rows are not membership in the
  byte model's image at the cut.
- These are process deaths, not power loss. The stores were on ZFS, and D14's
  durability qualification is untouched.
- The DTN image was not run, nor were the checkpoint cuts
  (`CHECKPOINT_CUTS`), `native_block_fault.py` (it needs a tmpfs loop device),
  or the recovery-fidelity unit tests.
- W2 is reachable only through the injection in this source; see its
  qualification.
- No fix was made. W1 to W3 are for the coordinator.
