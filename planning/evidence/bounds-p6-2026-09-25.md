## What changed

- **CBOR** (books/cbor.lisp, cbor-invariants.lisp): an additive wide uint,
  RFC 8949 §3 additional information 27, canonical only above 2^32 - 1
  (§4.2.1): `fn-cbor-encode-uint-wide`, `fn-cbor-decode-unsigned-wide`,
  `fn-cbor-decode-prechecked-wide`, `fn-cbor-u64-bytes/-from`,
  `*fn-cbor-max-uint64*`.  `*fn-cbor-max-uint*` keeps 2^32 - 1: its uses in
  35 other books are u32 fields (frame fields, checkpoint TREE naturals,
  `fn-cbor-u32-bytes` guards), and widening the constant would misstate
  them.  No existing cbor function changed.
- **Records**: sequence, txid, generation, charge and a natural stamp are
  `fn-record-uint64p`; every record uint goes through the wide codec
  (`fn-record-uint-encode`, `fn-record-uint-decode`).  Schema octet
  (`fn-record-schema-octet`): 0 for a legacy stamp, 2 for a stamped record
  with a field above 2^32 - 1 (`fn-record-widep`), else 1.  The exact
  decoder refuses `:schema` when the header octet is not the record's, so
  every accepted input is still the encoding of its record.  Fixed overhead
  1 083 -> 1 111 octets (seven 9-octet uint heads).
- **Translation at open** (books/records-schema-v1.lisp): the pre-P6 decoder
  frozen as `fn-record-v1-*` (records.lisp at e366c633, guards dropped).
  Old records' bytes are their own translation, so open rewrites nothing.

## Findings and open items

1. **Merge-blocking question for a persisted format-8 profile.** The article
   record ceiling at (A 32 768, G 65 535) rises from 17 138 486 to 17 138 514
   with the u64 overhead, and `fn-bs-profile-validp` refuses `R <
   fn-record-encoded-octets-ceiling(A, G)` (byte-store-frame.lisp:200).  A
   store that persisted format 8 with R = 17 138 486 (the development or
   scale preset written by P1's init or upgrade) would read as an invalid
   profile under this image.  Format-7 stores are translated at open and
   are fine (observed above).  Whether any such store exists (the live node
   was upgraded to scale) must be checked before merge; the likely repair is
   a record ceiling at the widths the runtime can produce (u32 while the
   frontier and the POST charge are u32), not a raised preset.
2. Transaction IDs still stop at 2^32 - 1: the frontier payload is 5 octets
   and `T <= *fn-cbor-max-uint*` (byte-store-frame, P5's frame book).  The
   record can carry a u64 txid; the allocator cannot produce one yet.
3. Charge above 2^32 - 1 is refused at the POST boundary (above), and the
   retention ledger's codecs have not been audited for u64 charge.
4. **Segmented articles (P6 item 2) and item widths (item 3): not done.**
   The node holds each article body as an octet list in the logical owner
   and the served ARTICLE reply is computed by ACL2 from it (`fn-served-run`);
   a 1 GiB article is a 16 GiB list per copy.  Serving by segments with RSS
   bounded by the segment size needs the body to be a segment-chain
   reference in the node (representation design wave C, boundary 10) and a
   served step that yields per segment.  Neither the segment chain book,
   the segmented commit byte program, its cuts, nor the 1 GiB native run
   exists.

### What now works

The claim "records are u64" is now split into the part the system does and
the part only the codec does.  The node's record producers are proved to
stay inside the u32 widths the profile's R is sized for, so every record the
node stages is schema 1 and within R.  u64 is the width the codec and the
format-7 translation carry; no producer makes a wider value yet.

### Assurance chain

native entry (`store post`, the served POST, BP ingress) -> the POST
boundary `fn-sbud-post-boundary` (host/native/io.lisp `fnn-post-boundary`,
host/owner-host.lisp `fn-owner-post-boundary`) -> the producer
`fn-sn-article-record` (host/store-node-host.lisp, host/owner-host.lisp; BP
through `fn-bpi-record-for`) -> the prepare the host calls (`fn-spc-prepare`,
equal to `fn-sn-prepare` by `fn-spc-prepare-equals-specification-under-relation`;
the owner's `fn-pcar-sbud-prepare`, equal to `fn-sbud-prepare` by
`fn-pcar-sbud-prepare-is-sbud-prepare`) -> the maintained relation
`fn-sf-statep` (u32 frontier, committed txids strictly increasing below it),
established at open and preserved by every file transition
(store-files-invariants) -> the behavioural theorems below -> observed: the
native gate.  The boundary and the prepare are two host calls in that order,
so the composition `fn-post-admitted-article-record-is-within-r` is over the
host's order, not over one ACL2 function the host calls.

### Theorems (books/record-width-producers.lisp, PRF-123)

- `fn-sf-prepare-record-stages-u32-coordinates`: `(implies (and (fn-sf-statep
  s) (not (equal (fn-sf-prepare-record s record groups capacity) s))) ...)`
  sequence, txid and generation are `fn-record-uint32p`.  From
  `fn-sf-candidatep` (txid = frontier - 1, generation = txid, sequence = the
  committed count) and `fn-sf-record-listp-len-bound` (the count is at most
  the frontier).
- `fn-sn-prepare-stages-u32-coordinates`: the same for any record
  `fn-sn-prepare` stages (no state hypothesis: the prepare checks
  `fn-sn-statep` itself), plus `fn-record-p`.
- `fn-sn-article-record-stamp-and-charge`: the producer's stamp is u32
  (`fn-record-stamp-of-observation` answers `:clock-unusable` at 2^32 s and
  after) and its charge is its argument.
- `fn-sbud-post-boundary-admits-a-u32-charge`: `:ok` implies a u32 charge.
- KEYSTONE `fn-sn-prepare-stages-a-narrow-article-record`: `(implies (and
  (fn-record-uint32p charge) (not (equal (fn-sn-prepare s (fn-sn-article-record
  s obs msgid payload groups obligation-id subject evidence charge)) s)))
  (not (fn-record-widep (fn-sn-article-record ...))))`.
- `fn-post-admitted-article-record-is-within-r`: boundary `:ok` under PROFILE
  at `(len payload)` and `(len groups)`, and the prepare stages the producer's
  record -> `(<= (len (fn-record-encode record)) (fn-bs-profile-max-record-octets
  profile))`.  This discharges the width hypothesis of
  `fn-bs-profile-admits-every-article-record` at the host's sequence; no
  admitted-profile hypothesis is needed (the boundary refuses every group
  count under a value that is not a profile).
- `fn-bpi-staged-record-is-narrow`: `fn-bpi-policy-p` and a staged
  `fn-bpi-record-for` record -> not wide.

### Packet 1: profile monotonicity

- Constraint: `fn-profile-upgrade-keeps-verdict` (PRF-072) says every verdict
  the old profile gave, the upgraded one gives.  It holds because the
  history gate charges an article at a fixed figure,
  `*fn-store-article-publication-figure*` = 65 538, independent of A and G.
- Counterexample 1, the current system (tests/acl2/profile-monotonicity-tests,
  evaluated): a valid profile (T 4, H 250 000, R 196 608, A 32 768, G 500);
  an article at A in 400 groups of 255-octet names encodes to 135 641
  octets, passes the POST boundary's payload and group bounds and the
  publish gate's record check (<= R); with 184 462 octets committed the
  owner's verdict charges 65 538 and says `:admissible`; committed, the
  total is 320 103 > H, and every later open refuses the store
  (`fn-profile-replay-within-boundp`, host/native/io.lisp
  `fnn-durable-records`: "transaction recovery input exceeds configured
  bound").  Accepted, then unopenable.  At the saved presets it needs
  about 122 cross-posted groups with long names, or any A raised past
  about 64 KiB (bounds-join §5 plans 4 MiB).
- Counterexample 2, the derived figure: charging the article record of
  (A, G) is truthful (at the bytes where it admits, the worst article fits
  H), but raising A from 32 768 to 60 000 with H unchanged is an upgrade by
  `fn-profile-upgradep` after which the same state is `:unaffordable`.
- The two promises, named: *reopen* -- every existing store opens under the
  new profile: `fn-profile-upgradep` (H and R never shrink;
  `fn-profile-upgrade-keeps-replay-bound`, the per-file read bound); this is
  kept by every option below.  *Future admissibility* -- every verdict the
  old profile gave is kept: holds for a profile-independent figure; for the
  derived figure it needs H to grow by at least the figure's growth
  (H 250 000 -> 277 232 re-admits the state above).
