- The prior's bytes are unchanged at death.
- `recover` exits 0 with `transactions/` unchanged and `staging/` empty.
- Both readers reread the prior byte-identical and agree.
- A present candidate rereads identical by both readers and resubmits as

- **Bands.** Window 39, early 21, late 16, mid-article 8, over-limit 6.
- **Replies at death.** Every killed POST that got `240` (3) died in state
  "linked, no stage", and the owner had logged `accepted post` for it. No
  kill before that state found a `240` or an `accepted post` line.
- **Client exit codes.** rc 0 came only with `240 article received OK`. rc 1
  came only with `441 posting failed; the article was not received` or `441
  posting failed; a different article with this Message-ID is stored here`.
  rc 3 came only with no reply. The judge lists **0 failures** and exits 0.

**Finding K1 (wire wording; for the coordinator).** Of the 96 resubmissions,
56 were answered `240` and 5 `441 … not received` (over-limit). **All 35
resubmissions of a died-present-identical article** were answered `441
posting failed; a different article with this Message-ID is stored here`.
The judge counts these as correct: the stored article rereads identical to
the killed POST, and no transaction was added. On the 1a9dd747 production
image the same case was `441 posting failed; the article was refused` (W3).
It now names a kind, but the kind is conflict. The client resent its own
octets after an uncertain death and is told a *different* article is
stored. This is the `Injection-Date` effect the p2-wire record describes:
the restart takes seconds, so the resubmission never falls in the stored
article's second, and it is never classed duplicate. The client's safe
reading ("do not repost, something is stored") still holds. The wording of
a duplicate versus a conflict after a death stays with ember's pending
refusal-wording decision.
