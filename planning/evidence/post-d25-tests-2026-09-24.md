## The tests (method of [stale-native-tests](stale-native-tests-2026-09-24.md): the property against the current machine, never weakened)

- **`test_native_operator_verbs` budget case.**
  - Before: the test expected a same-bytes re-POST at the budget to get the
    conflict answer, because the owner injects headers into the stored copy.
  - Now, under D25: the same bytes are `441 posting failed; this article is
    already stored here`. A re-POST with one authored byte changed (the
    Subject) is `441 … a different article with this Message-ID is stored
    here`. Both cases are checked twice: at used = 1, where
    `transactions-used` must still be 1 after them (the strongest form, since
    the count could grow there), and at used = budget, where they are still
    the Store's existing-article answers and not the capacity refusal.
- **`campaign/test_native_operator_campaign`, the cross-entry retry.**
  - Before: one payload sent through `store ROOT post` and then
    `operator CFG post` (and the other way round) was expected to be a
    conflict, second rc 1.
  - Now: the second submission is the duplicate, rc 0. It reads `DUPLICATE`
    through the operator entry and `duplicate` through the store entry.
    `after_second == after_first` is checked: transactions, staging and
    frontier are identical byte for byte, so no transaction was written.
  - New: a third submission through the second entry carries a changed
    Subject under the same Message-ID. It is the conflict, rc 1, and
    `after_changed == after_first`. The driver
    (`tests/campaign/native_operator_campaign.py`) writes
    `candidate-changed.art` and records the row. The docstring of `reread`
    now says the same thing.

## `peer list` renders the D23 rows (books/native-admin.lisp)

- The native `operator CONFIG peer list` line (`fn-native-admin-peer-report`)
  now ends with one `TAG=VALUE` word per row, in row order, after `auth=`:
  - `carries-principal=HEX` for an NNTP peer's carried principals;
  - `carries=EID` and `releases-for=EID` for a BP boundary.
  The words are rendered by `fn-native-admin-peer-extra-octets` from the
  peer's own row group (`fn-cfg-rows-with-key`), in the function that
  renders the rest of the line. The typed record
  (`fn-cfg-peer-of-rows`) does not hold these rows. The host adds no
  formatting: `fnn-admin-query` still writes the ACL2 octets as they are.
- There is no `bp-boundary list` verb, and adding one was not in scope. A BP
  boundary is a peer row group with `transport-bp`, and `peer list` lists it
  (`address=<EID> port=0 security=-`), now including its `carries` and
  `releases-for` words.
- **The keystone: `fn-native-admin-peer-extra-decode-lists-exactly-the-rows`.**
  Take the rendered section followed by the line's newline and read it back
  with `fn-native-admin-peer-extra-decode`, three readers in sequence. It
  yields exactly the rendered values (`fn-native-admin-peer-label-octets`)
  of the `carries-principal`, `bp-boundary-carries` and
  `bp-boundary-releases-for` rows, in order, with multiplicity, and leaves
  `(10)`. It has one hypothesis, `fn-native-admin-peer-extra-cleanp`: no
  value renders with a space or a newline. Its lemma is
  `fn-native-admin-peer-list-decode-of-list-octets`, the round trip of one
  tag's list.
  - Scope: the theorem covers the section after `auth=`. It does not parse
    the base fields.
- Teeth (tests/acl2/native-admin-tests.lisp), all reached from real `peer
  add` and `bp-boundary add` plans:
  - The exact line for a peer with two carried principals, and for a
    boundary with two carried sources and one release issuer.
  - `extra-cleanp` holds on both records, and the decoder returns the exact
    lists.
  - A boundary that has only `releases-for` lists no carried source, which
    separates the two kinds.
  - A record with no D23 rows renders nothing extra.
  - One `must-fail` for the hypothesis: a row `dtn://a b/` renders as two
    words and does not read back as the row.
- **Finding (not fixed; it belongs to the add verbs):** `bp-boundary add`
  admits a carried or release EID that contains a space
  (`fn-native-admin-bp-eid-wordp` checks only `fn-cfg-labelp` and the scheme
  prefix). Such a value is exactly what the `must-fail` shows is ambiguous in
  the list. A space is invalid in an RFC 9171 EID, so refusing it at add
  would discharge the hypothesis for every admitted record.
- The Python `fn peer list` (`tools/run_store.py peer_listing`, the Python
  host being retired) formats its line in Python. This lane did not change
  it.
