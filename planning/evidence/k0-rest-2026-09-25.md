## PKT-080: coverage generic over the root name

### What changed

`fn-bs-k0-coveredp` (books/byte-store-k0-step-lemmas.lisp) was "related, or a
pending committed-history rename both of whose resolutions are related". Its
second disjunct is now generic over the `:root` name. It is one disjunct,
renamed, and the marker is its instance at `committed-history.json`:

- `fn-bs-k0m-root-rename-opp`: a `:set-entry` on `:root` whose name is not
  the allocation frontier. `fn-bs-k0m-has-root-rename`,
  `fn-bs-k0m-drop-root-rename`, `fn-bs-k0m-drop-rename-choices` and
  `fn-bs-root-rename-dropped` are stated over it (they were the `-marker`
  functions, stated over the marker's name).
- `fn-bs-k0m-root-rename-onlyp (ops name ino)`: every pending root entry
  operation is the set-entry of NAME to INO, and NAME is not the frontier.
  It gained the name argument.
- `fn-bs-k0s-root-name` and `fn-bs-k0s-root-target`: the first pending root
  operation's name and inode. `fn-bs-k0s-root-rename-landed` makes that entry
  durable, and `fn-bs-k0s-root-rename-pendingp` (was `-marker-pendingp`) asks
  that both resolutions are related.
- The commutation `fn-bs-k0m-root-rename-crash-is-a-resolution-crash` (was
  `fn-bs-k0m-marker-rename-crash-is-a-resolution-crash`) and
  `fn-bs-k0m-crash-of-pending-root-rename` are stated for any NAME. The
  marker program's theorems (`fn-bs-k0-marker-replaced-cut-relation` and the
  bridge-marker corollaries) use it at the marker's name, unchanged in
  statement.
- The bridge lemmas that talked about "the root marker" were renamed:
  `fn-bs-k0b-covered-without-root-rename-is-related`,
  `fn-bs-k0b-covered-with-root-rename-is-pending`,
  `fn-bs-k0b-relation-has-no-root-rename`, and so on. The one statement that
  changed is `fn-bs-k0b-rename-keeps-root-rename-status`. Its hypothesis is
  now "the destination is not `:root`, or it is the frontier", because a
  rename onto the checkpoint name now changes the status.

### The rename arm

`fn-bs-k0-step-inputp`'s `:rename` arm admits `:staging` onto any `:root`
name except the frontier, with the root quiet and the source present
(`fn-bs-k0s-root-rename-targetp`). It admits a rename onto `config.json`
only from an inode that is fenced and allocated and whose durable content
passes `fn-bs-config-okp`. The result is covered, for every outcome:
`fn-bs-k0s-root-rename-covered`, from `fn-bs-k0s-root-rename-ok-pending`. The
dropped resolution is the unlink of the stage
(`fn-bs-k0s-root-rename-dropped-is-unlink`). The landed resolution is related
by `fn-bs-k0m-with-root-entry-preserves-relation` for a name that is neither
config.json nor the frontier. For config.json it is related by
`fn-bs-k0s-with-config-entry-preserves-relation`: the new inode becomes the
config entry and the first element of the authority inode list, so it must be
fenced, allocated and pass the check. The root barrier over the pending
rename, with any outcome, lands it or drops it
(`fn-bs-k0s-root-rename-barrier-resolves`, which was `-marker-barrier-resolves`).

### The two programs (books/byte-store-k0-step-bridge-root.lisp, new)

The state checkpoint (`fn-bs-scp-program`, `store-checkpoint.fnsc`) and the
profile upgrade (`fn-bs-profile-program`, `config.json`) are create,
write-all and fsync-file on a stage, a rename onto one root name, and the
root barrier, with no kernel observation.

- `fn-bs-k0r-pairs-by-step`: the keystone `fn-bs-step-preserves-k0-coverage`
  at the rename and at the barrier, after `fn-bs-k0p-stage-pairs-by-step`
  (lane k0-cuts) for the stage. Hypotheses: the entry pair is related and
  outside the recovery window; `:root` and `:transactions` are quiet; the
  stage is a name and is absent; the octets are typed; the root name is a
  name other than the frontier; and, for config.json, the octets pass
  `fn-bs-config-okp`. Conclusion: the three stage states are related, the
  state after the rename is `fn-bs-k0s-root-rename-pendingp`, and the state
  after the barrier is related.
- `fn-bs-k0-state-checkpoint-cuts-relation-by-step` and
  `fn-bs-k0-profile-cuts-relation-by-step`: at the five cut pairs of each
  program's successful run (`fn-bs-run`), pairs 1, 3, 5 and 9 are related,
  pair 7 is covered, and the kernel is the entry kernel at every cut. The
  profile theorem adds `(fn-bs-config-okp octets)`.
- `fn-bs-step-at-state-checkpoint-pairs-preserves-k0-coverage` and
  `fn-bs-step-at-profile-pairs-preserves-k0-coverage`: every step of the
  program, taken from the pair the successful run reaches before it, with
  any outcome, leaves a covered pair and the kernel unchanged. The stage
  fsync takes `:ok` or a well-formed crash selection of its writes
  (`fn-bs-k0r-fsync-outcomep`). So the host's error arms are covered by the
  same theorem: every error at or after the rename is reported as
  uncertain, and every error before it as a known failure.

### Teeth (tests/acl2/byte-store-k0-step-bridge-root-tests.lisp, new)

Witness: the second publication's entry pair (`bskc-rb`, `bskc-rk`, from the
cuts tests). It is related, outside the recovery window, with `:root` and
`:transactions` quiet. Each program runs from it, with checkpoint octets
`(70 78 83 67 1 2 3)` and the profile's `fn-bs-initial-config-octets`. Every
cut holds as claimed. The replaced pair is covered but not related, its two
resolutions differ, and the landed profile resolution names the new inode at
config.json. The root barrier over each pending rename satisfies the
keystone's precondition and conclusion, both with `:ok` and with an EIO that
drops the entry.

There is one `must-fail` per hypothesis of the cuts theorems:

- the relation (the initial image under the same kernel), for both programs;
- the stage a name (`7`), for both;
- the stage absent (`bskc-occupied`), for both;
- typed octets (`(300)`);
- `:root` quiet: the frontier run's attempted pair 11, which is related and
  has the frontier rename pending. The checkpoint's rename there adds a
  second root entry, so the replaced cut is not covered;
- the configuration check (profile only): octets the check refuses. The
  landed resolution's config.json fails the check.

`:transactions` quiet and the recovery-window guard have no `must-fail`.
They are the keystone's step-input guards, as for lane k0-cuts' stage
lemma, and the conclusion is not shown to need them.

## PKT-086: the authority barriers' error outcomes

### What is proved (books/byte-store-k0-authority-error.lisp, new)

A failed fsync of `:root` or `:transactions` lands a selection of that
directory's pending entry operations. A related state outside the recovery
window has at most one pending entry operation on each of the two
(`fn-bs-pending-shape-okp`). A selection of one entry operation keeps it or
drops it, so the result is the fence's result or `fn-bs-k0a-drop-dir`, the
state with that directory's pending operations removed
(`fn-bs-k0a-error-is-fence-or-drop`).

- `fn-bs-k0a-drop-dir-preserves-relation`: from a related state outside the
  recovery window, dropping every pending entry operation of any directory
  keeps the relation. The durable projections are those of the state before,
  the pending shape loses entries, and the authority inode list's pending
  part becomes a sublist.
- Keystone `fn-bs-k0a-authority-fsync-error-preserves-relation`: related,
  outside the recovery window, the directory `:root` or `:transactions`, an
  outcome other than `:ok`, and for `:transactions` with a pending link the
  phase `:record-attempted`. Then the error result is related. The landing
  half is `fn-bs-k0s-root-fence-preserves-relation` (k0-cuts) or
  `fn-bs-k8-pending-link-fence-preserves-relation`.

`fn-bs-k0-step-inputp`'s `:fsync-dir` gains this arm, and the keystone
`fn-bs-step-preserves-k0-coverage` covers it. This closes the
`:transactions` barrier's error outcome (the K7 pair-11 EIO, now for any
related state) and the root barrier's error outcomes over a pending frontier
rename. The crash selection is not a hypothesis: for entry operations every
selection lands or drops.

### Teeth (the same test book, appended)

Witnesses: the frontier run's attempted pair 11 (the frontier rename pending)
and the record run's attempted pair 10 (the link pending, `:record-attempted`).
For each, an EIO that lands the entry and one that drops it give different
states. Both are related, and the keystone's precondition and conclusion
hold. There is a `must-fail` for the relation: the initial image under the
same kernels. The other hypotheses have no `must-fail`:

- the recovery-window guard is every step kind's;
- landing or dropping staging entries keeps the relation too, so the
  directory clause has no separating instance;
- the phase clause for a pending link has no separating reachable instance.
  At the record run's pairs 7 and 8 (`:record-data-durable`, link pending),
  both EIOs leave related states. The clause is kept because the landing
  half stands on the k8 fence lemma, which is stated in `:record-attempted`.

## Not done, and why

- **`:mkdir` and `:link-eexist`.** The relation needs `config.json`, so it
  does not describe the store before initialisation. Covering them needs a
  pre-init relation. The init program has its own whole-program theorem,
  `fn-bs-init-program-establishes-relation`. Not started.
- **The recovery window (P-RECOVER).** Every step kind carries
  `(not (fn-bs-replay-visiblep ks))`. Covering the window needs
  `fn-bs-replay-matches-scan` preserved per step. That is a second relation
  with its own pending shape (the rolled-back frontier), and it is its own
  packet. Not started.
- **The in-process re-recovery at host/native/admin.lisp:107**
  (`fnn-admin-verify-under-lock`: `fnn-bridge-reset`, then `fnn-recover` in
  the same process after a configuration publication). This runs the
  recovery window a second time in one process. The window's relation
  assumes `Store.recover` runs once per process (the `(null (fn-sf-successes
  s))` conjunct of `fn-bs-replay-matches-scan`), so this depends on the
  recovery-window item and was not started.
