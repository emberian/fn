## 1. The recovery window as step kinds (988fae30)

`books/byte-store-k0-window.lisp` (new). Keystone
`fn-bs-k0w-step-preserves-relation`:

```lisp
(implies (fn-bs-k0w-step-inputp bs ks step outcome)
         (let ((bs1 (mv-nth 1 (fn-bs-step bs ks step outcome groups capacity)))
               (ks1 (mv-nth 2 (fn-bs-step bs ks step outcome groups capacity))))
           (and (fn-bs-store-relation bs1 ks1)
                (or (equal ks1 ks) (equal (car step) :observe)))))
```

`fn-bs-k0w-step-inputp` requires a kernel in the recovery window
(`fn-bs-replay-visiblep`) and a related pair. The pending list may still
hold the one un-fenced `:root` rename or `:transactions` link that a dead
process left in the page cache. The step kinds it admits:

- `:cut`.
- `:fsync-file` on a fenced target, any outcome. The step is the identity
  (`fn-bs-k0w-fenced-fsync-file-is-identity`). The relation fences every
  authority inode (`fn-bs-k0v-related-root-lookups-are-fenced`).
- `:fsync-dir` with `:ok` on any directory
  (`fn-bs-k0w-fence-preserves-relation`), or any outcome on a directory
  with nothing pending (the identity). The fence keeps the view, so it
  keeps the scan (`fn-bs-k0w-fence-keeps-scan`, from
  `-fence-keeps-lookup`, `-names` and `-read-records`). It moves the one
  pending authority entry into the durable directory. There are three
  cases: `:transactions` with a link pending, `:root` with a rename pending
  (the durable frontier becomes the scanned one), and any other directory.
- `(:recover)`, `(:recovery-barrier :ok)` and `(:recovery-barrier :uncertain)`.
  These keep the kernel's frontier, records and successes. If the result
  stays in the window, the relation carries over
  (`fn-bs-k0w-window-kernel-swap`). If it leaves the window (the fifth
  barrier to `:ready`, or `(:recover)` to `:fault`), then `:root` and
  `:transactions` must be quiet (`fn-bs-k0w-quiet-window-exit`).

`books/byte-store-k0-step.lisp`: the old precondition is now
`fn-bs-k0-outside-step-inputp`. `fn-bs-k0-step-inputp` is the disjunction
of that precondition and `fn-bs-k0w-step-inputp`. The keystone
`fn-bs-step-preserves-k0-coverage` has the same statement and covers both
disjuncts: `fn-bs-k0-outside-step-preserves-k0-coverage`, and the window
keystone through `fn-bs-k0s-covered-of-relation`. `fn-bs-k0w-step-inputp`
is disabled after the keystone. Every bridge book discharges the first
disjunct unchanged, and none of them was edited.

The subject is `fn-bs-step` (model-only, as for the rest of PRF-041's K0
events).

## 2. The recovery program by the step, and admin.lisp:107 (999573a0, 3ede2018)

`books/byte-store-k0-recover-program.lisp` (new).

- `fn-bs-k0v-recover-program-by-step`: from any byte state related to the
  kernel the host's reopen installs, `(fn-bs-recovered-kernel f r 0)`, with
  anything pending:
  - every step of `fn-bs-recover-program` satisfies `fn-bs-k0-step-inputp`
    at the pair the run reaches before it (`fn-bs-k0v-steps-coveredp`);
  - every pair of the run is related;
  - the run has 17 pairs and ends at `(fn-bs-k0v-drained bs)`, where the
    `:transactions`, `:root` and `:parent` fences have been applied and
    authority is quiet, with the kernel `:ready`.

  The run itself is `fn-bs-k0v-recover-program-run`. The quiet-image
  theorems of `byte-store-k0-recovery` are the case with nothing pending.
- `fn-bs-k0v-host-rerecovery-keeps-relation-at-every-cut` covers the host
  line `host/native/admin.lisp:107`, `(fnn-recover store)` in
  `fnn-admin-verify-under-lock`, which runs after `fnn-bridge-reset`.
  `fnn-recover` reads the live view and reaches `fn-cpo-open-observed`
  through `fnn-bridge-recover` and `fn-store-sn-recover`. The hypotheses
  are three:
  - the pair `(bs, ks)` is related;
  - `:root` and `:transactions` are quiet;
  - the open over `(fn-bs-scan-store bs)` succeeds.

  Under them, the host's kernel is related to `bs` itself (the live state,
  not a crash image). Every step of the recovery program run from it is
  covered, and every pair is related. The entry is
  `fn-bs-k0v-quiet-pair-relates-to-rerecovered-kernel`: with quiet
  authority the live scan is the durable frontier and records
  (`fn-bs-k0v-quiet-scan-is-durable`), which the old kernel admits
  (`fn-bs-k0v-quiet-kernel-admits-durable`). The old kernel may be in any
  phase and may carry successes; the reset discards it.

The quiet hypothesis is what the admin process has. It opened the store
through the same recovery, whose fences drain `:root` and
`:transactions`, and it then published only a configuration record, which
touches neither directory. The hypothesis is sufficient; it is not shown
to be necessary.

## 3. `:mkdir` and `:link-eexist`: a pre-init relation (c0efbc85)

`books/byte-store-k0-pre-init.lisp` (new).

- `fn-bs-k0i-pre-init-relation bs ks`: the kernel is the initial state,
  and `:root` has no allocation-frontier entry, durable or pending.
- `fn-bs-k0i-pre-init-crash-image-is-uninitialised`: every crash image of
  such a state has no frontier entry, and its scan faults.
- `fn-bs-k0i-step-preserves-pre-init`: every step kind except `:observe`,
  including `:mkdir` and `:link-eexist`, keeps the pre-init relation with
  any outcome, unless it names the frontier entry in `:root`
  (`fn-bs-k0i-step-inputp`). `fn-bs-k0i-run-keeps-pre-init` lifts this to
  every pair of a run.
- `fn-bs-k0i-current-init-prefix-keeps-pre-init`: every pair of the fresh
  initializer (`fn-bsi-current-init-program`) before its frontier
  publication is pre-init, starting from `*fn-bs-empty-store*` with any
  outcomes. `fn-bs-k0i-existing-config-publication-keeps-pre-init` does the
  same for the existing-config publication (`:link-eexist` onto
  config.json, as in the history-retry program).
- The join: `fn-bs-k0i-current-init-program-splits` writes the program as
  that prefix, followed by the frontier publication and the final fences.
  `fn-bsi-current-init-program-establishes-relation` (already in the tree)
  relates its last pair to the initial kernel.

## Teeth

`tests/acl2/byte-store-k0-recover-program-tests.lisp`. The witnesses are
reachable states where a process died with an entry issued but not fenced:

- the K5 fixture's second publication at attempted pair 10, with the link
  pending;
- its second allocation at attempted pair 11, with the frontier rename
  pending.

Both are related to their reopened kernels, and the full program
conclusion holds for both. The drained state differs from the entry state.
The link witness's records are one longer than its durable namespace. The
rename witness's durable frontier is the scanned frontier minus one. The
keystone holds at window arms including a barrier EIO on a quiet directory,
a file fsync EIO and `(:recovery-barrier :uncertain)`.

Each `must-fail` removes one hypothesis:

- **Barrier error, window step inputp.** A `:transactions` EIO that drops
  the pending link breaks the relation. The one that applies it keeps the
  relation, but the arm does not admit it.
- **Quiet clause, window step inputp.** The fifth barrier with the link
  still pending gives a `:ready` kernel that is not related.
- **Relation, program theorem.** The initial image under the reopened
  kernel.
- **Open success, host theorem.** No configuration record, so the open is
  refused.
- **Relation, host theorem.** A pending same-octet write to the config
  inode. The view, the scan and the open are unchanged, but the inode is
  not fenced.

Hypotheses with no separating instance:

- The fenced-target clause of `:fsync-file`: the recovery program fsyncs
  only authority inodes.
- The quiet hypothesis of the host theorem: the link-pending pair holds the
  conclusion too, and the test book asserts this.

`tests/acl2/byte-store-k0-pre-init-tests.lisp`: the witness is the fresh
prefix run with the sample config. Every step answers `:ok`, every pair is
pre-init, config.json is durable, and staging is still pending. There are
also an early EIO run, a failed `:mkdir`, and the existing-config
publication (10 pairs, with `:link-eexist` answering). Each `must-fail`
removes one hypothesis:

- **Step inputp.** A link onto the frontier name leaves the state not
  pre-init.
- **Relation.** An initialised store stays non-pre-init under `:cut`.

## Not done, and why

- **The frontier publication's pairs in init.** The pairs from the
  frontier link to the end of init are not proved. The pending frontier
  entry's dropped resolution is pre-init and its landed resolution should
  be related, but the landed state needs the config and frontier facts
  that the whole-program theorem tracks. The old `fn-bs-init-program`
  (byte-store-relation) is not restated either.
- **Barrier errors in the window.** A directory barrier's error outcome
  with an authority entry pending in that directory is not covered. The
  dropped entry changes the view, so the scan no longer matches the
  kernel. The host reports `(:recovery-barrier :uncertain)` and
  StoreIndeterminate.
- **`(:recover)` from `:replaying` with an entry pending.** Its `:fault`
  arm leaves the window while the entry is still pending. The host's
  reopen reaches the program at `:recovering`, where `(:recover)` stutters.
- **The administrative publication.** Its syscalls at admin.lisp (config
  directory, staging cleanup) have no byte program, so the relation at
  line 107 is a hypothesis of the host theorem.
- **Stutter lemma finding.** The existing global
  `fn-bs-recovered-kernel-recover-stutters` (byte-store-k0-recovery) did
  not fire as a rewrite rule in this world, even with
  `fn-bs-recovered-kernel` disabled. A local copy was used. Not
  investigated.
