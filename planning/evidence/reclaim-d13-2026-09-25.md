# Content reclamation under D13: the decision, the tombstone, what it keeps (2026-09-25)

Lane `reclaim-d13`, branch `lane/reclaim-d13` from dev `e366c633`. It
answers STO-010's row C ("content reclamation ... needs D13") with STO-014,
PRF-088 and SCN-048. The spike (`spike/storage`, `bbd12ffa`) prototyped
`spike-store reclaim` by rewriting records to header-plus-stub payloads;
nothing was taken from it but two lessons: the node model cannot release an
archive undertaking (the charge stays), and a resend of a reclaimed article
must still be a duplicate.

## The rule

`admin retention set keep-forever | released-by-all-holders |
release-after DAYS` (`books/native-admin.lisp` `fn-native-admin-plan`,
kind `:set-retention`; `host/native-admin-host.lisp` stages it through
`fn-store-cfg-peer-delta-record`, the owner's live arm through
`fn-native-admin-plan-deltas`). It stages two `:set-limit` rows,
`retention` 0/1/2 and `retention-days`, through the ordinary configuration
record (`books/reclaim-rule.lisp`). Limits, not policies:
`fn-cfg-row-upsert` keys a row on (a, b), so two `policy set retention X`
records with different ids would both survive and the first would win.
Finding: that is true of every `policy set` slot today (only
`path-identity` exists, and it is set once).

- `fn-rcl-config-rule-after-retention-set`: under `fn-rcl-rulep rule`,
  `(fn-rcl-config-rule (fn-cfg-apply v gen stamp (fn-rcl-rule-deltas rule)))
  = rule` for every prior value `v`. Replay applies the same `fn-cfg-apply`.
- No row reads keep-forever (`fn-rcl-config-rule-without-a-row-keeps-forever`,
  by definition, not cited), so every existing store behaves as before.

## The decision

`fn-rcl-verdict rule now holders verdicts article` (`books/store-reclaim.lisp`)
answers `:reclaimable` or the first reason the article stays:
`already-reclaimed`, `rule-keeps`, `rule-refused`, `too-recent`,
`verdict-needs-payload`, `held-reader-pin`, `held-consumer-cursor`,
`held-feed`, `held-bp-obligation`. Holders are
`(pins cursors feeds bp)`: a reader pin `(group . n)` holds that article; a
consumer cursor `(group . ack)` holds every number above `ack`; a feed
`(peer retiredp msgid...)` to a live peer holds what it lists; a BP
obligation names a Message-ID.

- `fn-rcl-reclaimable-is-no-obligation-names-it` (no hypothesis):
  reclaimable iff not a tombstone, the rule is a rule and not keep-forever,
  it permits the stamp at `now`, no authorship verdict, and no entry of
  `(fn-rcl-obligations h)` names the article.
- `fn-rcl-reclaim-never-touches-a-held-article`: if an obligation names the
  article, or the rule is keep-forever, `fn-rcl-reclaim-if-permitted` is the
  identity.

## The tombstone

`fn-rcl-tombstone-of payload msgid` (`books/store-reclaim.lisp`, layout in
`books/reclaim-tombstone.lisp`): NUL `FN-RCL1`, a source flag, SHA-256 of
the payload, SHA-256 of its D25 source under its own Path agent, the payload
length (u64), the agent length (u64) and the agent. It replaces only the
payload octets of the article record; the record keeps Message-ID,
sequence, txid, generation, groups, memberships, obligation id, content
subject, release evidence, charge and stamp. `fn-rcl-tombstonep` reads at
most 89 conses of any payload.

- `fn-rcl-reclaim-keeps-the-duplicate-history` (no hypothesis): every
  Message-ID is accepted after exactly when before; a reclaimed ID is
  refused by `fn-accept-prepare` again (witness in the test book).
- `fn-rcl-reclaim-keeps-every-binding`, `fn-rcl-reclaim-keeps-the-numbering`:
  msgid, groups, memberships, pin and stamp of every article; per-group next
  numbers, next txid and pending; every other article whole.
- `fn-rcl-reclaim-preserves-statep`: under `fn-statep`.
- `fn-rcl-prepare-commutes-with-reclaim`: under `fn-statep`, staging then
  reclaiming equals reclaiming then staging (acceptance never reads a stored
  payload). This is the replay step for every record other than the
  reclaimed one.
- `fn-rcl-existing-action-after-reclaim`: for M present and not already a
  tombstone, `fn-rcl-action-over` (the body of `fn-rcl-existing-action`,
  `fn-rcl-existing-action-is-action-over-by-definition`) is unchanged for
  every Message-ID, submission and group list, or SHA-256 collides on the
  compared pair (the submission against the removed payload, or their D25
  sources; collision resistance, about 2^128 work, is assumed, A-CRYPTO),
  or the submission names a different agent under which the removed payload
  still gives back a source. `fn-rcl-existing-action-is-pb-without-a-tombstone`:
  before any reclamation it is `fn-pb-existing-action`.

Decisions of the lifetimes table that reclamation leaves as they were:
duplicate (history), conflict (D25, above), numbering (nexts), verdict lookup
(`fn-sn-verdicts` is a separate slot reclamation does not touch, and an
article with a verdict is not reclaimed).

## Served

`fn-nntp-article-response` (`books/nntp-responses.lisp`) answers a
tombstone `423 article reclaimed` (number, current) or `430 article
reclaimed` (Message-ID), session unchanged.
`fn-nntp-msgid-retrieval-answers-reclaimed` is stated of
`fn-nntp-msgid-retrieval-indexed`, which `fn-nntp-step` calls
(books/nntp.lisp), under the index correspondence the connection carries;
`fn-nntp-number-retrieval-answers-reclaimed` of `fn-nntp-number-retrieval`
(`books/nntp-reclaimed.lisp`). The branch-level theorem in nntp-responses is
a branch of the definition and is not cited.

Opening `fn-rcl-tombstonep` inside nntp-responses' own retrieval proofs
took that book from 1.7 s to 56.6 s at 2 jobs on persvati (manifest
`certify-20260925T091712Z-3132888`); it is closed locally there now.

## Teeth

`tests/acl2/store-reclaim-tests.lisp`: positive witnesses assert both
sides of the decision keystone for every holder kind (pin on 1 holds 1 not
2; cursor at 0 holds 1; cursor at 1 holds 2 not 1; feed live holds, retired
does not; BP; keep-forever; release-after 1 day at 86,400 s and 86,399 s;
a verdict). Hypothesis removals (`must-fail`): the rule keystone with
`(:release-after 0)`; the no-row default with a row; statep with a
non-state; prepare-commutes with a non-octet payload (reclaim repairs it,
the orders differ); the held keystone with no holder and a releasing rule;
D25 with a held tombstone reclaimed again (a resend becomes a conflict with
no collision); served with a live article, no group, and no identifier.
Unmet: the `(fn-find-article m articles)` hypothesis of the D25 keystone has
no counterexample (a list without M is unchanged); it stays because the
proof over lists containing NIL articles did not go through without it.

## Not done (open, in order of what blocks the claim)

1. `store reclaim` and `store reclaim --dry-run` on the native host: read
   holders, rewrite each reclaimable record's transaction file with the
   tombstone payload through a stage/fsync/rename/fsync program with named
   cuts under K0, drop the derived state checkpoint, refuse records inside a
   pack. Nothing on disk is reclaimed by this packet.
2. `status` counts: `fn-rcl-summary` computes reclaimable and reclaimed
   counts and octets; the host does not print them yet.
3. Native N=5,000 run (SCN-048, planned) and the cut campaign.
4. The reclaimed record's own replay step (`fn-accept-complete` of the
   tombstone-staged record equals reclaiming the original): the direct proof
   split on `floor` and did not return in 60 s.
5. Holders are an argument: the consumer projection, feed state and FNBS
   rows are not read yet; reader pins have no durable form in the tree.
6. The retention ledger keeps the reclaimed article's charge (the node
   cannot release an archive undertaking); admission headroom does not grow.
7. Articles with an authorship verdict are refused; the tombstone would
   have to carry the verdict and recovery skip re-verification.
8. OVER by Message-ID answers 503 for a reclaimed article and NEWNEWS lists
   it; the brief asks for both to drop it.
