## What is proved

Keystone: `fn-bs-step-preserves-k0-coverage` (books/byte-store-k0-step.lisp).
Its subject is `fn-bs-step`, the byte-model step that every `fn-bs-*-program`
runs through. No host line calls it. The native host is tied to the programs
only by `tools/native_program_check.py`, a static check that the host's
syscalls appear in the programs' order. This is the same model-only status as
the rest of PRF-041's K0 events.

1. **The root barrier from any related state (PKT-083).**
   `fn-bs-k0s-root-fence-preserves-relation`
   (books/byte-store-k0-step-root-fence.lisp, new):

   ```lisp
   (implies (and (fn-bs-store-relation bs ks) (not (fn-bs-replay-visiblep ks)))
            (fn-bs-store-relation (fn-bs-fence-dir bs :root) ks))
   ```

   If nothing is pending on `:root`, the fence is the identity
   (`fn-bs-k0r-quiet-root-fence-is-identity`). Otherwise the relation's
   pending shape forces the root operation to be the frontier rename's one
   set-entry, onto a fenced inode whose content decodes to the kernel's
   frontier candidate. The kernel is in a frontier-new-visible phase and no
   transaction link is pending (`fn-bs-k0r-related-root-pending-facts`). The
   fence makes the entry durable, and the frontier arm of
   `fn-sf-crash-imagep` admits that value. It leaves the config entry, the
   transaction table, the records and every inode as they were. The lemma is
   the transaction-barrier lemma `fn-bs-k8-pending-link-fence-preserves-relation`
   restated for `:root`.
   `fn-bs-k0-step-inputp` gains the arm
   `(and (fn-bs-store-relation bs ks) (equal d1 :root) (equal outcome :ok))`
   under `:fsync-dir`. The existing marker arm (any outcome over a pending
   committed-history rename) is unchanged. The keystone's statement is
   unchanged. Its fsync-dir case lemma cites the new fence lemma.

2. **The stage prefix, by the keystone (PKT-084).**
   books/byte-store-k0-step-bridge-prefix.lisp (new). The frontier and
   record programs both begin with create, write-all and fsync-file on a
   fresh staging name. The states after these steps are `fn-bs-k0p-s1`,
   `-s2` and `-s3`, defined through the syscalls themselves, so empty octets
   are covered too. The marker's `fn-bs-marker-b1..b3` need non-empty
   octets. `fn-bs-k0p-stage-pairs-by-step` derives the relation at all
   three states from the entry pair by three instances of the keystone. Its
   hypotheses: the pair is related and outside the recovery window, `:root`
   and `:transactions` are quiet, the stage is a name and absent, and the
   octets are typed. `fn-bs-k0p-frontier-run-prefix` and
   `fn-bs-k0p-record-run-prefix` restate each program's run up to its
   staged-durable cut as these states. Each is a shape fact by
   definitional unrolling, with the run's continuation left as a
   `fn-bs-run` term. Then, with the per-cut theorem's hypotheses exactly
   (related, `fn-bs-frontier-inputp` / `fn-bs-record-inputp`, the stage
   absent):
   - `fn-bs-k0-frontier-created-and-written-cut-relation-by-step`, pairs 2 and 4;
   - `fn-bs-k0-frontier-staged-durable-cut-relation-by-step`, pair 7: the keystone at the `(:frontier-file :ok)` observation;
   - `fn-bs-k0-record-created-and-written-cut-relation-by-step`, pairs 1 and 3;
   - `fn-bs-k0-record-staged-durable-cut-relation-by-step`, pair 6: the keystone at `(:record-file :ok)`.

3. **The record-linked cut (PKT-085).**
   `fn-bs-k0-record-linked-cut-relation-by-step` (pair 8) is the keystone at
   pair 6's link step. `fn-bs-k0p-record-link-inputs` discharges the step's
   inputs there and shows that the link returns `:ok`:
   - the name is `(fn-bs-txn-name (len (fn-bs-durable-names s3 :transactions)))`,
     because the durable names are the entry's
     (`fn-bs-k6-file-cut-dirs-are-input-dirs`), their count is the record
     count (`fn-bs-k6-related-staged-durable-name-count-is-record-count`),
     and the candidate's sequence is that count.
     `fn-bs-k0p-staged-candidate-is-next-sequence` exports this last fact.
     It is a copy of a local lemma of byte-store-record-provenance-bytes,
     which this lane did not change;
   - the source is fenced, allocated and holds the frame
     (`fn-bs-k6-file-cut-source-is-fenced-frame`, `fn-bs-k0-file-cut-has-new-inode`),
     so it reads as the candidate;
   - the destination is absent (`fn-bs-k6-related-input-file-cut-final-name-absent`);
   - the durable record list is exactly the kernel's
     (`fn-bs-k0p-record-staged-durable-records`). The relation at pair 6
     leaves two possibilities, the kernel's list or that list with the
     candidate appended. The durable list's length is the name count, which
     equals the kernel's record count, so it is the kernel's list.

4. **The frontier-durable cut (PKT-083).**
   `fn-bs-k0-frontier-durable-cut-relation-by-step`
   (books/byte-store-k0-step-bridge-frontier.lisp, new), pair 13, is the
   keystone at pair 11's root barrier with the new `:ok` arm.

5. **The chains are by the step end to end.** In
   books/byte-store-k0-step-bridge.lisp, `fn-bs-k0-frontier-replaced-cut-relation-by-step`
   now takes its predecessor from the staged-durable corollary, and
   `fn-bs-k0-record-attempted-cut-relation-by-step` and `fn-bs-k0b-record-linked-kernel`
   take theirs from the linked corollary. `fn-bs-k0-frontier-reserved-cut-relation-by-step`
   moved to -bridge-frontier and takes its predecessor from the durable
   corollary. Every frontier and record `-by-step` theorem is therefore
   derived from the entry pair by the keystone. The per-program lemmas
   supply only shape facts: lookups, phases, pending entries and
   reachability. The generic run lemmas (`fn-bs-k0b-cut-after-step` and its
   neighbours, `fn-bs-k0b-record-program-steps`, `fn-bs-k0b-run-consp-backward`,
   `fn-bs-k0b-syscall-carries-kernel`) moved from -bridge to -bridge-prefix
   unchanged.
   The per-cut theorems of byte-store-k0, -k0-staging and -k0-marker stay in
   place. PRF-041 lists both.

## Teeth (tests/acl2/byte-store-k0-cuts-tests.lisp)

Witnesses are reachable: the K5 fixture's second allocation (`bsk0-f-good`)
and second publication (`bsk5-record-2-run`).

- Root fence: the frontier run's attempted pair 11 (related, the frontier
  rename pending on `:root`, `:frontier-attempted`). There the fence changes
  the state, the relation holds after it, and the keystone's precondition
  and conclusion hold for `(:fsync-dir :root) :ok`. The quiet-root case is
  witnessed at the record run's attempted pair 10. `must-fail` for the
  relation: the initial byte image under the same kernel. The
  recovery-window hypothesis has no `must-fail`. Every kind of
  `fn-bs-k0-step-inputp` carries this guard, and the fence is not shown to
  need it.
- Stage prefix: the witness is the second publication's entry pair and its
  frame, and `s3` equals the record run's pair 5. There is a `must-fail`
  for each of four hypotheses: the relation (initial image), a name (stage
  `7`), the stage absent (occupied by inode 0, which the write then
  reaches), and typed octets (`(300)`). The quiet `:root` and
  `:transactions` directories and the recovery-window guard are the
  keystone's step-input guards, and the conclusion is not shown to need
  them. A test, not a tooth, shows that the conclusion holds at the frontier
  run's replaced pair, where a rename is pending on `:root`.
- Corollaries: at both witnesses every hypothesis holds, and every claimed
  pair is related (frontier 2, 4, 7, 13; record 1, 3, 6, 8). The link's
  step inputs hold at pair 6. For each program there is a `must-fail` for
  each of three hypotheses: the relation (initial image), the input
  contract, and the absent stage (the run stops at its first syscall). The
  input contract is broken by its octet type `(300)`. For the record there
  is also a frame broken case: the first record's frame under the second
  name, which leaves pair 8 unrelated.

  crash selection of `:apply`/`:drop`) are not step kinds. Only `:ok` is

  `:mkdir`/`:link-eexist`, the `:staging` and `:transactions` barriers'

  above).

  stale and nothing else.
