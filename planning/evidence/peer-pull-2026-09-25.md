# peer-pull: the NEWNEWS pull feed as owner work (PRF-100, 2026-09-25)

Dev lane `lane/peer-pull` from `dev` 9034f767. It re-implements theorem 6 of
the spike record (`spike/mega:planning/evidence/spike-peering-2026-09-25.md`):
the pull feed that the spike ran from `tools/fn_peering.py pull` with a JSON
cursor. Everything here is `:logic`, guard-verified, with no SPIKE marks.

## What was built

- `books/peer-pull.lisp` (prefix `fn-pull-`): the round machine the owner
  steps (`fn-pull-begin`, `fn-pull-step`, `fn-pull-close`), the cursor
  `(peer since advances)`, the FNPL journal (codec on the generic frame
  grammar, magic `FNPL`, the FNFD envelope, `fn-pull-journal-scan`,
  `fn-pull-replay`) and the pulled peers read from configuration rows
  (`fn-pull-plans`).
- `books/scheduler-peers.lisp`: the pull schedule table `fn-sched-pull-*`
  beside the feed scheduler table; no existing event changed.
- `books/peer-carriage-rows.lisp`: the `pull-interval` slot is single-valued
  like the budget rows (a new `peer pull` supersedes the old interval).
- `books/native-admin-peer.lisp`, `books/native-admin.lisp`:
  `fn operator CONFIG peer pull NAME SECONDS` as an `:extend-peer` plan.
- `host/owner-host.lisp` `fn-owner-pull-plans`; NEW
  `host/native/pull-service.lisp` (the worker, a lifecycle hook beside
  feed-service, registered in `host/native/operator.lisp` and loaded by
  `host/native/build.lisp`).

How a round runs: DATE; `NEWNEWS <the peer's inbound accept-groups> <since>
GMT`; for each listed Message-ID, `IHAVE` to THIS node on a logical transit
connection of that peer (`fn-owner-open-peer` and `fnn-owner-handle-chunk`,
the listener's own entry for that peer), so the answer is the node's own
served decision; `ARTICLE` from the peer only after a local 335, its octets
forwarded unchanged as the IHAVE body; QUIT. The spike instead fetched every
article first and injected over TCP from a bound source address.

Design choice, stated: the cursor is persisted in `<store>/pull/` as FNPL
frames in FNFD's envelope, with FNFD's phase machine (`fn-feed-journal-phase-
step`, every non-durable append fences) and filename codec, not as a Store
transaction record. A new Store event family touches about 25 books
(store-events, store-node, replay, the carried/commit/identity invariants,
records-concrete, checkpoint); the configuration history is bounded by the
operator's `max-config-generations`, so it cannot carry a cursor that moves
every round. The deputy may want the Store record; that is a follow-up.

## Theorems (books/peer-pull.lisp unless named)

Host lines: `host/native/pull-service.lisp` `fnn-pull-round` calls
`fn-pull-begin`, `fn-pull-begin-effects`, `fn-pull-step-pair` (=
`fn-pull-step` as a list), `fn-pull-close-effects`, `fn-pull-close`;
`fnn-pull-journal-open` calls `fn-pull-journal-scan` and `fn-pull-replay`;
`fnn-pull-worker` calls `fn-pull-schedule`, `fn-sched-pull-due`,
`fn-sched-pull-start`, `fn-sched-pull-finish` and (through
`fn-owner-pull-plans`) `fn-pull-plans`.

- `fn-pull-close-advances-only-past-a-fully-answered-round`: if
  `(fn-pull-close r)` differs from the round's cursor, the round's phase is
  `:done`, `fn-pull-all-answeredp` holds of its listed ids and answers (each
  is 235, 435 or 437), and the new instant is the peer's DATE less 1000 ms.
- `fn-pull-unanswered-id-holds-the-cursor`: a listed id whose answer is not
  terminal makes `fn-pull-close` the round's own cursor.
- `fn-pull-step-answers-only-from-the-local-node`: a step that changes the
  answers had a `(:local . octets)` event and added exactly
  `(current . (fn-pull-local-code octets))`.
- `fn-pull-step-keeps-the-cursor-and-journals-nothing`: no step changes the
  round's cursor or wildmat or emits a `:journal` effect.
- `fn-pull-step-newnews-names-the-cursor`: every NEWNEWS a step sends is
  `(fn-pull-newnews-octets wildmat since)` of the round.
- `fn-pull-journal-is-the-cursor-at-every-cut`: for a startable cursor C
  that the journal J replays to, the replay after the begin's records is the
  round's cursor, after any events (which journal nothing) still the run's
  cursor, and after the close's records the closed cursor.
- `fn-pull-recovery-asks-the-dead-rounds-newnews`: the round begun after
  replaying the journal of a round that died anywhere asks that round's
  instant.
- `fn-pull-decode-of-encode`: every FNPL record decodes to its kind and values.
- `books/scheduler-peers.lisp`: `fn-sched-pull-due-is-due-and-idle`,
  `fn-sched-pull-started-is-not-due`, `fn-sched-pull-finished-waits-its-interval`.

Not proved, and named: that the peer lists again what it listed for the same
instant is RFC 3977 section 7.4's NEWNEWS semantics; the "never skips an id"
claim is these theorems plus that.

## Teeth (tests/acl2/peer-pull-tests.lisp)

A whole round as the host drives it (greeting, DATE, NEWNEWS with a repeated
id, local greeting, 335, forwarded article, 235, 435) and a second round
differing in one reply (436). Witnesses for every keystone; `must-fail` per
hypothesis: the held round's ids are not all answered; the good round moves
(no unanswered listed id); a peer's 435 records nothing and is not local; a
journal replaying to an older cursor, and a cursor with a non-natural advance
count, each break the cut correspondence; starting over from the fresh cursor
asks a later instant; the schedule is due at exactly NOW plus the interval
and never for the nil key. The FNPL trailer digest (A-CRYPTO) is not
evaluable in ACL2, so the sealed-frame scan is left to the native run; the
codec round trip and the torn-prefix repair are tested.

## Not done, and why

- The pull client has no TLS arm; a TLS transport is not pulled.
- A peer that keeps listing an id it cannot produce (430) holds the cursor
  until it stops listing it: the theorem's statement, a liveness cost.
- The cursor is an FNPL journal under the store root, not a Store record.
- The spike's `tools/fn_peering.py pull` still exists on the spike branch
  only; dev never had it.
