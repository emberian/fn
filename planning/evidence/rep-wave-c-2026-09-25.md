## 1. The harness and the counter (hbox, `/tank/fn/scratch/rep-wave-c/`)

The wave B record could not measure N = 10,000 or 32 KiB (the CLI `store
init` takes no profile and the `:development` profile refuses both) and its
call counter wrote nothing. Both are fixed here; the scripts are in
`rep-wave-c-2026-09-25/`.

- **The profile.** `load_prof.py` initialises the store through the
  operator verb, `operator CONFIG init --profile default GROUP`
  (books/native-operator.lisp `fn-nop-profile-preset-word`; the `:default`
  profile is `*fn-bs-profile-defaults*`, books/byte-store-frame.lisp: the
  transaction ceiling, 1 TiB of history, 16 MiB articles), decided by ACL2
  as the brief asked; the host names no bound. N = 10,000 articles of
  32 KiB are admitted (`init.out` in every run's work directory).
- **The counter.** `prof-raw.lisp` is the profiling entry (spliced before
  `(defttag nil)` into the prof image by `build.sh`, as the served-path-cost
  hook was); it loads `FN_PROF_LOAD` before `fnn-main` and starts one thread
  that waits for `FN_SPROF_DIR/start`. `count.lisp`, loaded at run time,
  wraps the `fdefinition` of each named function to count its calls while
  the thread has set `*fnp-active*`; at `stop` the thread writes
  `counts.txt` (or, without `FN_PROF_COUNT`, the sprof `flat.txt` and
  `graph.txt`), and any error in the thread goes to `error.txt`. Why the
  old one wrote nothing is not diagnosed (its `sb-profile` symbol lookup ran
  inside the thread with no error path); this one counts through the
  function cell, which every full call goes through, and reports a name the
  image lacks on stderr instead of silently profiling nothing.
- **Rounds.** `round.sh LABEL R`: the counts at N = 120 (2 KiB, the last 48
  POSTs), a CPU profile at N = 120 (32 KiB, the last 48), then the sized
  loads on the developer image, 2 KiB and 32 KiB by N = 120 and N = 10,000,
  every POST timed, RSS at a quarter and at the end. `rounds.sh` alternates
  base and after for three rounds under `systemd-run --user -p
  MemoryMax=40G`.

**Smoke test on the base image** (dev `3f326d40`, N = 12 at 2 KiB, the last
four POSTs counted; `smoke-count.json`): per served POST, `fnn-octet-list`
30, `fn-owner-chunk` 6 (six socket reads per POST at this size),
`fnn-metadata` 2 and `fnn-subject-id` 2 (the payload was digested twice per
POST, once at the attempt and once at the finish; both converted the payload

existing-article test and again inside the prepare), `fn-octets$c-list` 1
(the record's payload), `fnn-octets-fill` 1, `fnn-frame` 1, `fnn-trailer` 1.

## 2. What changed

- `books/sha256-buffer.lisp` (new; prefix `fn-shb-`, docs/prefixes.md). The
  third message reader for the word stobj of books/sha256-stobj.lisp: the
  padded message `(append PREFIX buffer)` at index i is the prefix's octet
  below the prefix length, the buffer's cell at i − lp below the message
  length (read by `fn-octets-get`, the abstract stobj's array read) and the
  padding past it (`fn-shb-byte`); the word loader, the block loader, the
  compression loop and the digest are the string reader's with the buffer
  and the prefix in place of the string (`fn-shb-load-word`,
  `fn-shb-load-block`, `fn-shb-compress`, `fn-shb-blocks`, `fn-shb-digest`).
  The entry `fn-sha256-of-prefixed-buffer` digests over a local word stobj;
  `fn-shb-subject-id` is the subject identity (`fn-id-subject` of the
  digest of `(fn-id-subject-prefix (len buffer))` then the buffer).
  Everything is guard-verified. sha256-stobj is untouched: an
  `include-book` of octets-stobj there would send its 300 dependents back
  to certification for no change in any theorem, so the reader is a book
  beside it, and the few recognizer-preservation facts it needs are
  restated locally.

  `fnn-core-buffer-state`) and `fnn-metadata-buffer` (`fnn-metadata` with the

## 3. Theorems (`books/sha256-buffer.lisp`)

| Theorem | Statement | Host line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fn-sha256-of-prefixed-buffer-is-sha256` (keystone) | `(equal (fn-sha256-of-prefixed-buffer prefix fn-octets) (fn-sha256 (append prefix fn-octets)))`, no hypothesis: on every prefix and every value the buffer digest is the list model of the appended message | through `fn-shb-subject-id` |
| `fn-sha256-of-prefixed-buffer-is-sha256-of-octets` | `(implies (and (fn-cbor-octet-listp prefix) (fn-octets-p fn-octets)) (equal (fn-sha256-of-prefixed-buffer prefix fn-octets) (fn-sha256-of-octets (append prefix fn-octets))))`: the instance on an octet prefix and a buffer value (`:rule-classes nil`; the hypotheses are proof support, as in sha256-stobj's instance: the sides agree on every object by the keystone) | - |
| `fn-shb-subject-id-is-id-subject-of-sha256-preimage` | `(equal (fn-shb-subject-id fn-octets) (fn-id-subject (fn-sha256 (fn-id-subject-preimage fn-octets))))`, no hypothesis: `fn-id-subject-of-payload` (books/identity.lisp) with `fn-sha256` in the place of the constrained `fn-frame-digest`, which is what books/crypto-attach.lisp attaches (`fn-sha256-stobj`, equal to `fn-sha256` by `fn-sha256-stobj-is-sha256`), so on the host image the buffer identity and the list identity are the same octets | `host/owner-host.lisp` `fn-owner-subject-id-buffer`, called by `host/native/io.lisp` `fnn-subject-id-buffer` under `fnn-metadata-buffer`, called by `host/native/owner.lisp` `fnn-owner-attempt` (the served POST) |

The keystone's proof is the correspondence of the buffer reader with the
list reader on the list's suffix, each a local lemma: the byte
(`fn-shb-list-byte-is-byte`: `fn-shs-list-byte` at index i on the suffix
at i is the buffer byte and the suffix at i + 1), the loaded word
(`fn-shb-load-list-word-is-load-word`), the loaded block
(`fn-shb-load-list-block-is-load-block`), the compressed block
(`fn-shb-compress-list-is-compress`), the block loop
(`fn-shb-blocks-list-is-blocks`) and the digest
(`fn-shb-digest-list-is-digest`); then `fn-sha256-stobj` is `fn-sha256` by
sha256-stobj's keystone. The suffix is `fn-shb-rest`, `nthcdr` that stops
at an atom, so that an improper value needs no hypothesis.

Not proved, and unchanged: collision and preimage resistance (A-CRYPTO).
The host discipline that the buffer holds the payload from the fill to the
prepare is A-HOST, as for boundary 6.

## 4. Teeth (`tests/acl2/sha256-buffer-tests.lisp`)

Both keystones have no hypothesis, so no must-fail case exists for them.
Every function the host may reach is `:common-lisp-compliant` (asserted).
On a live local buffer (`shbt-digest`: `fn-octets-from-list`, then the
digest in place): the FIPS 180-4 vectors (empty; "abc" with all of it in
the prefix, split, all in the buffer; the 56-octet message split at 0, 18
and 56; the 112-octet message split at 0 and 18) against their known
digests; the padding boundaries 55, 56, 63, 64, 65, 119, 120 and the
lengths 1,000 and 3,000 (the buffer past its first 1,024 cells, two
doublings) against `fn-sha256-stobj` of the appended list; the split does
not matter and the octets do (`(1)|(2)` = `nil|(1 2)` = `(1 2)|nil`, not
`(2)|(1)`, not `(1)|(3)`, one octet of 3,000 changed). The subject identity
(`shbt-subject`) on a 160-octet article, the empty payload and a
3,000-octet payload equals `fn-id-subject-of-payload` of the same octets
under the attachment (books/crypto-attach.lisp is included) and equals the
theorem's right-hand side, is `fn-id-subjectp`, and differs from the
identity of a changed payload and from `fn-id-subject` of the bare digest.

## 5. What is NOT done, and why

- **The owner state's representation (task 2).** The wire machine
  (`fn-scar-step-counted-fast` → `fn-served-feed-counted`, books/served-carried.lisp)
  consumes the socket read as a list into the connection's retained octets,
  and the POST body accumulates in the session state (books/nntp-post.lisp)
  before `fn-own-take` copies it into `fn-own-sub-octets`. Reading the chunk
  into the buffer without rewriting the line reader by index only moves the
  cons from the host to ACL2 (`fn-octets-list` at the boundary): a
  by-definition theorem and no cost change, which the brief's rule ("the
  escape hatch only where a theorem names it") rightly excludes. The real
  step is the design's boundary 10 (the owner as an abstract stobj: the
  retained octets and the in-flight body as arrays, one export per
  host-called entry), a lane of its own after the census; it is not started
  here. The counter says what it will remove: six `fn-owner-chunk` list
  conversions per 2 KiB POST.
- **Boundaries 7 to 9.** The spike's `books/frame-stobj.lisp`,
  `records-stobj.lisp` and `article-stobj.lisp` are the shape (their
  correspondences deferred under D28). The frame seal needs the digest of a
  *prefix range* of the buffer; `fn-sha256-of-prefixed-buffer` digests the
  whole buffer after a list prefix, and the range reader is the same lemma
  chain with a bound in place of the length (a `take` in the message). Not
  started.
- The obligation identity (`fnn-obligation-id`) and the frame trailer
  (`fnn-trailer`) still digest short lists (the Message-ID and a 32-octet
  identity; the protected prefix): one list per POST each, boundary 8's.
- `planning/ledger.*` is the coordinator's regeneration.
