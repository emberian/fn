## 1. Queued jobs are routed (finding 1 of bp-routing-2026-09-25)

Before this lane, a base FNBS job kept the TCPCL address it was queued with,
taken from the command line. Base jobs are A's request carrier
(`bp-obligation request`), B's receipts (`bp-node serve`/dispatch outbox) and
status reports.

**ACL2** (`books/bp-route-jobs.lisp`):
- `fn-bprt-job-route` runs at queue time. It returns the routed hop's
  `contact` port, keeping the job's own session parameters.
- `fn-bprt-send-decision` runs at contact time over the table as it is now.
  It returns `(:send ROUTE' HOP EID)` or `(:held :no-route | :no-live-hop)`.

Keystones:
- `fn-bprt-send-decision-offers-only-the-routed-hop`: a `:send` names exactly
  `fn-bprt-next-hop`'s boundary, on that boundary's contact port.
- `fn-bprt-send-decision-holds-without-a-route`.

The teeth are in `tests/acl2/bp-route-jobs-tests.lisp`:
- the table changed after queueing, so the job goes to the new better hop and
  never to the old one (a must-fail);
- route removed gives `(:held :no-route)`;
- a boundary with no contact gives `(:held :no-live-hop)`;
- another destination is not routed (a must-fail).

**Host** (`host/native/bp-service.lisp`):
- `fnn-bps-peer-routed-p` gates every opening contact:
  - `fnn-bps-attempt-ready`;
  - `fnn-bpc-drive-contact`, which serves both `bp-contact tick` and serve's
    receipt contact.
- `fnn-bps-send-effect` asks `fn-bprt-send-decision` for the route. It then
  passes the hop's enrolled EID as the expected TCPCL peer. Before this, the
  expected peer was `nil`.
- `fnn-bps-queue-route` is the queue-time choice, used at these sites:
  - `bp-node.lisp` outbox;
  - `bp-node.lisp` report queue;
  - `bp-obligation.lisp` request carrier.

Where the route table comes from:
- `bp-node serve` reads the live owner table.
- `bp-obligation request`, `bp-service resume [STORE]` and
  `bp-contact tick ... [STORE]` read the Store's table.
- A command without a Store keeps the queued address, i.e. unrouted, which is
  the old behaviour.

**Native, route removed** (dtn7 lab `--native --no-cuts --routes removed`,
production DTN image, lab2/r1-removed-native):
- A's request logs `BP queue route destination=dtn://receiver/
  decision=no-route` and then `BP queued job no-route ... (held; the
  obligation stays)`. Log a-1-send `536d0dff95bfffee`.
- `bp-service resume` holds it the same way. Log a-2-resume
  `ff4685ec2da56c1a`.
- B gets nothing (no verdict).
- A's work ends `status=restart-observed pinned=yes`: the pin is kept.

With routes present, the same request logs `BP queued job routed
hop=return-boundary port=…` and goes through (below).

## 2. Fragmentation and reassembly (RFC 9171 §5.8; RFC 9174 Transfer MRU)

RFC 9174 separates two limits:
- **Segment MRU.** fn's TCPCL session machine already segments within one
  transfer (`XFER_SEGMENT`).
- **Transfer MRU.** A bundle longer than the peer's Transfer MRU must be
  fragmented at the BP layer or not sent.

`books/bp-fragment-send.lisp` (`:program`, SPIKE) provides `fn-bpfs-plan WIRE
MRU`, which returns one of:
- `(:whole)`;
- `(:fragments W1..Wn)`, each at most MRU;
- `(:refused :no-fragment | :mru-too-small | :malformed)`.

How the fragments are built:
- Each fragment is the parent's primary block through `fn-bpf-fragment-block`
  (or `-refragment-block` for a fragment parent), so it has the fragment flag,
  the offset and the total ADU length.
- Its payload is one contiguous extent.
- **Every** extension block is replicated. The RFC requires only the
  replicate flag and leaves the rest to the BPA. A creation-time-zero bundle
  needs its Bundle Age block in each fragment.

`fnn-bps-send-effect` plans after SESS_INIT, from the negotiated Transfer MTU.
The first fragment rides that session; each further fragment gets its own
session to the same hop. The job's one attempt is `:accepted` only when every
fragment was acknowledged. After a first fragment has gone, a failure is
`:uncertain`, never `:failed`.

Receiving side: kind-5 custody of each fragment, then a kind-18 family
publication of the whole, then the A3 delivery. This was already fn's
behaviour: a fragment is held and never delivered until the family is
complete, and a partial set expires by the bundle's lifetime
(`bp-fragment-expiry-activation-2026-09-24`). This lane runs it end to end:
- **dtn7 lab, `--relays 0 --b-transfer-mru 4096 --article-lines 200`**, on
  the production image (lab2/r0-fragment-native):
  - A logs `BP fragmenting length=10339 peer-mru=4096 fragments=3`;
  - B takes three kind-5 fragments (ADU 3979 + 3979 + 2276), then the
    10457-octet kind-18 whole, written after the third;
  - then `BP node delivery request-accepted`, `receipt-accepted`, pin
    released.
  - Logs: a-1-send `3835939e885992fe`, b-serve-1 `f014f7d7135f91c9`, a-serve
    `3bf87d0117db46ea`.
  - Run 1 (lab/r0-fragment, bridge-authored request) is the same, with b-serve
    `e89fe656bf16c095`.
- **`tests.test_bp_fragment_node_native`: OK** (run 1, 64 s) on the DTN
  developer image. It covers a nonzero-offset fragment held, not delivered and
  surviving a restart, then offset zero, then one delivery.
- **ACL2 teeth** (`tests/acl2/bp-fragment-send-tests.lisp`):
  - at MRU 300 a 1000-octet ADU becomes more than one fragment, each at most
    300;
  - `fn-bpf-reassemble` of the decoded fragments is `(:ok payload)`, exactly;
  - each fragment unfragments to the parent primary and carries the parent's
    blocks;
  - a missing fragment is `:missing`, never `:ok` (a must-fail);
  - the no-fragment flag is refused;
  - an MRU below the overhead is refused.

Custody semantics: the Bundle-in-Bundle and custody-signalling drafts are not
supplied, and custody is not in RFC 9171. "Custody" here means fn's held-row
semantics:
- a durable kind-5 row holds each received fragment or bundle;
- it is released only by a durable successor record: family publication,
  delivery, forwarding result or expiry deletion;
- nothing is ever inferred from a transport acknowledgement.

## 5. N16-F1 (identifier reuse after rotation) and generation retirement

**N16-F1.** `fn-bpnr-rotation-checkpoint` publishes the checkpoint with
operation frontier `(EPOCH . 0)`, which is the rotation operation's own id.
`fnn-bps-publish-generation` writes that checkpoint. A reopen then takes epoch
EPOCH+1.

The teeth are in `tests/acl2/bp-node-counterexamples-tests.lisp`:
- the reopen event's epoch is 3;
- the held row is kept;
- the first new operation is `(3 0)`;
- a stale `(:persist-result 2 0 :durable)` completes nothing;
- a must-fail shows the unrepaired checkpoint reopens at 2.

The finding's own witness still holds of `fn-bpnr-checkpoint-of-event`.

**Retirement.** After the selection of G is durable,
`fnn-bps-retire-generations` removes what `fn-bpnr-retired-names` names:
- every generation directory below G, where `lifecycle` is generation 0;
- stray `.bp-generation-*` stages.

For each, it removes the files, then the directory, then runs the root
barrier. Rotation is admitted only with no legacy outbound record, so an old
directory holds only received rows the checkpoint covers. The teeth witness
that the selected directory is never retired.

Native `test_rotation_killed_at_each_cut_keeps_held_rows` (extended) **ok** in
both runs:
- kills at directory, stage and replace;
- then a clean rotation to g4, which logs `generation retired` for
  `lifecycle` and g1 to g3;
- `lifecycle` is gone and only g4 remains;
- held=1 recovers from the checkpoint alone;
- new work lands in g4 and a second rotation carries both rows.

Coordination: `spike/storage` (481fc710) has no BP generation cleanup (its P3
is Store checkpoints), so this lane owns it.

## 7. Deferrals (every `;; SPIKE:` site)

1. `books/bp-route-jobs.lisp`:
   - (a) There is no durable route-change record. A re-routed job's kind keeps
     its queue-time address, and the contact-time route is not persisted.
   - (b) There is no machine-level theorem that the base machine's `:cl-send`
     reaches only the routed hop. Today this is a host composition:
     `fnn-bps-send-effect` calls `fn-bprt-send-decision`.
   - (c) The contact host is loopback only.
2. `books/bp-fragment-send.lisp`:
   - (a) The book is in `:program` mode. The exact-reassembly theorem is
     missing (only witnesses exist).
   - (b) There are no per-fragment custody records: one attempt covers all
     fragments.
3. `books/bp-node-rotation.lisp`, `fn-bpnr-rotation-checkpoint`:
   - (a) The `:rotate` arm still admits the projection whose prior is the
     replay's, while the host publishes this checkpoint.
   - (b) The recovery keystone is not restated for this checkpoint.
4. `books/bp-node-rotation.lisp`, `fn-bpnr-retired-names`:
   - The retirement theorem is missing, and there is no model crash point for
     a partially removed directory.
   - Also missing: `…-never-the-selected-directory`, which needs the round
     trip of `fn-bpnr-generation-of-name` over `fn-bpnr-generation-directory`.
5. `host/native/bp-service.lisp`, fragmentation in `fnn-bps-send-effect`:
   aggregating fragment outcomes is a host decision.
6. `host/native/bp-service.lisp`, `fnn-bps-retire-generations`: removal is
   host-driven and not modelled.
7. `host/native/bp-service.lisp`, `*fnn-bps-connection-local-uncertain*`:
   - The connection-local rule for base transfers is a host decision.
   - Spec 4.3.1 states it only for forwarding.
   - The ACL2 owner and its theorem are deferred.
8. Commands with no Store (`bp-service run`, and `resume` or `tick` without
   `[STORE]`) keep the queued address, i.e. they are unrouted.
9. Fragmentation applies to base jobs only (`fnn-bps-send-effect`). Held
   transit over the routed `:session` still waits on the MRU
   (`fn-bpnp-forward-mru-waitp`).

## 8. What `dev` must prove to own this

- **Fragment reassembly is exact.** For a bundle B without the no-fragment
  flag and an MRU above the overhead:
  - the decoded views of `fn-bpfs-plan`'s fragments reassemble with
    `fn-bpf-reassemble` to `(:ok (fn-bpb-payload B))`;
  - every fragment is at most MRU;
  - every fragment's primary unfragments to B's primary;
  - any proper subset is not `:ok`.
  This must be stated over a guard-verified logic-mode `fn-bpfs-plan`, with
  its D27 concrete twin.
- **A routed queued job is offered only to the routed hop.** For every base
  machine state and table, a `:cl-send` effect the served step emits for a
  queued job carries the route `fn-bprt-send-decision` names for its
  destination over the current table. With no route, the step emits no
  `:cl-send` and the job and its obligation stay. Both must hold for the
  function the host calls, with the `:via`-style table in the contact event.
- **Route change is durable.** A new record kind (or field) fixes the address
  an attempt was made on, and replay agrees with it.
- **N16-F1.** The `:rotate` arm proposes the rotation checkpoint, and the
  recovery keystone holds with the event's epoch field: after reopen, no
  operation id equals the rotation's.
- **Retirement.** Recovery over the root without `fn-bpnr-retired-names`
  equals recovery with them, and every partial removal is a crash point whose
  recovery is the same.
- **Connection-local base transfer.** An `:uncertain` base transfer requeues
  and costs only its connection, as an ACL2 decision with the exit-code
  mapping stated.
- **Held transit fragmentation.** The `:session` arm fragments instead of
  waiting on the MRU, with per-fragment attempt records.
