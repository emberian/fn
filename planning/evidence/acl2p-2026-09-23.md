# ACL2(p) on fn's slow proofs: no gain, and not a toolchain to certify with (lane ACL2P, 2026-09-23)

Branch `tooling/acl2p` from `dev` 4b44c217. The question, from ember: "is
there any way to parallelize ACL2? I keep seeing only a single core in use."
The runner already runs books in parallel (`tools/certify_books.py --jobs`),
and [the profiler](certification-cost-2026-09-23.md) found the wall is one
chain of books in which one process runs alone. This lane measured whether
ACL2(p), the ACL2 build whose waterfall proves subgoals on several threads,
makes one large proof faster on our books, and whether its certificates are
interchangeable with the plain build's. No book was edited; nothing was
published to `/home/ember/fn-certcache`.

**Answer.** No. On a proof that does not split (`peer-inbound`'s 97-million-step
guard proof) ACL2(p) never used more than one core and took the same time,
880.8 s against 885.8 s. On the 29,280-way split (`bp-release-invariants`)
the recommended mode (`:resource-based`) took 276.2 s against 277.7 s. The
only mode that saved anything, `:full`, crashed with the default settings,
ran out of an 8 GB heap with the limit error disabled, and with a 32 GB heap
finished in 258.1 s, 7% faster, holding 20.3 GB and 5,380 threads where the
plain build held 0.78 GB and 2 threads. Most of that proof's time goes into
simplifying the one top goal, which produces the split, and that runs on one
thread in every mode. The subgoals come after it and are the only part the
waterfall can spread. The certificates are interchangeable in practice (below),
but ACL2's documentation advises against ACL2(p) for final certification, and
`tools/certs.py` counts it as a different toolchain.

One trap cost a rerun. The system books must be certified in ACL2's default
book-hash mode, as `w25`'s were, which means leaving `ACL2_BOOK_HASH_ALISTP`
unset. fn's certificates record each system book they include by
`(:book-length . n) (:book-write-date . d)`, and the tarball keeps the
write dates, so both trees then agree. With `ACL2_BOOK_HASH_ALISTP=NIL`
(the setting fn's runner uses for fn's own books) the ACL2(p) system books
got checksums, and every plain-certified fn book was reported "book
recertification is probably required".

Where the time goes in `bp-release-invariants`. This is the per-10 s cores
timeline of the `:resource-based` and 32 GB `:full` runs. The first 13 s
(the book's earlier, small events) use 5 to 6 cores. From about 20 s to
about 240 s one core is busy while the prover simplifies `Goal` into the
29,280 cases. Only after that do subgoals exist to hand out. `:full` then
spawns thousands of threads and reaches 2 to 3 cores for about 20 s.
`:resource-based` bounds the work, so it gains nothing. `peer-inbound`'s
guard proof has two subgoals and no split. Nothing in it is parallel, and
every mode sat at one core for 14 minutes.

ACL2(p) with waterfall parallelism off (`nil`) costs 3 to 9% over the plain
build on the same proof (301.6 s against 277.7 s, 913.8 s against 885.8 s).

## Interchangeability

What ACL2 says, verbatim, for each direction. The driver was
`(include-book "books/<book>")` in a fresh process, with the other build's
`.cert`, `.fasl` and `.port` installed:

- The `bp-release-invariants` certificate from the 32 GB `:full` run, included
  by plain ACL2: `Summary / Form: ( INCLUDE-BOOK "books/bp-release-invariants" ...) / Rules: NIL / Time: 0.31 seconds`. No warning.
- The plain `bp-release-invariants` certificate, included by ACL2(p): the same, 0.35 s. No warning.
- The `peer-inbound` certificate from the ACL2(p) `:full` run, included by plain ACL2: 0.71 s. No warning.
- The plain `peer-inbound` certificate, included by ACL2(p): 0.84 s. No warning.
- Plain ACL2 certified `peer-inbound-invariants` (dev bytes) on top of the
  ACL2(p)-certified `peer-inbound`: certified in 1.8 s. The log has no
  `Uncertified`, book-hash or compiled-file warning.
- Both builds name themselves `"ACL2 Version 8.7"`, and that string is the
  version each certificate records.

The certificate bytes:

- `bp-release-invariants.cert` is byte-identical (sha256
  `9b3d43aed9eb6c8bc9a03f424699d6370d7a33c5cc46d3d4b604349110056b84`) from
  plain, ACL2(p) `nil`, `:resource-based` and 32 GB `:full`. The `.fasl` files
  differ.
- `peer-inbound.cert` is not byte-identical. It is `e9c16dd0...` from plain and
  `6c447cad...` from both ACL2(p) runs. Read back with the ACL2 reader, the
  only differences are the 41 absolute rune numbers in the `cert-data`
  type-prescription records (each 21 higher under ACL2(p), whose boot-strap
  world defines more runes) and the certificate's trailing hash. The
  `bp-release-invariants` certificate carries no such records, so it matched.
- `tools/certs.py` would not treat them as one toolchain. The compatibility
  identity is built from the launcher, core and runtime hashes
  (`tools/acl2_toolchain.py`), and the cores differ, so the identities are
  `1b4169e9...` and `2e15de37...`. `install-set` groups cache entries by
  origin and that identity and installs one group, "never a per-book
  mixture". An ACL2(p) lane would get no hits from the plain cache, and its
  pairs would form a separate group.

What ACL2's documentation says (ACL2 8.7, `books/system/doc/acl2-doc.lisp`):

- `parallelism`, "IMPORTANT NOTE": "We hope and expect that every evaluation
  result is correctly computed by ACL2(p), and that every formula proved using
  ACL2(p) is a theorem of the ACL2 logic ... However, we do not guarantee these
  properties. Since ACL2(p) is intended to be an aid in efficient evaluation
  and proof development, we focus less on ironclad soundness." Also: "SBCL and
  Lispworks both currently sometimes experience problems when evaluating the
  ACL2 proof process (the "waterfall") in parallel. Therefore, CCL is the
  recommend Lisp." The hang above is one of those problems, on SBCL.
- `unsupported-waterfall-parallelism-features`: "A book certified with ACL2(p)
  might subsequently cause an error when included with ACL2. As of this
  writing, however, we have only seen this for a book in which
  `deftheory-static` is used." And: "we suggest using ACL2 for final book
  certification, rather than ACL2(p), to lower the risk of unsound book
  certification." With waterfall parallelism on, `with-prover-time-limit`
  and `accumulated-persistence` are unsupported. Those are the two tools the
  discovery rule (F2) and the ten-second rule in `planning/how-we-work.md`
  use. `override-hints` and clause processors that modify state need a trust
  tag; fn's books use neither.
- `set-waterfall-parallelism`: `:resource-based` "typically achieves the best
  performance ... while maintaining system stability" and is the recommended
  value. `:full` "always parallelize[s]". On SBCL, ACL2(p) does not read the
  core count and assumes 16 unless `ACL2_CORE_COUNT` is set. The form "will
  not affect waterfall-parallelism when including a certified book that
  contains that call". Put before `certify-book`, it did not enter the
  portcullis: the `bp-release-invariants` certificates are identical with and
  without it.
- `waterfall-parallelism-for-book-certification`: the supported way is a
  `<book>.acl2` with `#+acl2-par (set-waterfall-parallelism t)` before
  `certify-book`, which is the form this lane's driver used.

## Recommendation: neither the lane toolchain nor the freeze toolchain, and no book class

1. **No speedup where our time is.** The two slowest proofs the profiler
   found are one long rewrite of one goal. One has no split (0% gained). In
   the other, the split is produced serially (0% gained with the recommended
   mode, 7% with a mode that crashes by default and needs 26 times the
   memory). ACL2(p) helps only when many independent, costly subgoals come
   out of a cheap split. That shape is exactly what the ten-second rule
   repairs with hints, at 805 s to 3.7 s and 307 s to 2.3 s, and a repaired
   book leaves ACL2(p) nothing to spread.
2. **It costs even when off.** `nil` mode is 3 to 9% slower than plain.
3. **The freeze must not use it.** ACL2's own documentation disclaims
   soundness guarantees for ACL2(p) and advises plain ACL2 for final
   certification. fn's assurance rules do not allow a certification whose
   prover says it may be unsound.
4. **The lane toolchain should not use it either.** A lane on ACL2(p) would
   get no cache hits (different toolchain identity), would lose
   `with-prover-time-limit` and `accumulated-persistence` (the discovery
   tools), and on SBCL can hang instead of failing. A hang is the failure
   that F2 exists to stop.

The answer to ember's question is that one ACL2 process proves one goal on
one core, by design. fn already runs books in parallel. The remaining single
core in a run is the chain of large books, and the fix for that is to make
the large books small, which the cost lanes are doing.
The one ACL2 mechanism that takes the chain out of the wall is provisional
certification. [The profiler](certification-cost-2026-09-23.md) lists it, and
`triage-2026-09-22.md` records the decision that it is the discovery mode and
not the certification mode.

## What is unverified

- Every mode ran once. There is no variance figure, and the two books ran at
  the same time on disjoint CPUs, which shares memory bandwidth.
- Only SBCL was tried. ACL2's documentation recommends CCL for ACL2(p). A CCL
  ACL2(p) could avoid the hang, but it cannot remove a serial 220 s
  simplification.
- `:top-level` and `:resource-and-timing-based` were not run, and neither
  was any run with more than 8 cores. Given the timeline (one core for about
  220 of 258 s), more cores can speed up only about the last 20 s.
- Interchangeability was tested on two books and one dependent, not a tree.
  The rune-number difference in `cert-data` was harmless in the includes
  shown here. Whether ACL2 renumbers or ignores those numbers on include was
  not traced in ACL2's source.
- `peer-inbound`'s pre-fix bytes were certified against `dev` 4b44c217's
  closure, not 549c750a's. Its five direct includes are byte-identical
  between the two, and the step count matches the profiler's exactly.
