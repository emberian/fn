# Why 1,000 POSTs took 6 s in the harness and about 90 s in N's m3.py

Answer: the filesystem. The same client, article and image give a POST p50 of 5.7 ms on tmpfs and 142 ms on ZFS; each durable POST costs one fdatasync of the owner's log, and an fdatasync on hbox ZFS costs 100 to 150 ms when the box is loaded.

Evidence (CONVERGE-3 production image fn-host, dev 0b4d3b183, hbox load 20 to 23, filled preset, 1 connection, 20 s window, `post-rate` c1; runs pr-tmpfs and pr-zfs in this directory's siblings):

| work dir | POSTs in 20 s | POST p50 | p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| /dev/shm (tmpfs) | 2962 (148/s) | 5.7 ms | 10.3 ms |
| /tank/fn/scratch/load-harness/work-zfs (ZFS) | 120 (5.9/s) | 142 ms | 376 ms |

Mechanism, checked item by item:
- Connections and pipelining: both m3.py and the driver use one connection and one POST at a time (`POST`, wait for 340, write the body and the terminating dot, wait for the final line); nothing is pipelined in either, so the client cannot account for a 15x difference.
- Waiting for the durable 240: yes, in both. The driver times from the `POST` line to the 240, which is the durable POST. E's hook (prof2.lisp, `tops-img-psv` and `tops-core-hbox`) counts 1.0 to 1.03 fnn-durable-barrier/fnn-log-fdatasync calls per POST on the image and on fn-core: one sync per POST, so the sync cost is the POST cost on a slow-sync filesystem.
- Article shape: identical (`rep_measure.article(i, 2048)`: fixed header, 78-character body lines, 2048 octets, one group); m3.py and the driver import the same function.
- Where the store lived: m3.py wrote under /tank/fn/scratch/n-mem3b (ZFS) and took 82 to 96 s per 1000 POSTs (about 90 ms each, E measures about 88 ms on ZFS); the driver defaults to /dev/shm/fn-load-LABEL (tmpfs, 4.5 ms p50, 6 s per 1000).
- The 4 KiB write + fdatasync site probe (`site` in every cell, `site.fdatasync_p50_ms`) separates the two: tmpfs 0.001 to 0.002 ms; E measured ZFS on hbox 143 ms loaded and persvati 4.8 ms.

So the driver does measure the durable POST; its default store directory was the difference. `--work PATH` selects the filesystem (box passes it through), every result records `box.fs`, and the T-POST bar's p50/p99 checks apply only on ZFS (a tmpfs POST number is never read as a node number). The first W13 control (ctl-w13) differed from N's A arm by anon memory for the same reason: 1000 POSTs in 6 s leave more garbage than 1000 POSTs in 90 s.
