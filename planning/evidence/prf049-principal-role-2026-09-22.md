# PRF-049: the principal-bound inbound peer role (lane t9b-role, 2026-09-22)

Plan T9, row PRF-049; requirement HST-004.

## What is proved, and over what

The subjects are what the host calls. `fn-auth-step` is the dispatcher
`books/served.lisp` `fn-served-dispatch` calls, and host/owner-host.lisp
`fn-owner-chunk` reaches it through `fn-own-read` and `fn-served-step`.
`fn-ocfg-open` is what host/owner-host.lisp `fn-owner-open` calls.

- `fn-auth-step-binds-a-peer-role-only-by-a-principal-login`
  (books/nntp-auth.lisp). Two hypotheses: the session had no peer role, and
  the step's session has one. Conclusion: the session was well-formed, not
  handshaking and unauthenticated; its policy accepts the channel; the event
  was `AUTHINFO PASS` with one token after a USER, and that token checks
  against the cached name's verifier; the subject is that credential's
  principal; the role is `fn-auth-principal-match` of the principal under the
  pinned configuration; and the role is principal-derived.
- `fn-auth-step-principal-login-binds-exactly-the-unique-match`. Eleven
  hypotheses: a well-formed session; not handshaking; no peer role; no
  subject; the policy accepts the channel; the line is command input; the
  tokens are AUTHINFO, PASS and one token; a cached name; the secret checks.
  Conclusion: the reply is 281, the subject is the principal, and the role is
  exactly the match. That is a peer for one match, and a reader that is still
  authenticated for a mismatch or a duplicate. The argument-length bound is
  proved from the 510-octet line bound (`fn-auth-pass-line-arguments-are-in-bounds`),
  so it is not assumed.
- `fn-auth-step-starttls-clears-a-principal-role`. Two hypotheses: the
  session was not handshaking, and the step's session is. Conclusion: no
  subject, no cached name, no principal-derived role, and the old role is kept
  exactly when it was not principal-derived.
- `fn-ocfg-open-begins-unbound` (books/owner-config.lisp). One hypothesis:
  the open was accepted. Conclusion: a connection is at the allocated
  identifier, and it has no role, subject, cached name, TLS layer or
  handshake.

Protected-only before TLS is `fn-auth-step-protected-only-refuses-authinfo-before-tls`,
which belongs to T7 and PRF-031 and is cited, not restated. The first
keystone's channel conjunct gives the same result for the role.

Covered scope: one step of `fn-auth-step` and the OPEN transition of
`fn-ocfg-open`. Folding these over a whole read is OB-AUTH-FOLD's job. That a
wrong secret fails the check is A-CRYPTO; it is witnessed, not proved.

## The repair

`fn-auth-bind-principal-peer` used to bind on the auth-principal row count
alone. A `:set-peer` delta with keyed rows is admissible, so one row group
can carry both an auth-source-address row and an auth-principal row. The
record that group denotes is the source-address record. The old binding
gave that group the principal's role, and the role was not principal-derived,
so `fn-auth-clear-principal-peer` kept it across STARTTLS. The binding now
takes the peer only through `fn-auth-principal-match`, which reads the record
back and requires `(:principal HEX)`. The teeth include this table
(`*aut-cfg-shadow*`), what the old definition read from it, and the login
that now leaves the connection a reader.

## Teeth

- tests/acl2/nntp-auth-teeth-tests.lisp, keystones 8 to 10. Each has a
  reachable witness built from a real enrolled credential and a replayed peer
  table: one principal peer beside a source-address peer, a duplicate table,
  and a table with no principal row. Each hypothesis has one `must-fail` and
  an evaluated refuting value.
- tests/acl2/owner-config-tests.lisp, `fn-ocfg-open-begins-unbound`. The
  witness is a reconnect through the owner's own path: `fn-ocfg-read` logs in
  and binds, then `fn-ocfg-open` opens again, and the same happens after
  `fn-ocfg-close`. The one hypothesis has a `must-fail` and a refuting value,
  the full owner.
- The must-fails are generated by macros (`aut-tooth`, `ocfg-r-unbound-thm`).
  Each macro first admits its full statement from the keystone. Because
  tools/teeth_check.py does not expand macros, it does not count these
  must-fails.
