## 1. N16-F1 (PKT-057), commit 6591fb74

The machine used to publish the projection checkpoint, whose operation frontier
is the replay's. A reopen then took the rotating epoch E, and its first
operation reused the rotation's id (E 0).

**Change**:
- `fn-bpnr-rotation-checkpoint ck epoch` (books/bp-node-progress.lisp) is the
  projection with frontier `(epoch . 0)`.
- `fn-bpnp-rotate-step` admits the same projection as before. It checks the
  read bound on the published bytes and proposes
  `(:persist-checkpoint E 0 G RCK)`.
- host/native/bp-service.lisp `fnn-bps-publish-generation` writes the
  effect's RCK; it no longer recomputes the checkpoint from the recovery event.

**Keystone** `fn-bpnr-recover-from-checkpoint-equals-full-recover` (restated;
books/bp-node-rotation.lisp). The hypotheses:
- plan0 is not damaged;
- rows0 replay `:ready` from plan0;
- ck1 is the projection of that replay and is a checkpoint;
- E is field 1 of `fn-bpnr-recover-auto-event` over plan0 and rows0;
- the file of `(fn-bpnr-rotation-checkpoint ck1 E)` exists;
- the later rows start after `(E . 0)`.

The conclusion:
- with no later rows, the reopen event is
  `(:recover-fnbs E+1 br sr (update-nth 3 (E . 0) replay) 0)`, so no
  operation after the reopen is the rotation's;
- with later rows, it equals the event over plan0 and `rows0 ++ suffix` with
  field 5 set to `(len suffix)`.

Host line: `fnn-bps-open` passes `(fnn-bps-plan service)` to
`fn-bpnr-recover-auto-event`.

The old statement over the projection is kept as the lemma
`fn-bpnr-recover-from-projection-equals-full-recover`. Two other theorems are
restated for the published file:
- `fn-bpnr-rotation-crash-recovers-old-or-new`: under the new file, recovery
  is the reopen event above.
- `fn-bpnp-rotate-step-proposes-only-own-projection`: the effect's fifth
  field is `fn-bpnr-rotation-checkpoint` of the own projection.

Keystone proof: every step is in minimal theory. The keystone itself proves in
0.03 s; the book's prover time is under 1 s.

**Teeth** (tests/acl2/bp-node-counterexamples-tests, N16 section):
- The trace effect carries RCK. The reopen is epoch 3 with frontier (2 . 0)
  and count 0. New work is at (3 0). A stale `(:persist-result 2 0 :durable)`
  is inert.
- The finding's witness: publishing the projection reopens at 2, and the stale
  completion completes new work. There is a must-fail for this.
- The keystone witness over real kind-5 rows (the publisher's name and frame):
  - row A at (1 . 0);
  - row B at (3 . 0), where equality holds;
  - one must-fail per hypothesis:
    - row B2 at the rotation's own id (2 . 0), which full recovery takes and
      the checkpoint refuses;
    - a damaged plan0;
    - absent octets (budget 2);
    - an unready prefix.
- The `fn-bpnr-checkpointp ck1` hypothesis has no tooth, and this is stated in
  the book. The only field of ck1 that the published file does not already
  constrain is the frontier. The frontier is a pair of decoded u64s for every
  row the codec decodes, so no reachable replay separates the hypothesis. The
  chaining lemma needs it.

## 3. Outbound fragmentation (PKT-061), PRF-114

`books/bp-fragment-send.lisp` (new, prefix `fn-bpfs-`) replaces spike/bp's
`:program` planner with a logic-mode, guard-verified `fn-bpfs-plan WIRE MRU`.
It answers one of:
- `(:whole)`;
- `(:fragments W1 ... Wn)`;
- `(:refused :malformed | :no-fragment | :mru-too-small)`. `:mru-too-small`
  covers both an MRU below the overhead and an empty payload.

How the fragments are built:
- The parent's primary block goes through `fn-bpf-fragment-block`, at the
  parent's own ADU offset plus the local offset, with the parent's total, for
  a fragment parent.
- Every extension block of the parent is carried.
- The payload block carries one contiguous extent of CHUNK octets, where
  CHUNK = MRU − 8 − overhead. The overhead is the encoded length of the empty
  fragment at the largest local offset.

**Host line.** `host/native/bp-service.lisp` `fnn-bps-send-effect` calls
`(fnn-core 'fn-bpfs-plan wire mtu)` in the session's `:on-ready`. `mtu` is
`fn-tcl-negotiated-transfer-mtu` of the negotiated session. The first
transfer rides that session, and each further fragment gets its own session
to the same hop. Each further transfer is read through
`fn-bpfs-fragment-outcome`, so a connect that sent nothing after the first
fragment is `:uncertain`, never `:failed`. The spike made this decision in
the host; here ACL2 makes it. Both native builds include the book.

**Keystones** (subject `fn-bpfs-plan`; hypothesis: the answer is
`:fragments`):

- `fn-bpfs-plan-fragments-fit-mru`: every Wi is at most MRU octets. It rests
  on two facts:
  - `fn-bpfs-fragment-primary-length-shift`: moving the offset changes the
    primary's encoding by exactly the change of its CBOR head;
  - the payload block's exact length (`fn-bpfs-payload-block-length`).

  Together they give `fn-bpfs-fragment-length-bound` (≤ overhead + 8 +
  len d).
- `fn-bpfs-plan-fragments-reassemble-exactly`: the decoded views of the Wi
  (`fn-bpfs-views`, over `fn-bpb-decode`) draw the parent's payload exactly on
  `fn-bpf-canvas` at the parent's ADU offset (`fn-bpfs-base`), and all carry
  the parent's total. **No data cap**: nothing bounds the payload length or
  the number of fragments (`fn-bpfs-canvas-of-extents`, `fn-bpfs-views-of-cut`).
- `fn-bpfs-plan-fragments-restore-parent`: every Wi decodes, and each
  decoded fragment:
  - has the parent's extension blocks;
  - has the fragment flag;
  - has the parent's ADU key;
  - for a whole parent, has a primary that unfragments to the parent's
    (`fn-bpf-whole-fragment-unfragments-to-parent`).
- `fn-bpfs-plan-fragments-reassemble-within-caps`: the receiver's executable
  `fn-bpf-reassemble` answers `(:ok payload)` on the views when all of these
  hold:
  - the parent is whole;
  - its payload is at most `*fn-bpf-max-length*` (65538);
  - there are at most `*fn-bpf-max-fragments*` (64) fragments.

`fn-bpfs-fragment-outcome` has two theorems, both named `-by-definition`:
after the first fragment it is never `:failed`, and it is accepted iff the
transfer is.

**Teeth** (`tests/acl2/bp-fragment-send-tests.lisp`, real encoded bundles):

- Witnesses:
  - a 1000-octet ADU at MRU 300 gives more than one fragment, all ≤ 300, and
    the largest is ≥ 290;
  - the canvas equals the payload;
  - `fn-bpf-reassemble` gives `(:ok payload)`;
  - the last fragment unfragments to the parent;
  - a fragment parent (ADU offset 500 of 3000): its first view is at 500,
    every view has total 3000, and the canvas at 500 is its payload;
  - a 70000-octet ADU at MRU 40000 gives two consecutive views whose bytes
    are the payload.
- Refusals:
  - no-fragment;
  - MRU 40;
  - an empty payload;
  - a malformed wire.
- A missing fragment is `:missing`.
- One must-fail per hypothesis. Each inner form was evaluated to NIL or to
  an `:invalid` answer, not to an error.
  - The +8 head reserve: cutting at MRU − overhead makes the second
    fragment 402 > 400.
  - `:fragments`, for the exactness keystone: a `(:whole)` answer's canvas
    is all gaps.
  - `:fragments`, for restoration: `(:whole)` sends the parent itself,
    which has no fragment flag.
  - `:fragments`, for within-caps: `(:invalid :bounds)`.
  - The whole-parent hypothesis: the fragment parent's views carry total
    3000.
  - The fragment cap: a 10-octet chunk gives 100 fragments.
  - The length cap: the 70000-octet ADU.
- `fn-bpfs-fragment-outcome`: `1 :failed` → `:failed`, `2 :failed` →
  `:uncertain`, with a must-fail.

### What remains (named, not done)

- **Held-transit fragmentation.** `fn-bpnp-forward-scan` still records an
  `:mru` wait (`fn-bpnp-forward-mru-waitp`) for a held row whose image
  exceeds the session MRU. The row is not cut, and fixing this needs a
  machine change in the `:session` arm. Only the base-job send path
  (`fnn-bps-send-effect`) fragments.
- **The receiver's caps are not lifted.** `fn-bpf-inputsp` and the held-row
  recognizer `fn-bpf-fragmentp` keep `*fn-bpf-max-length*` 65538 and
  `*fn-bpf-max-fragments*` 64. `fn-bpf-canvas`'s guard is the capped
  recognizer, so the uncapped exactness theorem is a logical statement: the
  executable reassembler runs only within the caps (the 70000-octet witness
  reads the views directly). Lifting them changes `fn-bpf-fragmentp`, which
  the progress, rotation and selection books disable by name, and puts the
  whole BP closure under recertification. The bound would then become a
  store-profile value (D27).
- **Other data caps on the sender's path, unchanged:**
  - the bundle codec's `*fn-bpb-max-data*` (1 MiB);
  - its `*fn-bpb-max-input*` (1 MiB): `fn-bpfs-plan` decodes WIRE with
    `fn-bpb-decode`;
  - `bp-service run` reads the ADU bounded by the transfer MRU argument.
- **No per-fragment custody.** The job's one attempt covers all its
  fragments. A partial send is `:uncertain`, so the job is retried whole.
- **No D27 concrete twin.** `fn-bpfs-plan` runs on octet lists; the host
  passes `wire` as a list.
- `fn-bpfs-views`, `-restoresp`, `-parent` and `-chunk` are specification
  vocabulary and are not guard-verified; the host does not call them.
- PKT-062 (production DTN labs) and PKT-064 (the waits slot): not started.
  The dtn7 lab's `--b-transfer-mru` option (spike/bp) is not ported.
