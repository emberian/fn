# node-disk: where the public node's store goes, by fsync cost per mount (2026-09-26)

Lane `lane/node-disk` (Opus 5.5), a measurement lane with no books and no host
change, from dev 8dc094f9. D36 says the public node's store goes on "the fastest
durable disk hbox has, chosen by a lane that measures fsync cost per mount".
Ids: PKT-579 (the decision packet), PKT-580 (what remains). Raw outputs, the
probe and the round script are in node-disk-2026-09-26/.

## The recommendation (the paragraph the public-node brief quotes)

Put the fresh public node's store in a new directory on hbox's NVMe root
filesystem (ext4 on /dev/nvme0n1p4, Samsung MZVLB512HAJQ, mounted at `/` at
boot; 44 GB free, 91 percent used; for example `/srv/fn-public/`), not on
`tank`. A 4 KiB O_APPEND write followed by fsync costs 3.1 ms median there
(5.9 / 3.4 ms p95, two rounds ten minutes apart, load 14.1 and 7.8), against
66.6 / 33.2 ms median (116.7 / 66.6 ms p95) on `tank`. At 7.2 fsyncs per commit
(commit-regression) the durable publication is about 22 ms on the NVMe against
240 to 480 ms on `tank`; with about 10 ms of owner CPU per POST at N = 10,000
(performance-2026-09-26 row 5) one connection should sustain about 30 POSTs a
second on the NVMe (about 35 at 6 fsyncs), against the 1.4 to 2.1 measured on
`tank` (service-envelope). This is an estimate from the probe, not a measured
POST: PKT-580 (1) measures it. The one faster durable mount, the Samsung 860
EVO SATA SSD's ZFS pool `rpool` (0.79 ms median, about 64 POSTs a second
estimated, the tmpfs level), is rejected for now because it is the pre-harden
system's pool imported by hand (`altroot=/othersys`, `cachefile=none`,
aes-256-gcm with its key in a LUKS zvol): it does not come back after a reboot
by itself. It becomes the better choice (ZFS snapshots, 4x lower barrier cost)
only if ember makes it boot-imported (PKT-579). What would change the `tank`
figure: an SLOG device on `tank` (there is none, and the NVMe has no free
partition for one), or a less full pool (92 percent, 209 GB free, and its NVMe
special vdev is 100 percent full).

## ZFS facts (inventory.txt)

- **No dataset has sync=disabled.** Every dataset of `tank`, `rpool` and
  `bpool` is `sync=standard`, `logbias=latency` (rpool sets `sync=standard`
  locally; the rest inherit the default). No figure above is faked durability.
- **No SLOG on any pool** (`zpool status`: no `logs` section), confirming
  commit-regression. No L2ARC cache device.
- `tank`: 2.73 T mirror, 2.53 T allocated, 209 G free, 92 percent, frag 49
  percent; datasets `tank` (/tank, holds /tank/fn including the live node's
  /tank/fn/node; there is no separate /tank/fn dataset), `tank/dregg-build`
  (1.52 T, special_small_blocks 32K), `tank/backups/...` (517 G, unmounted).
  Its special vdev nvme0n1p3 is 13.6 G and **100 percent full** (13.5 G
  allocated, 8 K free): new metadata now lands on the HDDs.
- `rpool`: the pre-harden Ubuntu root on the SATA SSD, imported with
  `altroot=/othersys`, `cachefile=none` (not in /etc/zfs/zpool.cache, so not
  imported at boot), encryption aes-256-gcm, keylocation
  file:///run/keystore/rpool/system.key (a LUKS zvol). `bpool` (its boot
  pool) is on the same SSD.
- The NVMe is fully partitioned (p1 EFI, p3 tank's special vdev, p4 the ext4
  root): no free space for an SLOG partition without repartitioning.

## PKT-579 DECISION (for ember to confirm): the public node's store location

- Trace: D36 (the store on the fastest durable disk, chosen by measurement);
  PKT-442 (d) and PKT-477 (2) (the storage tier); service-envelope (1.4 to 2.1
  POSTs a second on `tank`); commit-regression (7.2 fsyncs per commit); this
  record's table.
- Constraints: durable (sync honoured, no sync=disabled); comes back after a
  reboot without an operator; outside /tank/fn/node (D34: a fresh deploy, no
  migration); room for the store (41 MB at N = 10,000 x 2 KiB; a 32 KiB-article
  history is larger); no root action by a lane.
- Default (this lane's recommendation): a new directory on the NVMe ext4 root
  (e.g. `/srv/fn-public/`). 3.1 ms per fsync, about 22 ms of barriers per
  commit, about 30 POSTs a second estimated on one connection (the v1 target is
  10). Backups by `store export` to `tank` (ext4 has no snapshots).
- Rejected: (a) `tank` as it is: 33 to 67 ms per fsync median, 240 to 480 ms of
  barriers per commit, about 2 POSTs a second measured, p95 over the 250 ms
  target, and a 47 s stall observed. (b) `rpool` (the SATA SSD): 0.79 ms, the
  fastest durable mount (about 64 POSTs a second estimated) with ZFS snapshots,
  but it is not imported at boot and its key is in a hand-opened keystore; the
  cost of choosing it is a root change by ember (`zpool set
  cachefile=/etc/zfs/zpool.cache rpool`, a key-load unit, a dedicated dataset
  `rpool/fn-public` with its own mountpoint outside /othersys) and a
  reboot rehearsal; with that done it is the better mount and this default
  flips. (c) tmpfs: not durable. (d) An SLOG on `tank`: needs a new device (the
  NVMe has no free partition); it would lower every `tank` barrier and needs
  no code, but not today.
- Affected: the public-node brief's store path and its operator config; no
  store format, no proof, no caller changes. What continues without the
  decision: the public-node lane can build on the NVMe default, which needs
  no root action.

## PKT-580 what remains

1. A measured POST (tools/service_envelope.py's POST rows, 1 and 8 connections)
   on the chosen mount, so the 30-a-second estimate becomes a figure; the
   probe times file append+fsync only, while a commit also renames, links and
   unlinks, and on ext4 a directory fsync is a journal commit of about the same
   cost (counted inside the 7.2).
2. A quiet-box repetition of the `tank` row (both rounds ran beside
   qualification 69046a76's units).
3. `tank`'s special vdev is 100 percent full; the pool is at 92 percent. Not a
   node question, but every `tank` workload (gates, certcache, the live node
   on /tank/fn/node) pays it: for ember.
4. If rpool is made boot-imported (PKT-579 (b)), re-run this probe on the new
   dataset before moving the store.
