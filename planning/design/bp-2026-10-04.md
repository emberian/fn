## 0. Scope and the boundary with the NNTP catch-up lane

This packet is the **BP** path: `bp-node serve`'s retained session loop
(`host/native/bp-session.lisp` `fnn-bp-session-loop`), its eight service
classes (`books/bp-session-scheduler.lisp` `fn-bpsched-service`), the
contact driver for base jobs (`books/bp-node-job-cursor.lisp`
`fn-bpnjc-contact-next`), the forward plan for held transit
(`books/bp-node-forward-plan.lisp` `fn-bpnp-forward-plan` +

(`host/native/bp-service.lisp:1132` `fnn-bps-receive`), the receipt path
(`books/bp-release-authority.lisp`, `books/bp-release.lisp`), the ION
workflow (`host/native/workflow.lisp` `fnn-workflow-ion-submit`,
`books/bp-ion-lifetime.lisp`), and the signed R/Q scenario (SCN-1125).

It is **not** NNTP peer pull / XFNCATCHUP. `books/peer-round-driver.lisp`
(PRF-1260, SCN-1090), `host/native/catchup-spool.lisp` (`fnn-csp-worker-*`,
no caller), `host/native/pull-service.lisp`, the FNPL/FNCU cursors and the
"ACL2 round deadline for pull/catch-up (S054/S067)" of the 10-03 handoff
belong to the CATCHUP lane (`a70763bae49abee31`). Where the swarmplan row
says "BP / peer catch-up controller", the BP half is the **contact drain**:
after a disconnection, one contact must drain a peer's backlog of queued
base jobs and forward-pending held transit, one bounded step at a time.
That drain is what §2 calls a round. The handoff's third interim bound, "a
max passive session for bp-node (S025)", is BP's and is decided in §2.6.

## 1. The rule today (coordinates, verified in source)

### 1.1 The loop (`host/native/bp-session.lisp:160-215`)

One writer. Each iteration: (i) one funded listener attempt
(`fn-bpsched-listener-index`, `fn-bpsg-acquire-turn`: one candidate slot,
yield if occupied, PRF-1309); (ii) one retained slot's turn
(`fn-bpsched-next-slot`: round-robin over the bank's slots); (iii) one
service class (`fn-bpsched-service`: `:fragment :dispatch :expiry :outbox
:forward :receipt :report :rotation`, `fn-bpsched-next` mod 8); then the
work credit (`fn-bpsched-work-credit`) decides whether to sleep 1 ms. This
loop is what SCN-1110 observes; it landed on dev 10-03 (`de5594be5` and
ancestors) and has **never run on an image** (the DTN image did not load
`bp-session.lisp` until `83b905e89` on `integrate/20261004`).

| class | host arm (`host/native/bp-node.lisp:1150-1212`) | ACL2 subject | work per turn today |
|---|---|---|---|
| `:fragment` | `fnn-bps-fragment-progress` | `fn-bpfj-step job 4096` (`fn-bpfj-step-is-bounded`) | one 4096-position sweep quantum, carried across turns (S026 carry landed, `bp-service.lisp:1071`) |
| `:dispatch` | `fnn-bpnode-dispatch-one` | `fn-bpnp-step :progress` | one held row |
| `:expiry` | `fnn-bpnode-expire-turn` | `fn-bpnf-step :expire-held` | O(held) scan in one call |
| `:outbox` | `fn-bpah-outbox-view-after` | `fn-bpah-outbox-view-after st after` | one outbox row, cursor `outbox-after` carried |
| `:forward` | `fn-bpsched-forward-entry (fn-bpnp-forward-plan held table) busy` | both | **O(held × table)**: every forward-pending row's `fn-bprt-outbound-choice` (contactable + next-hop pattern match + hop-route over the table), plus `(reverse held)`, every turn |
| `:receipt` | `fnn-bpnode-receipt-turn` | `fn-bpnjc-contact-next st peer routing offered cursor` | O(c) `fn-bpnjc-drop` skip + scan to the first ready job; cursor `(f c held)` carried in `fnn-bp-receipt-cursor` |

### 1.3 What is unbounded or whole-frame (the findings this packet answers)

F1. `:forward` does O(held × table) route decisions per turn and reverses
the held list each turn; over a drain of n rows that is O(n² × table).
Same shape as the quadratic PRF-1260 names on the NNTP side.

F2. The receive callback is whole-frame: `fnn-tcl-deliver-transfer`
(`host/native/tcpcl.lisp:169`) hands the *complete* materialized transfer to
`fnn-bps-receive`, whose one ACL2 call
`fn-bpnpf-admitted-receive-event profile admission config wire observation`
decodes every block, checks every CRC and decides admission over the whole
wire; `fnn-bps-persist-kind-five` then writes it. The prefix probe
(65,540 → 4 visits, `planning/evidence/bp-prefix-probe-source-2026-10-03.json`)
bounded one hot spot inside the decoder, not the step. The transfer's final
XFER_ACK is held until this returns (S026's residue; S068).

F3. An incoming session that completed SESS_INIT has no bound: a peer that
keeps sending KEEPALIVE holds its slot forever. With `inbound` slots
(profile default 2) exhausted, `fn-bpsg-acquire-turn` yields on every
listener attempt. The loop itself is not starved (SCN-1110's claim), the
*admission* is. The RFC idle timeout (§1.2) expires a silent peer; it
cannot expire a keepalive peer, by design.

F4. `fn-bpnjc-drop c jobs` is O(c) per `:receipt` turn (the cursor is a
count into a list). Bounded decisions, unbounded pointer-chasing.

F5. `fnn-bpnode-forward-contact` (`bp-contact tick`, the one-shot verb
without a listener) runs the whole plan in one call. It is a drain verb;
left as is.

F6. ION X10A/X10B are fixed at source on dev (`books/bp-ion-lifetime.lisp`
`fn-bpit-helper-seconds`, green at digest `04eaab7b`, manifest
`certify-20261003T095031Z-1342869`; `host/native/workflow.lisp:409`
refuses *before* `fnn-workflow-ion-private-directory` and before any
`fnn-app-publish`), and their natives
(`tests/test_native_ion_workflow.py::test_observation_binds_explicit_route_and_survives_reopen`,
`::test_unrepresentable_lifetime_refuses_before_attempt_or_helper`) have
not run on an image of current source.

F7. SCN-1110 (`tests/test_bp_node_native.py:249`) and the signed R/Q
scenario SCN-1125 (`tests/test_native_consumer_exchange_two_nodes.py:576`,
prepared in `744b3b51e`) have never executed.

## 2. Decisions

### 2.1 What a BP round is

A **round** is one bounded drain of one backlog against one peer, made of
scheduling steps the loop interleaves with every other slot and class. Three
rounds exist, each with its own ACL2 cursor:

| round | backlog | begins | one step | ends | carried across steps | carried across rounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **contact round** (base jobs: request carriers, receipts, reports) | `fn-bpn-machine-state-jobs` with status `:queued` for PEER | the `:receipt` class finds the receipt cursor not `done` | `fn-bpnjc-contact-next st peer routing offered cursor` → `(:offer …)` opens one outgoing session with its own `outbound-ms` deadline | `(:close)` or `(:held KEY DECISION)`; a lost session requeues (`:requeued`, §4.3.2 of `specs/bp-node-machine.md`) | `(f c held)` + OFFERED, in `fnn-bp-receipt-cursor` | nothing in memory: job status is durable (FNBS kind 7/8/9); OFFERED is per contact by design (`fn-bpnj-contact-offers-each-job-at-most-once`) |
| **forward round** (held transit) | `fn-bpnf-held-list` rows with status `(:forward-pending)` | the `:forward` class finds no cursor | **new**: `fn-bpfc-turn cursor held table busy quantum` → `(:entry E cursor')` opens one outgoing session; `(:yield cursor')`; `(:drained)` | `:drained` (a full sweep found no startable peer) | the cursor (position + peers seen) | nothing: row status is durable (kind 6/8); a new sweep starts at the oldest row |
| **receive round** (one inbound transfer) | the materialized octets of one transfer | XFER_SEGMENT with the END flag materialized (`fn-tcim-turn`, bounded) | today: one whole-frame call (F2); §2.4: one `fn-bprx-step` quantum | kind-5 durable → XFER_ACK released; or refused/uncertain by name | the decode job (§2.4) | nothing: custody is the kind-5 record |

What is *not* a round: a session. A session is the transport under one
step of a contact or forward round (or the carrier of a receive round); its
deadline is `fn-bpsched-deadline` (outgoing) or the §2.6 bound (incoming).

### 2.2 Deadlines: each one an ACL2 decision, each one named by what it bounds

- The contact and forward rounds have **no wall-clock deadline of their
  own**, and this packet declines to add one. A sweep deadline that resets
  the cursor would starve the tail of a long backlog (the opposite of what
  S054/S067 want); one that keeps the cursor is the same as a yield. What
  bounds a round is (a) the per-session deadline `outbound-ms` already in
  force, and (b) the per-step work quantum (§2.3). A round over n rows takes
  at most ⌈n / Q⌉ `:forward` turns of scanning plus one session per started
  entry; nothing in it waits on a peer longer than `outbound-ms`.
- The receive round's deadline is the sender's (the peer's transfer timer);
  what fn bounds is its own work per step (§2.4).
- The incoming session gets the §2.6 bound.
- `outbound-ms` stays a profile row (`bp-session-profile`, `fn-bpsp-read`,
  default 30,000). It is a transport attempt policy, not a data limit
  (D27): a 10 MiB fragment plan is many sessions (`fnn-bp-session-rearm`),
  each under its own deadline, and F7 (SCN-077, MET 09-28) is unaffected.

### 2.3 Fairness is a bound, not a promise (until the actor rebuild)

What the loop *does* bound, and the keystones that say so:

- every retained slot gets a turn within `count − 2` iterations
  (`fn-bpsched-next-slot` is a rotation over `[2, count)`);
- every service class gets a turn within 8 iterations (`fn-bpsched-next`);
- a forward sweep examines every forward-pending row within ⌈n / Q⌉
  `:forward` turns (K3 below) and starts at most one session per turn;
- within one sweep, after a peer's session starts that peer is `busy` and
  the sweep continues *past* it (K2/K3), so two peers with pending rows get
  sessions on consecutive `:forward` turns while slots allow.

What it does **not** promise: that a peer's backlog drains (PRF-018's
A-FAIRNESS/A-PEER/A-HOST hypotheses, still planned), that admission is fair
across inbound peers (F3), or anything about wall time. The spec rows for
these bounds say "fairness is not bounded" in those words (handoff item 4).

### 2.4 Whole-frame decode/CRC/publication (F2) — the bound per scheduling step

Decision: the receive round becomes a resumable job carried in the service,
exactly as the fragment job is (`fnn-bps-fragment-pending`), stepped by one
quantum per `:dispatch`-class turn (`fn-bpsched-service` gains no class; the
job is stepped before `fnn-bpnode-dispatch-one` on the same turn), with the
final XFER_ACK held until the kind-5 record is durable — that is already the
rule (`fnn-tcl-deliver-transfer`'s counted path; `fn-tcl-final-count-ready-p`),
only the *work* between materialization and the ACK moves out of the
callback. D27: exhausting a quantum yields or resumes; it never truncates.

Shape (slice 2, not this packet's first slice):

```
(fn-bprx-start profile admission config wire observation)   ; → job
(fn-bprx-step job quantum)                                   ; → job'
(fn-bprx-finishedp job)                                      ; → t | nil
(fn-bprx-answer job)   ; = (fn-bpnpf-admitted-receive-event profile admission config wire observation)
```

Keystones (Props; satisfiable, teeth, premise-inhabitation stated):

- K4a `fn-bprx-step-is-bounded`: the number of octets the step reads
  (`fn-bprx-cursor job'` − `fn-bprx-cursor job`) ≤ quantum, and the step
  conses O(quantum). Satisfiable: a job over a 5-octet wire with quantum 4
  advances by 4 then by 1. Teeth: that witness, and a `must-fail-checked`
  that an advance of quantum + 1 is not a step. Premises inhabited: any
  `fn-bprx-jobp` and `posp` quantum.
- K4b `fn-bprx-run-is-the-whole-answer`: iterating `fn-bprx-step` to
  `fn-bprx-finishedp` gives `fn-bpnpf-admitted-receive-event` of the same
  inputs — the executable cursor refines the logical whole-frame decision
  (the "boundary theorem, not a twin" rule of AGENTS.md). Satisfiable: the
  SCN-077 fixture bundle. Teeth: the four-node fixture's accepted bundle
  and one with a corrupted CRC (refused by name in both). Premises: the
  profile/config/wire shapes `fn-bpnpf-admitted-receive-event` already
  guards.
- K4c (host, not ACL2): the callback returns `:source-yield` with the job
  and the ACK stays held until `fn-bprx-finishedp`; the witness is
  `tests/test_bp_node_native.py::test_control_input_advances_while_received_source_is_held`
  extended to a decode that spans turns.

Why not do it inside `fn-bpc-dec`'s prefix probes: they bound a *lookup*,
the CRC and the block walk are still a traversal of the whole wire. The
cursor must be at the level of the receive event, where the ACK is held.

### 2.5 Receipt → release: which receipt releases what

`docs/architecture.md:34-39` keeps a transport acknowledgement, a retention
receipt and an application outcome distinct. The tree has two retention
ledgers and this packet names them so no slice confuses them:

| retained thing | ledger | released by | never released by |
|---|---|---|---|
| the retained **wire** of an outgoing attempt (spool file, fragment plan, held row's forwarding image) | FNBS job/row status (`:attempting` → `:forwarded`/`:requeued`, kind 8/9; held row kind 6/8) | the transport outcome of *that attempt*: XFER_ACK of the whole transfer → `:forward-result KEY :accepted` → `:forwarded`, which no record undoes (`fn-bpn-apply-record-keeps-forwarded`); a refused/lost transfer → `:requeued` | a BPA submit result, a bundle status report (RFC 9171 §6.1; `fn-bpn-report-observe-next` logs it), a keepalive, a session close |
| the **application obligation**: the article/archive pin and the FNWF work (`fn-bprl-pins`, `fn-bprl-work-pinnedp`) | FNRJ/FNWF (`books/bp-release.lisp`) | exactly one event: an application receipt whose issuer is authorized at the delivering neighbour and that names the exact held obligation (`fn-bpah-receipt-names-obligationp`, `fn-bprl-release-okp`, D23), published as `fn-bpah-receipt-release-record` through `fn-owner-bp-receipt-release-record` | every row of the first ledger: `:forwarded` is not release; an ION observation (`observed-v1|…`, X10A's binding) is a transport fact (`test_deletion_report_intent_recovers_and_observation_does_not_release`); an expired/deleted attempt leaves the obligation intact (`specs/bp-path.md` "Bundle attempts have finite transport lifetimes") |

Decision: no new mechanism. What is missing is the *composed* statement,
owed as a Prop with teeth before any receipt-path change:

- K5 `fn-bprl-pins-unchanged-by-transport-events`: for every event the
  node machine takes that is not a `:receipt-release` (`:cl-receive`,
  `:forward-result`, `:session`, `:expire-held`, `:persist-result`, status
  report observation), `fn-bprl-pins` of the workflow image is unchanged.
  Satisfiable: the SCN-1110 fixture's accepted request leaves the sender's
  pin (the test asserts `receiver_articles() == 1` and the pin survives,
  `test_disconnected_delivery_restarts_and_releases_only_matching_obligation`).
  Teeth: a release record *does* change the pins (the positive witness
  asserts the full conclusion on each event kind; the removal witness shows
  the release event is the one that moves it). Premises: `fn-bp-statep`
  and the event recognizer `fn-bpnj-host-eventp`. This is proof-owed
  (filed with slice 4, below), and the spec row it lands under is
  `specs/bp-path.md` "Release decision".

### 2.6 S025: the incoming session bound (the handoff's interim bound (c))

Decision: an incoming session that has made **no transfer progress** for
`passive-ms` is terminated by fn with SESS_TERM reason 3 (Busy, RFC 9174
§6.1 Table 9: "the entity is too busy to handle the current session") when
the bank's incoming class is at capacity, and left alone otherwise.

- Progress = any XFER_SEGMENT received or XFER_ACK sent on that session
  (`fn-tcl-session-last-rx` restricted to transfer messages; a KEEPALIVE is
  not progress). A session mid-transfer is never expired by this bound
  (RFC 9174 §6.1.1: termination waits for in-progress transfers).
- The bound fires only under contention: the listener's last acquire of
  class `:incoming` answered `:bp-session-yield`. On a quiet node a silent
  but live peer keeps its slot. This is why it is Busy, not Idle timeout
  (the RFC idle timeout is 2 × keepalive of *silence*, which a keepalive
  peer never triggers), and not Resource Exhaustion (nothing was exhausted;
  a slot was wanted).
- `passive-ms` is a profile row of `bp-session-profile` (default 120,000),
  read by `fn-bpsp-read`, next to `outbound-ms`; its spec row says the
  bound is admission policy and that fairness across inbound peers is not
  bounded.
- ACL2: `fn-tcrt-passive-deadline progress-at passive-ms`,
  `fn-tcrt-passive-timeout-p phase now deadline in-transfer contended`
  in `books/tcpcl-retained-turn.lisp`, same shape as the init deadline, with
  the theorem `fn-tcrt-passive-timeout-excludes-transfer-and-quiet`
  (`-by-definition`). The host arm is the `:local` action of
  `fnn-tcl-turn`; the termination goes through the session machine's
  SESS_TERM handshake (`fn-tcl-make-sess-term 0 3`), so the grant settles
  by the existing receipts.
- Native: SCN-1110's keepalive peer, with `inbound 1` and a third peer
  waiting, loses its session after `passive-ms` and the third peer is
  admitted; a peer mid-transfer at the deadline completes. Slice 3.

Checked while reading (a first draft of this packet had it wrong): the RFC
idle timeout *is* driven natively (`fn-tcl-host-tick` on each `:read` turn,
§1.2), so a silent established peer already expires per §6.2 and slice 3
adds nothing there. What no native has observed is that expiry on the
retained loop: SCN-1126/1127 cover only the pre-session timers. Slice 3's
native adds the silent-established case beside the keepalive case.

### 2.7 Multi-peer routing as a bounded machine — the first slice

The forward round's cursor (§2.1) replaces the per-turn whole plan in the
`:forward` arm. `fn-bpnp-forward-plan` stays as the **logical model** (its
keystones `fn-bpnp-forward-plan-has-one-session-per-peer`,
`fn-bpnp-forward-plan-offers-a-row-on-one-session`,
`fn-bpnp-progress-dispatch-names-the-routed-hop` are cited by PKT-261 and
`bp-contact tick` still drains by it); the cursor is the executable the
loop calls, connected by a boundary theorem (K2). Not a twin: one subject,
one refinement.

Book `books/bp-forward-cursor.lisp` (prefix `fn-bpfc-`):

```
(fn-bpfc-cursor pos seen)            ; (:bpfc POS SEEN): POS rows of the oldest-first order examined, SEEN the peers already planned or found busy
(fn-bpfc-turn cursor held table busy quantum)
  ; → (:entry (PEER HOP EID PORT) cursor')   one startable peer found
  ;   (:yield cursor')                        quantum exhausted, more rows remain
  ;   (:drained)                              the sweep reached the end
(fn-bpfc-run cursor held table busy quantum fuel)   ; iterate turns to the first non-yield (fuel = (len held))
```

A row is *startable* when it is `(:forward-pending)`, not deleted (slot 14
nil), its next hop (slot 11) is an EID not in SEEN and not in BUSY, and
`fn-bprt-outbound-choice` of its destination over TABLE answers `:hop` —
the plan's own condition (`fn-bpnp-forward-plan-rows`) plus BUSY, which
`fn-bpsched-forward-entry` applies today.

Keystones (PRF-1311), each a Prop before proof, with its ATLAS fields:

- **K1 `fn-bpfc-turn-advances-at-most-quantum`**: for `posp` QUANTUM and
  a cursor of `natp` POS, the turn's new POS is ≤ POS + QUANTUM, and the
  turn calls `fn-bprt-outbound-choice` at most QUANTUM times (stated as:
  the rows examined are `(take k (nthcdr pos ordered))` with k ≤ QUANTUM —
  the decision count is the examined-row count by construction). Satisfiable:
  held = 5 rows, Q = 2, from POS 0 → `:yield` at POS 2. Teeth: that
  witness; with Q = 5 the same held drains; `must-fail-checked` on an
  advance of Q + 1. Premise-inhabitation: every natp POS and posp Q.
- **K2 `fn-bpfc-run-is-the-plan-choice`**: from the initial cursor
  `(:bpfc 0 nil)`, `fn-bpfc-run` with fuel `(len held)` answers `(:entry E …)`
  iff `(fn-bpsched-forward-entry (fn-bpnp-forward-plan held table) busy)`
  is E, and `:drained` iff that is nil. Satisfiable: the forward-plan test
  fixture (`tests/acl2/bp-node-forward-plan-tests.lisp` `*fp-held*`,
  `*fp-table*`: two rows to two hops) gives E = the relay entry with
  busy = nil and the relay-b entry with busy = (relay). Teeth: both; and
  with both peers busy, `:drained`. Premise-inhabitation: `true-listp held`
  (the plan's own guard); any table and busy list.
- **K3 `fn-bpfc-turn-after-a-yield-is-the-larger-turn`** (as admitted; the
  draft name was `fn-bpfc-resume-is-the-suffix`): resuming a yield of
  quantum q1 with quantum q2 is the turn of quantum q1 + q2 from the
  original cursor. The draft statement, kept for its witnesses: a turn from `(:bpfc k seen)` over
  HELD examines exactly what a turn from `(:bpfc 0 seen)` over the
  oldest-first order with its first k rows dropped examines, with positions
  shifted by k — no row skipped, none revisited. Satisfiable: the 5-row
  fixture resumed at 2. Teeth: positive at k = 2; `must-fail-checked` that
  resuming at k and at k + 1 give the same examined rows. Premise:
  k ≤ (len held).
- **K0 `fn-bpfc-turn-is-executable`** (guards): guard-verified at `t`,
  as `fn-bpnp-forward-plan` is.

Covered scope: the turn still *skips* to POS by pointer-chasing
(`nthcdr` over the oldest-first order). That order is `(reverse held)`
computed **every turn** — allocation O(n) per turn, not per sweep (the
first draft of this sentence said per sweep; `fn-bpfc-turn` as admitted
reverses on each call). The per-turn bound is therefore on route decisions
only, which is where the O(table) pattern-matching cost was, as
`fn-bpnjc-drop` bounds decisions and not pointer-chasing for the contact
round. The remaining obligation, the held set as an indexed representation
so that a position skip is O(1), is wave 4 (ST4/ST7 shape) and is named in
the spec row, not hidden.

Host (`host/native/bp-node.lisp` `:forward` arm): a `forward-cursor`
variable beside `outbox-after`; `:entry` starts the session
(`fnn-bpnode-forward-start`, unchanged) and advances the cursor, except that
a start which yields for an occupied outgoing slot keeps the old cursor so

That is the `fn-bprt-outbound-choice` count, because each examined row increments `pos` once, including rows that fail the test. `:drained` is outside the implies. The book comment says work is “bounded in route decisions and allocation.” `fn-bpfc-ordered` is `(reverse held)` on every turn; §2.7 correctly says allocation is O(n) per turn. K1 does not mention it.
- K3 is addition of quanta from a `:yield`, same `held` / `table` / `busy`. §2.7’s draft sentence — a turn from `(:bpfc k seen)` examines the suffix, “no row skipped, none revisited” — is not the `defthm` in the book. The `defthm` is `fn-bpfc-turn-after-a-yield-is-the-larger-turn`. Teeth written for the draft can go green while the cursor the host keeps is unproved.
- `fn-bpfc-run` returns at the first non-yield. Nothing states that the next `:entry` is the plan’s second element. §2.3’s “two peers … on consecutive `:forward` turns (K2/K3)” does not follow from K2 or K3.
- `seen` sticks for the sweep. A peer found busy is ineligible after their slot empties, including a later row of theirs. The old arm called `fn-bpnp-forward-plan` every turn, so a peer who became free was eligible on the next `:forward` turn. K2 freezes `busy`, so it cannot say this.
- `pos` indexes `(nthcdr pos (reverse held))` of that call. A deletion in front of `pos` makes the next turn read another row. There is no generation on `(:bpfc POS SEEN)`. How rows are inserted into `held` is not in the paste. A route-table change behind the cursor is invisible until `:drained`.

Slot-yield matches the comment when the discarded cursor is the only update: the peer is not committed to `seen`, so the same row is offered again. The push is outside that guard:

```lisp
(unless forward-awaiting (setq forward-cursor (third answer)))
(when (and once job) (push (first entry) forward-tried))
```

`busy` prepends `forward-tried` whenever `once` is set. If a `:bp-session-yield` still returns a job, the next turn treats that peer as busy and the preserved cursor does not bring them back. Whether `fnn-bpnode-forward-start` returns a job on yield is not in the paste.

`--once`: a quantum `:yield` stores `forward-cursor`, and a slot-yield sets `forward-awaiting`, both of which the pending lambda ORs in. `forward-awaiting` is cleared at the top of the `:forward` arm, before the new answer, so the flag still covers the other seven classes. `:drained` sets `forward-cursor` to nil. The loop’s exit also requires `(zerop (hash-table-count (fnn-bpsb-held bank)))`, while the scan walks `fn-bpnf-held-list`. The paste does not show these are the same set. If the hash table can be empty while that list is not, `--once` returns with forward-pending rows still queued.

The 2,000-row probe (old turn 2,000 decisions, new ≤ 64, same entry) is a witness for K2’s frozen first choice. It is silent on a second peer, a mutated `held`, and slot-yield.

### Q4. §2.4 and §2.5

The layer cut is right. F2 is `fn-bpnpf-admitted-receive-event` over the whole wire after `fn-tcim-turn` has materialized the transfer, with the final XFER_ACK held until that returns. Prefix probes bound a lookup. The cursor belongs at the event whose ACK is held. K4b is the right composed statement for the decoder: step to `fn-bprx-finishedp` equals `fn-bpnpf-admitted-receive-event` of the same inputs. K4a’s advance ≤ quantum and “conses O(quantum)” are the right cost claims. The teeth named (advance 4 then 1, must-fail of quantum+1, SCN-077 accept plus a bad CRC) pin the advance and the final answer. A step that rewalks from byte 0 passes them.

The schedule is open in four places the paste does not close. `fn-bprx-start` has no cost; a start that walks `wire` leaves F2 in the callback. One job, stepped on the `:dispatch` turn before `fnn-bpnode-dispatch-one`, does not say what a second materialized transfer does while the first is unfinished. `fnn-bps-persist-kind-five` is still one write after the answer, so the finish turn can be whole-frame. One quantum per eight iterations stretches ACK latency out to the sender’s timer. §2.2 accepts that, with no receive-quantum size and no claim about a duplicate when the sender’s timer wins.

K5 is the negative half of receipt→release. It says `fn-bprl-pins` is unchanged for events other than `:receipt-release`, and it names `:cl-receive`, `:forward-result`, `:session`, `:expire-held`, `:persist-result`, and status-report observation. §2.5’s table needs a stronger sentence: pins and `fn-bprl-work-pinnedp` change only when `fn-bpah-receipt-names-obligationp` and `fn-bprl-release-okp` hold, and the attempt ledger does move on that attempt’s `:forward-result` (`:forwarded` / `:requeued`). An ION observation is in the table and in `test_deletion_report_intent_recovers_and_observation_does_not_release`, and it is absent from K5’s list. If that list is the quantifier, K5 can be true while an observation releases a pin. The tooth “a release record does change the pins” sits outside the quantifier, so an unauthorized `:receipt-release` is a case K5 does not constrain.

## 7. DECISION

**AMENDED 2026-10-04 by bp2 after the advisory review (§6.1). Slice 1 is
DECIDED as landed. The rest is PROPOSED and narrowed. (e) is escalated to
ember.**

(a) A BP round is a bounded drain per §2.1. There is no sweep-level
wall-clock deadline (§2.2): both reviewers agree that a deadline which
keeps the cursor is only a yield. **Amended:** §4's per-round *attempt*
budget is adopted into the decision, not left conditional. After K
consecutive `outbound-ms` expiries in one contact or forward round
against one peer, that peer's round closes `(:lost)` and the existing
feed-loss backoff applies. It is a bound on attempts (D27), and it is
slice 3a, before S025. The §2.3 bounds are stated as bounds over a fixed
held list. The "consecutive `:forward` turns" sentence is a property of
the host arm with no keystone behind it.

(b) **DECIDED (landed at lane/bp2):** slice 1 is the forward-round cursor
`fn-bpfc-*`. It refines `fn-bpnp-forward-plan` + `fn-bpsched-forward-entry`
for one frozen held list, table and busy set (K2). K1–K3 are admitted and
their teeth repaired; the narrow certify is in the READY. The host
`:forward` arm calls `fn-bpfc-turn`. What it does not cover is in
`specs/bp-path.md` "The forward round": a mutating held list, stale SEEN,
the per-turn reversal, no generation on the cursor.

(c) The receive round becomes a carried job (slice 2). Before K4 is
stated, these are owed: the job's cardinality (one per service, or one
per session), the cost of `fn-bprx-start`, the kind-5 persist on the
finishing turn, and the relation between quantum and the sender's
transfer timer.

(d) Receipt→release gains no mechanism. K5 is restated as two conjuncts,
both owed:
- (i) For every node event other than an authorized `:receipt-release`,
  and for an ION observation, `fn-bprl-pins` and
  `fn-bprl-work-pinnedp` are unchanged.
- (ii) A `:receipt-release` changes them only when
  `fn-bpah-receipt-names-obligationp` and `fn-bprl-release-okp` hold, and
  it leaves the FNBS attempt ledger unchanged.

(e) **S025 is NOT decided. Escalated to ember**, as §7 of the first draft
said it would be if the reviewer disagreed. Both reviewers reject the
progress notion. Before slice 3, the bound needs:
- transfer progress in either direction;
- an intra-transfer segment-inactivity bound, so a peer that drips one
  segment per window does not hold an inbound slot indefinitely;
- a stated victim (the oldest idle incoming session);
- contention sampled when the bound fires;
- a lemma that a transfer-less SESS_TERM settles the grant.
The reason code is ember's call: Busy (3, kimi) or Resource Exhaustion
(5, grok). The lane leans 5, because the trigger the packet writes down is
"the incoming slot pool is full".

(f) ION X10A/X10B are source-done. Their natives on the published set
`45e05c7f` do not reach the subject: setUp fails with "store: canonical
Store refused retention event", because that image predates `e27ea2eb1`
and the current fixture. They are owed on the integrator's next set.

(g) SCN-1110 and SCN-1125 are runs on the integrator's batched set
(developer, dtn-developer, production). This slice rides the
pending-native batch on next.
