**The walk.** `fnn-owner-reclaim-walk` (`host/native/owner.lisp:7439-7461`) reads the captured history in
chunks of 1,024 rows through a pinned P3 root (`fnn-owner-history-root-pin` → `-at` → `-chunk-return`), and
then **accumulates every rewritten row** (`(push (first r) out)` … `nconc`) before `fnn-owner-history-root-prepare-rows`
(`history-root.lisp:216`, takes the whole `rows` list) and `fn-owner-orcp-load-catalog … rows`
(`history-root-host.lisp:224`). The generation-pinned read exists; the whole-store copy is still made twice
(the rewritten rows, then the rebuilt catalog's rows). `specs/storage.md:2152-2157` says so: "the reclaim
reservation remains the actual full-copy estimate".

**Release today.** Disk returns only by closing the last descriptor of an already-unlinked whole file: the
covered segments at the `:released` cut (`reclaim-cuts.lisp`), the previous checkpoint. A reclaimed record's
OLD handle keeps naming its file (`arena-forget` §0: `named=1:2:0`), so the file never goes quiet and its
blocks never return while the owner runs. Memory: the previous history generation goes when its last lease
returns; the previous Store/catalog columns go when the swap re-pins every connection (`fn-orcp-repin-conns`)
— which is why the swap needs `readers = 0`; resident payload octets in the paged child are never returned
(`arena-forget` §6); `fn-owner-access-cache` and `fn-bprj-bound-store` keep the old archive alive until
overwritten.

**History compaction** (STO-010 class H) is not implemented and `specs/storage.md:1157,1178,1448` says so.

**The curve.** `store reclaim --dry-run` offline measured 485 s at 100k (row 10b; `tools/scale_curve.py:444`),
"superlinear", cause not named.

## 3. The lean: one generation, one table, one release

**L1 — The generation table is the lifetime authority for everything a swap replaces.** **OPEN**: whether the history-root credit keys stay keyed by generation when the lease list goes (the lean: yes; lifetime and credit are separate ledgers). A reader of any root
off the owner's mutex pins the arena generation (it already does: publication, export, dry run, the pass,
a response plan). A reclaim swap RETIRES, at one stamp, every root it replaces, as items of the one table:

| item | today | under L1 |
|---|---|---|
| `H` (a reseated staged page) | retired at every COMPLETE (`io.lisp:6856`) | unchanged |
| `(:forget H)` (a reclaimed record's old handle) | designed (`fn-arf-retire-event`), unwired | retired in the swap quantum, applied by `fn-arf-apply-released` (forget: EXT entry + stage page freed; the file's name count falls; quiet → close → disk) |
| `(:history-root G)` (the replaced P3 generation) | a separate program-mode lease table, disposed when its leases return | retired as an item; applied by `fnn-history-root-retire-held G` when the table releases it. `fn-owner-hroot-retire-word` / the lease list are deleted (the twin); the credit side (`fn-mcr` keys) stays in the owner's ledger |
| `(:columns …)` (the replaced Store/catalog/view columns, memory) | dropped by pointer at the swap under `readers = 0` | retired as an item once connections stop being re-pinned in the swap quantum (F5's row); until then unchanged |

The pass's own walk already pins (`pin` below the stamp) and unpins after the swap; a reader that started
before the swap keeps reading the OLD roots until it unpins — snapshot = a root, a reader pins a generation
(D41 §2). The swap word's `readers = 0` stays for now, for one stated reason: connection archives are
re-pinned in the swap quantum and read `fn-cat` off the mutex (`arena-forget` §4(d), decision (d): F5 is
its own row). Under L1 nothing about the RECLAIMED handles' safety depends on `readers = 0` — only the
column swap does — so F5's row is the relaxation of one test, not a new mechanism.

**L2 — Who returns disk and memory, and when.** Returned by the `:release` walk, after the last pin at or
below the stamp ends, or after the oust (L5) at the latest:
- disk: a file's blocks, at the close of its last descriptor, which waits for the file's name count to reach
  0 (every reclaimed handle forgotten) and every issued cold read naming it (PRF-1057). No hole punch, no
  place reuse inside a live file (`arena-forget` §8.3 stands);
- memory, at the forget: the EXT entry and the staged page of each forgotten handle; at a history-root
  item's release: the generation's pages (`fn-hist$p-dispose`); at a columns item's release: the old
  columns. In the swap quantum itself: `fn-owner-access-cache` cleared, `fn-bprj-bound-store` rebound
  (c10: through the existing release operation, preserving work/receipts/txids), `fn-owner-reader-views`
  tested idle, the 14 globals of c10 §3 either cleared or shown idle by the swap word.
- NOT returned in this wave: resident payload octets inside a 256 KiB page of the paged child (needs the
  per-page live count; last slice, `arena-forget` §6).

**L3 — History compaction (H) is not scheduled.** It stays "not implemented" in `specs/storage.md`. Its
obligation is restated as a Prop below (K6) so that it is a statement and not a sentence; its precondition is
the anti-resurrection summary (D13, STO-010 row "Anti-resurrection summary": does not exist). Position: after
D41 stage 5 (P3 rows carry history) — wave 4. What reclaim already gives H: the tombstone IS the per-record
summary of a payload (D45); H summarises records, which the tombstone does not touch. With L5 the generation
bookkeeping is bounded without H, so H is not needed for bounded reclaim.

**L4 — The online snapshot producer is the walk with the identity rewrite.** **OPEN**: confirm with the harvest register that nothing on the two codex branches beyond `fnn-hpi-offer` is cited by a dev caller before marking them dropped. `fnn-owner-publish-captured`
already pins the arena and writes a checkpoint from captured rows (by pointer: a whole-store copy);
`fnn-owner-reclaim-walk` already reads chunks at a pinned P3 generation. One walk, two consumers: snapshot =
pin; for each chunk at G, each row into the incremental image builder (`fn-his-row-begin/-step`, PRF-1265,
verified: `history-image-build-rows.lisp:73`); unpin. Reclaim = the same walk with `fn-orc-chunk`'s rewrite
per row, then the rebuild and the swap. The harvest stub's two branches (`codex/operability-held16`,
`-producer`, pre-P3) and the parked `host/native/snapshot-producer.lisp` (calls `fnn-hpi-offer`, undefined on
dev) are **dropped**, not redone; the register row says "superseded by the pinned walk (reclaim-2026-10-04 L4)".

**L5 — The retention bound and the ousted laggard.** **OPEN**: the default B and whether a response plan in flight is ousted or only off-mutex publishers/exports (the lean: oust it; a client that never reads is the laggard the bound exists for). The table has no bound: one reader that never ends
keeps every retirement stamped at or after its pin pending for ever (every COMPLETE's staged pages, every
dropped segment's descriptor, every forgotten handle). The bound B (a profile parameter: generations a pin
may lag, never a bound on stored data — D27) is applied by the owner as `(:oust B)`: every pin below CUR − B
leaves the live table for an OUSTED table; the next `:release` answers what those pins covered; an ousted
reader asking `(:held G)` before its next quantum is answered `:ousted` BY NAME and reads nothing more.
Precedent: LMDB's free-by-generation (the oldest reader bounds the free list) and libmdbx's
handle-slow-readers (the laggard is kicked). Host consequences (RECLAIM/served lanes, after this slice):
`fnn-arena-pins-step` calls `fn-arpb-step`; `(:oust B)` is sent at every `:retire`/`:stamp`; every
off-mutex reader's quantum begins with `(:held G)`; a publication or export answered `:ousted` restarts at a
fresh pin; a response plan in flight ends at its quantum boundary and the connection is closed by name
(`ousted`, logged); an ousted pass defers by name. This is the fairness mechanism requirement 8 asks for:
release progresses within B generations whatever holder is stuck, without another commit.

**L6 — What is linear on the 1k→100k curve, and what is not.**
- The walk: O(N) rows, each one funded page read, one decode/encode, one hashed context probe
  (`fn-rclp-ctx-reclaimable`: `hons-get` on the fast index `fn-rclp-index-into`, `store-reclaim-pack.lisp:84`);
  resident today O(N) (the `out` accumulation) — linear in time, not in space. The fix is in the walk's
  consumer, not the walk: stream each rewritten chunk into `fnn-owner-history-root-prepare-rows`'s row loop
  (today it takes the whole list) — resident = one chunk + the P3 candidate pages + the context.
- The rebuild (`fn-orcp-rebuild-is-the-full-open`): O(N) time and O(N) memory until D41 stage 3 (paged
  catalog) and stage 5 (history on the same rows); stated as the stage debt, not fixed here.
- The swap: O(connections) re-pins; the catalog/history install O(1).
- The release: O(pending retirements) per `:release`; with L5, pending ⊆ the last B generations' items.
- The GROUP count (K4): carried at the rebuild, O(1) per GROUP, no payload read.
- **Row 10b (485 s at 100k, offline dry run).** From source, the ACL2 fold is linear under keep-forever
  (`fn-rcl-standing-verdict` short-circuits at `:rule-keeps`; the host entry guard checks only `octets`,
  `host/interfaces.lisp:1194`). The superlinear term is one of: (a) the open/replay that precedes the walk
  (shared with every verb; the curve's `open` probe separates it); (b) `fn-rcl-verdict-heldp` — a list walk
  of the Store's verdicts per candidate (`store-reclaim.lisp:238`), O(N·V), reached under a releasing rule
  and by the classes line (`expiry.lisp:489`) under every rule: O(N²) if every article carries a verdict
  entry; (c) `fn-rcl-bp-holders` — subject→Message-ID over the articles per non-archive pin (O(P·N));
  (d) the classes line's per-article arena probe (`fn-rcl-payload-tombstonep`: one read of each payload's
  first octets — linear, but N cold reads). The measurement that names it (RECLAIM lane, hbox, one run):
  time open / `fn-store-reclaim-context` / `fn-store-reclaim-ctx-classes` / the fold / the decision
  separately at 1k, 10k, 100k; the term whose ratio exceeds 10× per decade is the cause. The fix for (b)
  is one fast alist of held verdicts built once in `fn-rclp-ctx` (`fn-rcl-held-verdicts` exists for the
  classes line but the fold's `fn-rcl-standing-verdict` still takes the raw list).

**What would change my mind.** On L1: a holder that cannot pin before it reads (the fenced/in-flight log
members: `arena-forget` §8.2 — the retirement waits for the next COMPLETE's reseat, or the swap word gains
"no host log member"; either keeps L1). On L5: a client-behaviour argument that ousting a response in flight
is worse than unbounded retention — then B is large and the oust is logged, but it exists; a node with no
bound cannot promise bounded disk. On L4: a snapshot consumer that needs rows the P3 root does not carry
(none found: the root carries every event, `history-paged.lisp` header).
