## Vocabulary for the six statements

The following are Lisp **proposition text**, not admitted events. New names
are proposed interfaces, not existing definitions. Free variables are universally
quantified. Each block is the statement to audit; some blocks intentionally
retain a refuted requirement instead of advertising a weakened theorem.

ART is `(HEAD . BODY)`; empty packed BODY is **1**, not 0 or nil.
`fn-art-head`, `fn-art-body`, and `fn-art-octets` project HEAD, BODY, and
`(append HEAD (fn-bch-unpack BODY))`. `fn-art-headp` must include octet HEAD,
canonical `fn-bch-packedp` BODY, and the first-separator split (or all input
in HEAD and BODY = 1 when absent). Merely ending in CRLFCRLF is insufficient
for canonical keys. Empty-header input beginning CRLF also needs an explicit
choice: the current parser regards that initial CRLF as its separator.
No new arbitrary header size ceiling is proposed.

`fn-art-decision-down`, `fn-art-sub-down`, `fn-art-owner-down`,
`fn-art-carry-down`, and `fn-art-parse-down` are logical representation maps:
replace ART payloads/parse bodies by their octets and preserve **all** other
fields, tags, statuses, ordering and errors. They may not execute on the host
path. A decision's ART payload is `fn-art-decision-payload` (slot 4).
`fn-art-sub-of`/`fn-art-record-of` convert model submissions/records to the
proposed representation; their total-domain obligations remain blocked by 2.
`fn-art-buffer-down` is the logical byte sequence of a returned buffer/range.
These maps must be defined independently of the functions being refined;
defining a map to call its reference would prove nothing.

### 1. Split at the first separator — obstructed total-domain shape

Subject: `fn-art-split`, before packing. The decomposition is exact; the
separator predicate means the *first* CRLFCRLF, not an arbitrary occurrence.
This adds location to recomposition, since recomposition alone also accepts
a last-separator split.

```lisp
(mv-let (head body) (fn-art-split x)
  (and (equal (append head body) x)
       (or (and (true-listp head)
                (fn-art-first-separator-headp head))
           (equal body nil))))
```

Positive witness: X = `(88 58 32 97 13 10 13 10 65 13 10 13 10 66)`;
HEAD = its first eight octets, BODY = `(65 13 10 13 10 66)`.
No premise to remove. Mutation must fail: choose the last CRLFCRLF; exact
recomposition still holds, but the first-separator assertion fails.
Total-domain must-fail: X = `7`. With BODY nil APPEND returns a true list;
with the other arm HEAD must contain a separator, so APPEND is a cons. Neither
can return 7. A guard declaration would not restrict ACL2's theorem domain.
