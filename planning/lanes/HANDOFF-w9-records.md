## What no theorem lost

Checked per book by diffing the reader's view of the names (`tools/ledger.py`
expands `fn-defrecord`, so a migrated book reads as its events):

- No function and no theorem is lost.
- Added, per record: `<ctor>-injective` (`:rule-classes nil`, a name for one
  includer's `:use`, never an enabled rewrite) and the `deftheory
  <name>-internals` the macro withdraws on the spot.
- Added in `books/records.lisp` only: `fn-record-accessors-forward-consp`,
  the per-accessor `consp` family this book omitted. That is the gap
  `books/replay.lisp` papered over with a local
  `fn-replay-record-is-a-true-list`; a later lane can delete that bridge.
- The `<accessor>-of-<ctor>` statements are alpha-variants: the constructor's
  formals replace the hand-written short names.
- Every keystone statement is unchanged.
- Every recognizer keeps its conjuncts **in the order they were written**: a
  cross-field conjunct rides with the last field it reads, so `fn-statep`,
  `fn-retain-statep`, `fn-node-statep` and `fn-exchange-statep` are the same
  carried invariants ACL2 admitted before.
- Two hoists the macro forces, because it emits the recognizer inside the
  record's block: `fn-exchange-kindp` and `fn-exchange-fact-listp` move above
  the record whose recognizer reads them.

## What the macro grew, and why

Three options in `books/defrecord.lisp`, each paid for by a record in the
queue, each mirrored in `tools/ledger.py` and pinned on both sides
(`tests/acl2/defrecord-tests.lisp` record 4, `tests/test_ledger.py`
`test_recognizer_formals_come_before_the_record_variable` and
`test_recognizer_guard_and_deferred_verification`):

- `:recognizer-formals` --- formals prepended to the recognizer and to its
  forward fact, for a recognizer relative to context the record does not
  carry: `(fn-articlep configured x)`, `(fn-pendingp configured nexts
  next-txid x)`, `(fn-node-stagep acceptance committed x)`. The record
  variable stays `x` and stays last, so field types and `:extra` are
  unaffected.
- `:recognizer-guard` --- `fn-node-stagep`'s guard is `(fn-retain-statep
  committed)`; a generated `:guard t` would have been a different function.
- `:recognizer-verify-guards nil` --- `books/node.lisp` verifies its
  recognizers in an end-of-book batch, and `fn-node-statep` needs a hint
  there. Neither option touches the accessors, which stay `:guard t` and
  verified on the spot; `books/node.lisp`'s batch therefore drops the
  fourteen accessor lines the macro now covers, and so does
  `books/records.lisp`'s.

Nothing else in the macro changed, and no hand lemma had to be written back.

## The records that do not fit, and why (not a TODO: a decision)

Rule 4 of `planning/lanes/HANDOFF-w9-proof-infra.md` --- widen the macro or
leave the record hand-written; never reshape the record. These are left, and
the lint counts them:

- **Two constructors over one accessor family.** `fn-cbor-result`
  (`books/cbor.lisp`, `:ok`/`:error`, len 2 or 3), `fn-replay-result`
  (`books/replay.lisp`, `fn-replay-ok`/`fn-replay-fault` sharing
  `fn-replay-result-kind/node/sequence/reason`), `fn-record-parse-*`
  (`books/records.lisp`). `fn-defrecord` generates one shape of one fixed
  width and one constructor; a sum would redefine the shared accessors. A
  `fn-defsum` is the widening that would take these, and it is a separate
  design, not a flag.
- **A constructor that derives stored fields.** `books/checkpoint.lisp`'s
  `fn-checkpoint-make frontier sequence node` stores `(fn-state-groups (fn-node-acceptance node))` and
  `(fn-retain-capacity (fn-node-retention node))` at indices 2 and 3, and
  index 1 is a version constant `1` with no accessor. The macro's constructor
  is `(list tag formal ...)`.
- **A recognizer that takes the record first.** `fn-cpp-imagep image groups
  capacity`, `fn-cpp-entryp e groups capacity`, `fn-cpp-statep` (defined far
  below its record, over helpers that need the entry record). The records
  themselves fit with `:recognizer nil` and their recognizers stay
  hand-written where they are --- which is the migration above, blocked on
  `checkpoint-codec`.
