Summary: The node's own web page: turning it on, inviting friends, and
 what it does and does not do.

The node serves a web page for reading and posting. Friends need
only a browser. No Python, no second program.

*For a friend:* Open the address you were given. Press "Make your
account", type the invitation code, choose a name and a password.
The same name and password work in tin.

*What you see:* groups, the newest posts, one post at a time, a
form to post, and "Remove my post" on your own posts. Light, dark
or automatic colours. Phones work.

*After you post:*
- "Posted!": the server took it.
- "The server said no": nothing was published; the reason is shown.
- "We couldn't tell": the answer was lost. Look at the group before
  sending it again.

*For the operator:* after mission wrote fn.toml, as root:

    sh /opt/fn/install.sh --reader

It adds a [web] table to fn.toml: port 8920 on 127.0.0.1, behind
Caddy (proxied = true). Edit site and domain, restart the node,
and look for LISTENING-WEB 8920. Put share/fn/caddy/fn-web.caddy in
the Caddyfile with your web name. Then invite:

    fn operator /var/lib/fn/fn.toml account invite

*Why trust it:* it is the node. A sign-in is the node's AUTHINFO,
an account its XREDEEM, a post its POST. A friend sees what their
newsreader would. ACL2 builds every page; article text is escaped;
no page runs a script. No password is written down.

*Not yet:* threads, search, read marks, moderation, and checking a
signature yourself (fn-verify does that).

*Go deeper:* docs/web.md.
