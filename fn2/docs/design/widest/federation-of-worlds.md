# The widest fn2, from the federation of worlds (council draft, 2026-10-28)

Vantage: fn2 as the one node a community runs that carries all of its worlds (mail, lists, chat, source, packages, review, attestations, ledgers, games, sensors, documents, names) as ports of one semantics, federating with other nodes over NNTP feeds, reaching the disconnected over BP, and able to move a whole living world from one node to another. Built on the pruned kernel of UNIVERSAL-CONSTRUCTIONS §9 (Log, Decide, Fold, Name; Binding, Span, Frontier, Obligation), the planes of PLANES-AND-GAMES §2 and its interweaving addendum, and EXECUTION-MODEL's one executor.

## 1. The world from this vantage

A **world** is what a community actually has: a set of commit domains, the event kinds they admit, the Decide and Fold programs that give those events meaning, the protocol tables of the wires it speaks, and the authority that may change any of it. Today each of those lives in a different daemon with a different identity scheme, a different history, a different backup story and a different idea of what "the same message" means: Postfix and Mailman and public-inbox, a Forgejo with a Postgres, a Synapse with its own DAG, a Rekor instance, a Nix cache, a Nextcloud. fn2 from this vantage is **one node, one journal discipline, many worlds, every wire a port**. A port is a declared translation between a foreign data model and a world's events, with a stated loss. NNTP stops being "the protocol fn speaks" and becomes the first port and the federation transport. SMTP, JMAP, git's smart protocol, the Matrix server-server API, ActivityPub inboxes, an AT Protocol PDS, a Nostr relay, a crates.io sparse index, a Nix binary cache, a Rekor-shaped transparency API, CalDAV and the node's own web reader are more ports over the same worlds.

**A Tuesday at a node run by a small community (call it the ferns), on a homelab box.**

- 09:10. A contributor runs `git send-email`. The SMTP port receives it, decodes the patch series into the list world's domain (exact octets kept, Message-ID bound) and, through the repo world's port declaration, into candidate patch events in the repo domain. A reviewer reading the list in Thunderbird over NNTP, another in Element through the Matrix port, and a third in the web reader inside the node see one thread; their replies are three wires into one Log. Nothing is bridged by a bot; there is nothing to bridge, because there was only ever one room.
- 09:40. The patch carries a review obligation and a CI obligation, created in the same commit as the patch (`review ⊗ ci-pass`). A CI runner on a rented box is an ordinary principal: it takes the CI obligation as a fenced lease, builds, and posts an in-toto/SLSA provenance statement as evidence. The node checks the statement against the world's declared evidence predicate (builder key in the world's authority fold, subject digest equal to the patch's tree Name) and discharges the obligation. A second runner that wakes late is refused `fenced`.
- 11:00. The maintainer merges. In the repo domain this is a Decide transition under the world's merge program; it emits the land receipt. The node's next checkpoint claim includes it, and two peer nodes (a university mirror and a friend's node) **cosign** that checkpoint as witnesses. The ferns' history is now a transparency log in the CT and Sigsum sense, without running one.
- 11:20. A release tag. The crate tarball and the Nix store path become Names, bound to the source tree Name and the build provenance. The crates.io-index port serves the sparse index entry; the Nix substituter port serves the narinfo. Anyone else's rebuild that produces different bytes is recorded as a second value in the reproducibility Binding: `conflicted`, visible, never overwritten.
- 14:00. A field ecologist on a boat with no uplink has a delegated sub-domain of the Wild Earth world: species sightings, tile edits and sensor records commit locally under a lease with demarcated bounds (how many tile claims, how much of the shared sample budget). Bundles go out over BP whenever the satellite window opens; custody is an obligation released by the receiving node's receipt. Transient telemetry stays on the boat; only promoted readings cross.
- 16:00. A match in the community's RTS finishes on a relay; its input-batch stream and result land in the games world; replays are consumers.
- 20:00. The ferns move the whole community from the homelab to a better box at a friend's house. The world's commit domains hand off authority with a signed `ceded-to` record at the old node; the new node verifies the bundle stream by refolding (or by the certified checkpoint claim), takes the domains at incarnation n+1, and every peer, Matrix server, NNTP feed and BP neighbour re-points from the handoff event. Every receipt, Message-ID, git hash, Matrix event id and obligation from the old node keeps meaning what it meant.

**On the wire**: client ports (NNTP, IMAP/JMAP, SMTP, git, HTTP for the web reader, Matrix client-server) at the edge; between nodes, **bundles**, a self-verifying unit (world Name, domain, position range, Merkle inclusion against a signed checkpoint, the payload Names it references, a custody obligation id) carried by NNTP streaming (CHECK/TAKETHIS keyed by Name), BP/TCPCL for the disconnected, or a file. **Durable**: every world's Logs, payloads by Name, checkpoint claims and cosignatures, obligations, the authority and identity folds, the port declarations themselves. **Live**: presence, typing, cursors in a shared document, a CI job's log tail, a spectator's view of a match, in the same streams as their durable siblings (the interweaving addendum). **Fast**: game ticks and media, outside, under leases. **What crosses node boundaries**: bundles, cosignatures, evidence, handoffs, and port traffic to foreign systems; never a node's local article numbers, never a fold's internal state without its claim.

## 2. Ten things it would build or subsume

1. **Mail and mailing lists.** One list world speaking SMTP in, NNTP and JMAP (RFC 8620/8621) out, with archives that are the Log rather than a copy of it, which subsumes Postfix + Mailman + Dovecot + INN and does what public-inbox (lore.kernel.org) does with git, without a second history. Kernel developers would switch because b4, lei and NNTP readers keep working on the exact bytes, and replies from Matrix or the web land in the same thread with the same Message-ID discipline.

2. **Chat and social federation.** A room is a commit domain with a server order, so Matrix's state resolution v2 becomes a Decide at the room's authoritative node, and the node joins Matrix federation, ActivityPub (whose inbox/outbox already are Spans over a Log), AT Protocol (a repo is an MST of records: a Name shape and a Fold) and Nostr (NIP-01 signed events; fn2 is a relay that actually promises retention) as ports. Users switch from a Synapse or a Mastodon instance because one identity, one retention promise and one moderation history serve every protocol at once, and the loss between them is printed, not discovered.

3. **Source control as patch theory over the Log.** Patches are events with declared dependencies and a declared commutation relation (Darcs's patch theory, Pijul's categorical patches after Mimram and Di Giusto, 2013), so two nodes that applied the same patches in different orders hold trace-equivalent Logs and know it; git is a port that ingests objects as Names under git's tree shape and refs as a Binding. Forgejo and GitHub users switch because pushes, pulls and send-email are ports onto one repo, and history is witnessed instead of trusted to a forge's database.

4. **Review and CI as obligations with evidence.** A change creates obligations whose evidence formula is the project's policy (`review(2) ⊗ ci-pass ⊗ (license-ok ⊕ waiver)`), runners are fenced lease holders, and the land receipt is the Decide that consumes them. It replaces GitHub branch protection, Gerrit labels and Zuul's gating because the gate is a checked, recorded discharge that any peer can re-verify, not a status bit set by a webhook.

5. **Registries and transparency.** Crates, Nix outputs and OCI images are Names bound to their source Names and provenance; reproducibility is a Binding whose conflicts are recorded; every checkpoint is a signed tree head in the RFC 6962/9162 sense with inclusion and consistency proofs served by a port, and federated nodes cosign each other (CoSi, Syta et al. 2016; the Sigsum and transparency-dev witness network). This subsumes a crates.io index repo, cache.nixos.org, Trustix, Rekor and a CT log with monitors, because a registry mirror becomes a witness instead of a cache, and TUF's (Samuel et al. 2010) freshness and rollback threats become checkable against cosigned frontiers.

6. **Ledgers with escrow.** Double-entry transfers through Decide (balance ≥ 0 is not invariant-confluent, Bailis 2014), balances as Folds with per-event-kind certificates, escrow as `⊗` obligations, and cross-node payments as linked obligations under the escrow method (O'Neil 1986) and the demarcation protocol (Barbará and Garcia-Molina 1992). Communities running a TigerBeetle or a Postgres ledger beside their chat switch because a payment, its escrow and the conversation that agreed it share one witnessed history.

7. **Wild Earth, matches and replays.** Tile packages as Names with outboard trees and range leases, species and region catalogs as after-commit projections, match input streams and results as Logs under relay leases (PLANES §2.4), field builders reaching the world over BP. This composes with the game's own fast executors and replaces Steam Workshop, replay hosting sites and the bespoke asset CDN because content, provenance and results travel together between community nodes.

8. **Scientific and sensor provenance.** Observations and derived products as Names with W3C PROV-shaped Spans (used, wasGeneratedBy, wasAttributedTo), a STAC catalog as a projection, sensor streams interweaving transient readings and promoted durable ones, and overlapping instruments reconciled as a sheaf: local sections over regions, gluing as a checked Decide, obstructions recorded as conflicts (Robinson's and Curry's sheaf work on sensor integration). It replaces a STAC API server, DVC and a bucket of S3 objects because a dataset's lineage and its custody across a ship-to-shore BP link are one record.

9. **Documents and calendars.** In one domain, a document is a Log of operations with application-assigned element identities and a server order, so no CRDT metadata is needed; across disconnected nodes the document declares a merge-capable Fold (Automerge, Kleppmann; Peritext for rich text) and the node keeps both views honest. CalDAV and JMAP Calendars are ports; Nextcloud and Google Docs users switch because the same document is editable offline over BP, citable by Name and position, and reviewable under the same obligations as code.

10. **Identity and naming.** A principal is a world of one: a key event log with pre-rotation (KERI, Samuel Smith), current keys as a Fold, equivocation as a Binding, cross-system proofs as Keybase-style statements (this Matrix id, this git signing key, this Nostr npub, this DNS name are mine); naming is petnames per viewer (Stiegler) over registrar worlds whose Decide is DNS-like first-come or community-governed. It replaces Keybase, did:plc, PGP keyservers and per-service accounts, and answers Zooko's triangle by keeping all three corners as separate, explicit records.

## 3. What the kernel must contain

The eight interfaces are right and too small in five places, each the same failure: they describe one node's worlds, never a world that lives on several nodes, arrives from a foreign system, or moves.

**Log becomes a trace monoid per world, with foreign parents.** A free monoid over-specifies order, which UNIVERSAL §1.2 already says. Federation needs the world to *declare* the independence relation per event kind (Mazurkiewicz traces), so trace equivalence is computable: two nodes holding the same patches, the same ActivityPub deliveries or the same sensor readings in different orders know they hold the same world. And foreign histories are DAGs (git commits, Matrix `prev_events`, SSB feeds per author, AT Protocol commits); an ingested event carries its **declared foreign parents** as a Span, and the domain's local order is one linearization of that DAG. Positions stay local (L5); the foreign DAG is the federated truth.

**Decide becomes a named, replayable, delegable program.** Every committed outcome records the Decide program's Name (its revision) beside its command, so any node can verify `outcome = Decide_rev(state, command)` by re-execution; this is the versioned executable world: observations valid under the revision they happened in, migrations as events in the world's meta-domain. Authority to Decide for a domain is itself an **Obligation with holder and incarnation** (a lease on the domain), which makes handoff, failover and migration one record kind. And Decide must admit **delegated sub-domains with demarcated bounds**, so the boat decides offline within a budget it was granted and reconciliation is a check, not a merge.

**Fold and every program become Names.** Fold declarations, projection declarations, Decide programs, evidence predicates and port declarations are content-named terms in the machine DSL (Unison's content-addressed code is the precedent), so a world's meaning travels with its data and a checkpoint claim cites the fold Names it summarizes. A peer that does not trust a summary refolds with the same Name.

**Name gains a shape registry, including foreign shapes.** Name is a catamorphism over a declared shape; the registry must include git trees and commits (SHA-1 and SHA-256), Nix NAR, IPLD/CID DAGs, AT Protocol MSTs, OCI manifests, BLAKE3 outboard trees, RFC 6962 Merkle trees. A foreign identity is then a verified alternative Name of the same bytes: a Binding from foreign Name to native Name, established by recomputation and never by assertion.

**Frontier gains cosignature.** A checkpoint's frontier signed by other nodes (witnesses) is the only cure for EXECUTION-MODEL's `A-INDEPENDENT-FRONTIER` that does not require trusted hardware: federation *is* the independent witness. The record kind is `(world, domain, checkpoint Name, witness, witness's own checkpoint position)`; rollback is detected when a node presents a frontier below one it was witnessed at.

**Obligation spans nodes.** Evidence may arrive from foreign signers and foreign protocols (a Sigstore bundle, a BP custody signal, a Matrix receipt); the evidence predicate is a named program over evidence Names, and cross-node escrow is a pair of obligations linked by a condition (the Interledger/HTLC shape without a chain).

**Four new kinds the eight cannot express:**
- **World**: a manifest Name over (domains, event schemas with independence, Decide/Fold/predicate Names, protocol tables, ports, authority root). Revisions of a manifest are events in the world's meta-domain; a running world's state is always "this manifest revision × this history".
- **Port**: a declared translation `foreign ⇄ events` written in the command-grammar DSL, with a **loss signature**: the equivalence it preserves, as an element of a lattice of losses (exact octets; semantic content; threading; reactions; edit history; signatures). Ports are quotient lenses in the sense of Foster, Pilkiewicz and Pierce (2008), and functorial data migrations in Spivak's sense; composition of ports computes the loss of a bridge. This is the restricted update language that UNIVERSAL §7 reserved the lens vocabulary for.
- **Principal-as-Log**: the identity world above, so keys rotate, delegate (UCAN- or Biscuit-shaped attenuated chains as records) and equivocate visibly; Robigalia capabilities and remote principals use the same shape.
- **Bundle**: the one transfer unit for NNTP feeds, BP, world moves, mirrors and backups, verifiable against a cosigned checkpoint without trusting the carrier.

## 4. The one capability that would make it epic

**Transport of a living world along a certified equivalence.** One checked transition, `transport(world, equivalence, destination)`, covers four operations that every existing system treats as unrelated emergencies: **move** (the world leaves node A for node B with its authority: a `ceded-to` record at incarnation n, the destination's acceptance at n+1, every outstanding obligation and lease carried, every foreign identity still resolving), **fork** (a new world sharing the history up to a cut, with a new authority root, as a community splits), **mirror** (a read-only replica that witnesses), and **translate** (the world rendered into another protocol's model through a port, with the loss signature printed). The certificate is checkable by anyone: the destination's history is the source's history (bit-exact, by Merkle consistency proof against a cosigned checkpoint), or trace-equivalent to it (by the declared independence), or its image under a named port (by re-running the port); Decide and Fold Names guarantee the destination computes the same meanings. No existing system offers this: Bluesky's account migration moves one repo and depends on did:plc; ActivityPub's `Move` moves followers and loses everything else; Matrix rooms cannot change homeserver authority; Urbit ships cannot move to new keys without a breach that erases history; a git remote moves code and leaves issues, review, CI, releases and discussion behind.

A builder starts here: (1) the bundle format and the handoff record pair (`ceded-to`, `accepted-from`) on the existing Merkle log and checkpoint claim; (2) domain authority as a lease Obligation with incarnation and fencing; (3) refold-to-verify with fold Names cited in the checkpoint claim; (4) the demonstration: a list world plus a repo world move from one node to another while an NNTP peer and a git client keep working, and the only thing either client observes is a handoff event. Ports and their loss lattice come next, with Matrix ↔ list ↔ NNTP as the first composed bridge.

## 5. What it must never do

fn2 must never become the namespace that owns everything, as Urbit did: it composes with foreign systems by keeping their identities as exact octets and verified alternative Names, and a world that leaves takes everything with it. It must never lose in translation silently: every port declares its loss, every bridge computes it, and every promoted, ingested or translated fact names its origin and the revision under which it was decided. It must never let federation launder authority: a remote node's claim, cosignature or evidence is a fact about what that node said, checked by a declared predicate before it discharges anything, and no domain is ever decided by two nodes at the same incarnation.
