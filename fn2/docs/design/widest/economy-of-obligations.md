# fn2 as the economy of obligations (widest-role council, one voice; 2026-10-28)

Vantage: fn2 as the ledger that a civilization's commitments run on. Grounded in FN2-ORIENTATION, EXECUTION-MODEL §1-3, STORAGE-DESIGN §1 and §4, PLANES-AND-GAMES §2 and §5.1, the interweaving addendum, UNIVERSAL-CONSTRUCTIONS §0, §5.1, §6 and §9, and CLIENTS-AND-DATA-MODELS §0 and §2.2. Nothing was fetched; systems and papers are cited from memory by name.

## 1. The world from this vantage

Every economy is a graph of promises: I owe you goods, you owe me money, the bank owes both of us our deposits, the insurer owes the farmer if the rain fails, the member owes the cooperative a vote by Friday. Money is the special case of a promise that can be handed to a stranger. Today these promises live in five incompatible places: bank cores (mutable balances behind batch files), payment rails (ACH, cards and ISO 20022 messages whose central failure mode is the ambiguous timeout), blockchains (one global order bought at enormous cost for everything, including things that never needed an order), contract-management systems (PDFs whose obligations nobody tracks), and spreadsheets (most of the world's actual commons). fn2 already has the four things all of them lack together: a per-domain ordered log, a serial Decide at each domain's head, folds certified by the summary equation, and an Obligation that is "either outstanding or has exactly one authorized discharge" (UC §6.1). Read from this vantage, **fn2 is an obligation machine: a network of sovereign books, each a commit domain with its own Decide, in which commitments are typed linear resources with evidence formulas, money is a transferable obligation with a witnessed history, and books settle with each other by exchanging evidence instead of sharing a chain.**

Three properties make it different from everything above:
- **Exact retry.** Every turn carries a principal, an operation identity and its binding (expected prior version); a retry returns the original outcome without re-execution, and a contradictory reuse is refused by name (STORAGE decision 1). Stripe's idempotency keys are an API convention; here they are the commit protocol.
- **Typed uncertainty.** An answer is `accepted`, `refused(word)` or `uncertain(position)`. The rails' worst failure (the payment that may or may not have happened) becomes a first-class state with a fence and a resolution protocol, never a support ticket.
- **No consensus tax where none is needed.** CALM and invariant confluence (UC §5.1) say exactly which choices need an order: `balance >= 0`, uniqueness, auction clearing, claim allocation. Those run in one domain's Decide. Everything else (attestations, evidence, equivocation facts, reputation inputs, have-ranges) merges across nodes by union as Bindings, with no coordination at all.

**A Tuesday.** A flour cooperative of forty bakeries runs one fn2 node; its book is a domain. At 06:10 a member's agent submits a turn `transfer(member→pool, 18.40 EUR, op=7f3…, expect=v9121)`; the line drops, the agent retries, and receives the same `accepted(pos 88,412)` it would have received the first time. The pool's Decide posts a pending purchase hold against a mill's book on another node; the mill's book accepts its side, and the cooperative's evidence for posting is the mill's signed checkpoint claim plus an inclusion proof of the hold, witnessed by two cosigners the cooperative's constitution names. At noon, a member proposal to change the dues formula opens; every member now carries an Obligation `vote ⊕ abstain ⊕ proxy(to)` due Friday in the cooperative's time domain; delegations are a Span; the tally is a Fold; the new dues formula is a new Decide, content-named, installed by the old Decide's amendment rule. In a port warehouse the same afternoon, a pallet of insulin changes hands three times; each handoff discharges a custody obligation (BP custody, literally) and creates the next, and the temperature logger's readings are transient items in the pallet's stream until an excursion, when the carrier's policy promotes the reading to durable evidence and the parametric insurance obligation in the risk pool's book becomes dischargeable. In a review fleet, a seat contracts to review a pull request for 200 credits; the credits sit in escrow, the evidence formula is `land-receipt(pr) ⊕ rejected-with-reasons(pr) ⊕ expiry`, and the node's own metering of that seat's waits and commits is posted in the same units to the same kind of book.

**On the wire**: signed turns (command, principal, op-id, binding), outcomes with positions, checkpoint claims, inclusion proofs, attested claims (statements with issuer, subject, kind, supersedes), and obligation evidence. **Durable**: each book's journal, its obligation table, its folds (balances, positions, tallies), its installed Decide identities, its signed checkpoints, and the evidence it has accepted from foreign books. **Live**: quotes, bids-in-progress in continuous markets, sensor telemetry, presence of traders in a room, all transient items interwoven in the same streams as the trades and fills they precede (the addendum's per-item class). **Fast**: the matching inner loop of a high-frequency venue, which is a fast-plane executor under a fenced lease that commits fills as ordinary turns and withholds confirmation until the book's outcome (L3, output gating). **Across node boundaries**: never state, only claims about state: checkpoint claims, inclusion proofs, attested statements, equivocation evidence and obligation discharges.

## 2. Ten things it would build or subsume

1. **Double-entry books.** A book is a domain whose Decide refuses any transfer whose postings do not sum to zero per unit, whose balances are a Fold, and whose conservation (issued minus burned equals the sum of balances) is a declared fold law certified per event kind. It subsumes TigerBeetle's transfer primitive (accounts, transfers, pending/post/void, linked events) and Beancount/ledger-cli's plain-text discipline, and its users switch because they keep TigerBeetle's semantics while gaining arbitrary obligations, signed history and federation that TigerBeetle deliberately omits.

2. **Payments across books.** A cross-book payment is a pair of escrow holds whose evidence formulas reference each other's book through witnessed checkpoints, the shape of Interledger's atomic mode and Lightning's HTLCs without a shared chain or a routing network. It composes with ACH and ISO 20022 as adapters (a rail message is an external effect recorded as an outbox obligation, discharged by the rail's return file), and treasurers switch because ambiguous outcomes become `uncertain(position)` with a fence rather than a reconciliation week.

3. **Ricardian contracts with escrow and succession.** Ian Grigg's Ricardian contract (prose and parameters signed together, hashed into every transaction that relies on it) becomes a Name for the contract text plus a Name for its Decide, and Nick Szabo's "smart contract" becomes a bounded, deterministic, replayable Decide that only owns the obligations it created. It replaces Ethereum contracts for every agreement whose parties do not need global consensus, and contract-management suites (DocuSign CLM, Ironclad), whose users finally have obligations that are tracked, discharged by evidence and survive a party's death or dissolution through a declared successor rule.

4. **Markets, auctions and prediction markets.** An exchange is Decide at a domain head; Budish, Cramton and Shim's frequent batch auctions are literally one Decide per batch over sealed orders; sealed-bid auctions are commit-reveal (a Name now, an Obligation to reveal by a deadline); Robin Hanson's logarithmic market scoring rule is a Decide with a cost-function Fold, and resolution is an attested claim with an appeal window. It subsumes a matching engine plus its regulatory audit trail (the Consolidated Audit Trail exists because venues cannot prove their own order history) and Augur/Polymarket, whose users want the market, not the gas.

5. **Governance.** Votes are signed turns, delegations a Span (liquid democracy with cycles refused by Decide), tallies Folds, quorum a Frontier threshold, and Vote-as-obligation makes participation a tracked duty with `vote ⊕ abstain ⊕ proxy`; a constitution is a versioned Decide whose amendment rule is itself part of the Decide being amended. It subsumes Loomio, Snapshot, Aragon and Robert's-Rules spreadsheets, and offers Radical Markets' quadratic voting as a Decide over a voice-credit book; Ostrom's design principles (clear boundaries, monitoring, graduated sanctions, cheap conflict resolution, nested enterprises) each map to a primitive here, which no DAO framework can say.

6. **Law and adjudication.** A claim is an Obligation whose evidence formula names an adjudicator; filings and evidence are attested statements; a ruling is an attested claim that discharges or transforms obligations, and an appeal is supersession. It composes with court e-filing and replaces Kleros-style juries and arbitration case-management systems, whose users gain a record in which every ruling cites the exact positions it relied on and every remedy is mechanically enforceable against the books that agreed to the forum.

7. **Custody and supply chains.** Every handoff discharges one custody obligation and creates the next, with BP/DTN carrying the evidence across intermittent links, exactly as fn's bundle custody already does. It replaces Hyperledger Fabric consortium deployments (IBM Food Trust, TradeLens, both effectively dead because a shared ordering service among rivals was a governance cost nobody needed: handoffs only need signed bilateral evidence) and serves DSCSA pharma traceability, cold chain and police evidence lockers.

8. **Insurance and risk pools.** A mutual pool is a book whose members' premiums are obligations, whose reserve is a Fold read through a Frontier, whose claims are obligations discharged by adjudication or by a parametric oracle's attested claim, and whose reinsurance is an obligation on another book. It subsumes mutual-aid spreadsheets, Etherisc-style parametric policies and the bordereaux shuffling of the Lloyd's market, and pools switch because solvency becomes a checkable statement at a named position.

9. **Reputation and credit.** A reputation is a published Fold over witnessed history with a declared coverage (which books, which witnesses, which interval) and a declared loss (what history it could not see), signed as a projection statement `(fold-id, version, cov, digest)` as PLANES §2.2 already specifies. It replaces FICO-style opaque scores and marketplace feedback silos; a subject can reproduce their own score, dispute an input with evidence, and carry it to another market, and EigenTrust-style transitive scores become ordinary folds.

10. **Agent economies and metered everything.** Fleets contract, pay and are paid with the same obligations humans use; per-principal metering (EXEC rule 5, ember's "full up-front resource accounting") is posted as a book in resource units, so a node's bill to an agent and the agent's invoice to its client are the same kind of record, and Xanadu's transcopyright (every quotation pays its source automatically) becomes a fold over Spans of content Names with royalty obligations. It replaces Stripe metered billing plus usage logs, API credit systems and the emerging HTTP-402 agent-payment schemes, whose users want a receipt their counterparty cannot dispute.

## 3. What the kernel must contain

The pruned kernel (Log, Decide, Fold, Name; Binding, Span, Frontier, Obligation) is the right skeleton and the wrong size in four places.

**Obligation is too thin.** UC §6.1 gives `(creating event kind, holder, evidence formula, expiry, on-expiry outcome)`. An economy needs:
- **Two parties**: an *obligor* (who owes) and an *obligee* (to whom), separately transferable under different rules. Assignment of the right needs no obligor consent; delegation of the duty does (novation). Money is the obligation whose obligee is freely assignable.
- **Quantity in a unit**, with split and merge: an obligation of 100 EUR can become 60 and 40 to two obligees. Fungibility is a declared capability of the unit, not of every obligation.
- **Succession**: each principal declares a successor rule (a key rotation, an estate, a dissolved company's receiver), and an obligation whose party ends follows it instead of becoming orphaned. STORAGE §4.3 already says obligations survive account deletion; this names where they go.
- **Richer evidence formulas** that stay decidable and bounded: `⊗`, `⊕`, plus `k-of-n` attestation (multisig, quorum, jury), quantity thresholds (paid ≥ amount across several transfers), and **foreign evidence**: an event in another book, proved by that book's signed checkpoint claim, an inclusion proof, and a witness policy.
- **Continuations**: a discharge may create obligations (the next delivery, the next custody leg, the next premium), declared in the contract's protocol table (UC §6.2), so a contract is a finite protocol whose states own obligations.

**Decide must become data.** A Decide is a content-named program in a bounded, total, deterministic DSL (the machine DSL with work caps), installed in a domain by an event and amendable only by the installed Decide's own amendment rule. Every outcome records the Decide identity under which it was decided, so observations stay valid under the revision they happened in, which is ember's versioned executable world applied to law: an old contract is judged by its old rules, and any verifier can replay a book from its journal and its Decide Names.

**Fold needs conservation laws.** A Fold may declare an invariant over its state (per-unit sum zero; issued minus burned equals total balance; reserve ≥ declared floor) that every Decide outcome must preserve, certified per event kind by the local homomorphism certificate of UC §2.2. Double-entry is then a fold law, not an application convention.

**Frontier must reach across books.** A *witnessed foreign frontier* is the highest position of another book for which this book holds a checkpoint claim satisfying a named **witness policy** (cosigner set, quorum, freshness), as Certificate Transparency and Sigsum witnesses do. Foreign-evidence obligations are threshold reads on it.

Three record kinds become kernel, not application:
- **Claim**: an attested statement `(issuer, subject, kind, terms, depends-on, supersedes)`, already latent in fn's Statement object (CLIENTS §2.2 item 5). Oracle readings, appraisals, rulings, published folds and checkpoint claims are all Claims.
- **Unit**: a declared asset or resource kind with issuer, divisibility, fungibility, mint and burn authority, and conservation scope.
- **Witness policy**: a versioned declaration of which keys must cosign which origins' checkpoints, with the honest-quorum-intersection requirement STORAGE §4.4 already states.

And one protocol: **cross-book settlement** (§4), built from foreign evidence and holds.

Binding keeps its definition and gains its most valuable instance: **double-spend is equivocation**. A principal who signs two turns spending the same `(account, sequence)` into two books produces a Binding with two values, which is portable, merge-by-union evidence against that key. Offline payments over BP (a field team with no link for a week) cannot prevent a double spend, but they make it a provable debt, the move David Chaum's offline e-cash made with identity revelation, here made with ordinary records.

Span needs nothing new; ownership, delegation, membership and quotation graphs are Spans. Log needs nothing new; a book is a domain. Name needs one more subject: Decide programs and contract texts.

## 4. The one capability that would make it epic

**Settlement between sovereign books with no shared chain: proof-carrying obligations whose every step is discharged by witnessed foreign evidence, with exact retry, typed uncertainty, and equivocation turned into portable debt.** No existing system offers this: banks settle through a central ledger they must all trust (Fedwire, TARGET2), blockchains settle by making everyone agree on everything, Interledger had the atomic-mode shape but no evidence layer or witness model, and Lightning only works inside one chain's assets.

How a builder starts. A transaction `x` touching books A, B and C names a **settling domain** S, chosen by the parties (a notary, a clearing house, a counterparty, a cooperative's own book); S is just another fn2 domain with a published witness policy.
1. **Hold.** Each book's Decide accepts a pending hold for its leg (TigerBeetle's pending transfer, extended): an Obligation whose obligor is the paying account, whose quantity and unit are the leg, and whose evidence formula is `commit-in(S, x) ⊕ abort-in(S, x) ⊕ expiry(D, t)`, where D is a time domain S also observes. The hold reserves the funds; the book's conservation law counts it.
2. **Decide at S.** S's Decide records `commit(x)` iff it holds, before its deadline, foreign evidence of every leg's hold (checkpoint claim plus inclusion proof meeting S's witness policy for each book); otherwise `abort(x)` at expiry. This is two-phase commit whose coordinator is an auditable log, not a process.
3. **Post or void.** Each book discharges its hold when its witnessed foreign frontier of S passes the commit or abort; posting is a Decide whose precondition is that evidence. A book that cannot see S keeps the hold `uncertain`: fenced, counted, visible to its holder with the position at which it will resolve, never silently posted or released.
4. **Retry.** Every message in the protocol is a turn with an operation identity; resending any of them returns the recorded outcome, so a flaky link between books costs latency, never correctness.
5. **Equivocation.** If S signs both `commit(x)` and `abort(x)`, any party holding both has a Binding conflict on `(S, x)`: a self-proving fraud claim. Parties can require S to be bonded by an insurance pool (item 8), whose obligation is dischargeable by exactly that evidence, so the residual trust in S is priced and collateralized rather than assumed.

The first simulator scenario: three books on three nodes, S on a fourth, a partition isolating book C after it holds but before it sees S's commit, a retry storm from the payer, and S's key compromised to sign both outcomes. The statements to check: conservation holds in every book at every position; every hold is outstanding or has exactly one discharge; no book posts without witnessed commit evidence; C's hold is `uncertain`, never lost; the equivocation is discharged by the bond.

## 5. What it must never do

fn2 must never impose a global order or a consensus round on anything that merges as a validated fact, because the moment it charges every promise the price of a chain it has become one more blockchain and lost the economy that runs on bilateral trust and evidence. It must never hold a number that means value (a balance, a reserve, a reputation, a vote count) except as a Fold over a journal that justifies it, with no administrative adjustment that is not itself a signed, attributable event. It must never resolve uncertainty to look tidy: no obligation is discharged by silence, by wall-clock alone, or by a node's guess, and an ambiguous outcome stays `uncertain` with its fence and its position until evidence decides it.
