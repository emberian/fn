# fn2 as the substrate under other systems (a widest-role voice, 2026-10-28)

Vantage: fn2 is not a system people choose instead of Postgres, Kafka, git or S3. It is the durable layer those systems are re-hosted on, so that each becomes a projection of one log with a published frontier, and the whole stack shares one notion of "when", one notion of "what happened", and one notion of "who owes what". Kleppmann's "turning the database inside out" stopped at the boundary of one company's data pipeline; this takes it to the boundary of every durable system a company runs.

## 1. The world from this vantage

Every durable system already contains a log and pretends it does not. Postgres hides its WAL behind a heap; Aurora and Neon showed the opposite reading ("the log is the database": Verbitski et al., SIGMOD 2017; Neon's pageserver serving `GetPage@LSN`). Kafka is a log with no folds. git is a content-addressed store plus a tiny mutable map (refs) whose history expires with the reflog. A POSIX filesystem is a journal under a namespace whose crash semantics nobody can state (Pillai et al., OSDI 2014). Datomic is a log with four covering indexes and one transactor. A blockchain is a log with a signed checkpoint and an expensive way of choosing who appends. Search and vector stores are folds over somebody else's log, fed by CDC (Debezium) at a lag nobody can name. Redis is a fold with eviction. SQS and Temporal are logs of obligations.

fn2, from this vantage, is the one log they all share. Each system keeps its engine (planner, query language, wire protocol, clients) and gives up its durability layer. Its durable state becomes a stream or commit domain; its derived state (pages, segments, indexes, views, replicas, caches) becomes a declared projection with a frontier; its coordination (leader leases, consumer groups, ref updates, locks) becomes Decide and Obligation. Its process becomes a *projection executor*: a fenced, leased worker that reads by `seek`, applies, and publishes its frontier back as a signed statement.

**A Tuesday.** A five-person company runs a product on Postgres, a job queue, an S3 bucket of uploads, a search index, a Redis cache and a git monorepo. All six sit on one fn2 cluster of three nodes. At 10:14 a user uploads a photo and edits their profile. The application issues one `command` to fn2: the profile row change (a Postgres WAL record in the app's domain), the upload's content name (the bytes already `put`, leased), a job (an Obligation: "thumbnail owed for name N"), all at one position, p = 88,301,442. There is no outbox table, no dual write, no Debezium: the Postgres replica, the search projection, the cache and the queue are all folds of the same domain, and the transaction had exactly one commit. The application gets back a cut token for p. It redirects the user to their profile page, passing the token; the page server's Postgres read says `at-least(p)`, the search box says `at-least(p)` (the search projection's frontier is at p−40, so the read parks on the frontier for 3 ms), the cache entry for the profile is stamped p−9,000 and so misses by comparison rather than by invalidation message. Read-your-writes across four systems, from one integer vector.

At 11:00 a thumbnail worker (incarnation 7) crashes mid-claim; incarnation 8 finishes and 7's late result is refused `fenced`. At 14:30 an engineer opens a pull request that changes the `users` schema and the search mapping. CI calls `fork(domain = app, at = cut(main@now))`: a copy-on-write branch whose Postgres, index, bucket and queue are the same projections over the forked log, a staging stack at one instant without a dump. The migration runs as a *new projection version* racing the old; when it catches up, a Decide flips the read alias. At 16:00 an auditor asks what `orders`, the search index and the invoices bucket said at 09:00 on 1 October: three `exactly-at` reads at one cut, under a signed checkpoint claim.

**On the wire.** Between nodes: the journal, content names, checkpoint claims, Binding facts. Between fn2 and executors: the consumer protocol (`seek`, `wait`, `ack(cut)`), claims with incarnations, external-checkpoint records, signed frontier statements `(projection, version, cov, digest)`. Toward clients: the foreign wires unchanged (PG wire, Kafka, git smart HTTP, S3, 9P/NFSv4/FUSE, RESP), served by the executor or by fn2's own protocol tables. A client never learns fn2 exists unless it wants cut tokens.

**Durable**: the log, payloads, obligations, frontier statements, projection declarations (themselves events). **Live**: wake hints, cache contents, executors' working sets, transient items. **Fast**: an executor's apply loop, its own business under L3. **Across nodes**: names (bytes on demand, range-verified), events, cuts, frontier statements, leases; projections are rebuilt where needed unless a snapshot Name is cheaper than replay.

## 2. Ten things it would build or subsume

1. **Postgres on fn2.** Postgres's storage manager and WAL are replaced (the precedent is Neon: WAL to a quorum log, pages reconstructed on demand by WAL redo at an LSN); the WAL record stream is a durable fn2 stream in the database's domain, pages are a versioned projection, and replicas, point-in-time recovery and branching are `exactly-at` reads and `fork`. Users switch without regret because they keep every extension, the planner and psql, and gain Aurora's architecture on their own hardware plus a cut that their other systems also honour.
2. **Kafka on fn2.** A topic partition is a stream (partitions of one topic may share a commit domain when they need atomic multi-partition produce), consumer groups are claims with fenced incarnations, and offsets are frontiers that never expire under a quiet consumer (KAFKA-4682). Users switch because tiered storage and exactly-once stop being subsystems, and the same records feed Postgres and search without Kafka Connect.
3. **git and every VCS on fn2.** Objects are Names (SHA-1 and SHA-256 kept as validated aliases of the native BLAKE3 name), a ref update is a Decide with `expect = version`, divergent pushes from two remotes are a conflicted Binding recorded rather than one silently losing, and the reflog is the log itself, forever. Pijul's patch theory (Mimram and Di Giusto's categorical semantics of patches; pushouts as merges) and Jujutsu's operation log are folds and Log respectively, so git, Pijul, Jujutsu and Mercurial can share one object store and one durable operation history, and CI is an Obligation on a commit name.
4. **A POSIX filesystem on fn2.** The namespace is a fold over `create/rename/unlink/link` in one domain (so `rename` is atomic by construction), file contents are Names with outboard trees and range leases, `fsync` returns a position, and snapshots are pinned versions; it is served over FUSE, 9P or NFSv4, and on Robigalia it is the orthogonal-persistence image's directory face. Users switch because the crash semantics become a sentence ("after fsync returns p, every read at ≥ p sees the state at p") instead of the per-filesystem folklore ALICE catalogued.
5. **S3 and object stores on fn2.** `put` returns a name, `get(name, range, lease)` is BLAKE3/Bao range-verified, a bucket listing is a Span read at a position (consistent listing, which S3 only acquired in 2020), conditional writes are Decide, versioning is the log, and event notifications are subscriptions with lossless wake. Users switch because a listing, a version and an event now carry the same position as the database row that refers to the object.
6. **Datomic, XTDB and EventStoreDB on fn2.** Datomic is the nearest ancestor: immutable datoms, a transactor (Decide), EAVT/AEVT/AVET/VAET covering indexes (Spans), `as-of`/`since`/`history` (`exactly-at` and suffix reads); XTDB adds bitemporality, which is a declared valid-time domain on each event. Users switch because the transactor becomes per-domain rather than per-database, datoms can reference content names and obligations, and the history is independently verifiable through signed checkpoints.
7. **Blockchains, transparency logs and ledgers on fn2.** A chain is a Log with a witnessed signed checkpoint claim; what fn2 gives that chains lack is per-domain order without global consensus, equivocation recorded as a Binding fact (Certificate Transparency, Trillian, Sigsum's witness cosigning, Syta et al.'s CoSi), exact retries, and evidence-gated obligations for escrow; Sigstore's Rekor and CT logs become fn2 domains with witnesses. What it refuses: a global total order nobody needs, proof-of-work or stake as admission, and tokens whose value depends on the log's scarcity; a TigerBeetle-shaped double-entry ledger is a Decide plus a retraction-capable fold, which is what most "blockchain" deployments actually wanted.
8. **Search indexes and vector stores as projections.** Lucene/Tantivy segments are immutable sorted runs (the same shape as fn2's ordered-run engine) and an HNSW or DiskANN graph is a fold with declared approximation; each runs as an external projection executor publishing `(projection, version, cov, digest)`, so a query answer carries its position and a caller can wait for `cov ≥ p`. Users switch because the CDC pipeline and the reindex weekend disappear: a new mapping is a new projection version, rebuilt from the log and swapped by Decide when caught up.
9. **Caches as declared-loss folds.** A Redis or memcached entry is a fold value stamped with the position it reflects and a retention of `latest-only, evictable`; eviction is typed loss, and invalidation becomes a threshold comparison against the reader's cut instead of a message that can be lost. Users switch because "there are two hard things" loses one: a cache can be wrong only by being older than the token, and it says so.
10. **Message queues and durable execution as claims.** SQS, RabbitMQ, Celery and Sidekiq queues are a Log plus claims with incarnations and a dead-letter rule; Temporal and Restate are the larger target, since a workflow's event history is a Log, deterministic replay is a Fold, timers are time-domain frontiers, and activities are Obligations discharged by evidence. Users switch because the queue, the workflow history and the database row the workflow mutates commit at one position, which no current durable-execution engine can offer without its own database underneath.

Behind the ten, Lambda (Marz) and Kappa (Kreps) collapse: the log is the batch layer, after-commit maintenance is the speed layer, replay at a new projection version is the recompute, all under one frontier type. A database company is built on this the way Snowflake is built on S3: it ships an engine as a projection executor and inherits durability, replication, branching, audit, retention and cross-system consistency.

## 3. What the kernel must contain

For those ten to be ports, not bolt-ons, the kernel must make *a foreign engine as a projection executor* a first-class, checkable thing, and must make *a cut* something every engine honours. The pruned eight are the right algebra and too small in seven places.

**Log grows four things.**
- *Domains at scale and with lineage.* A substrate has millions of commit domains (one per database, per repo, per bucket, per Kafka transactional unit). Creating, splitting and merging domains are journaled events with a lineage record, in the manner of CockroachDB/Spanner range splits, so a cut over a split domain still means something.
- *Fork.* `fork(domain, at = p)` creates a child domain whose prefix is the parent's prefix through p, shared, with its own head. This is not in the eight; it is the free-monoid prefix order made into an operation, and it is what branching Postgres, staging stacks and agent sandboxes are. A fork's lineage is part of its checkpoint claim.
- *Record kinds with codecs, declared in the log.* A foreign event (WAL record, Kafka batch, packfile entry) is stored as its exact bytes plus an envelope extracted by a declared codec (a grammar-DSL parser), so projections and Spans select on `relation-oid`, `topic` or `path` without fn2 interpreting payloads. Codec changes are events; the codec at p is an `exactly-at` read.
- *Erasure.* A substrate under everything holds GDPR-bound data. Payload separation makes erasure possible: crypto-shredding of per-subject keys, or payload release under a declared erasure obligation, leaving a typed tombstone whose name still verifies the chain. A kernel record kind, not an operator script.

**Decide grows a cross-domain form.** UNIVERSAL-CONSTRUCTIONS declines products of free monoids. The substrate needs one controlled form of them: a *sequenced multi-domain commit*, where a command names a set of domains and a sequencing domain orders it deterministically before each participant applies it (Calvin, Thomson et al., SIGMOD 2012; deterministic databases avoid 2PC's blocking). Most cross-system atomicity disappears anyway, because the systems are folds of one domain; this covers the remainder (a rename across two filesystems, a transfer between two ledgers) and is charged to whoever asks.

**Fold becomes Projection, with five additions.**
- *Versioned interpretation.* A projection's identity is (sources, interpretation revision, codec revision); a new revision is a new projection racing the old; switching a read alias is a Decide. This is the "versioned executable world" essay made concrete: program revision × execution history, migrate and materialize as checked transitions.
- *Versioned retention as a mode.* `exactly-at(p)` for pages and rows needs keyed version state: keys suffixed by position in the ordered-run engine with a declared window (`versioned(window)`), the thing Neon's pageserver and Datomic's indexes both are.
- *The hosted-executor contract.* A foreign engine runs as a projection executor under a lease with an incarnation; it reads by `seek`, publishes snapshot Names through the external-checkpoint record, and publishes signed frontier statements. The contract states what a statement promises (every read it answers at ≥ p equals the declared interpretation over the log through p) and what fn2 does when it lies (a digest mismatch against an independent rebuild is a recorded Binding conflict on the statement).
- *Writes through Decide only.* An executor accepting an `INSERT` forwards it as a command and answers only after fn2's outcome (L3).
- *Declared loss and approximation.* Caches evict, vector indexes approximate; both declare it in the projection, and reads report it.

**Name grows aliases.** Foreign identities (git SHA-1 and SHA-256, S3 ETag, IPFS CIDs, OCI digests) are a Binding from a foreign name to the native name, admitted only after fn2 has recomputed the foreign hash over the bytes; a foreign hash collision (SHAttered) becomes a conflicted Binding, never an overwrite.

**Frontier grows the portable cut.** A cut token is a down-closed vector of `(domain, position)` plus, optionally, the projection versions it was read through. Every projection executor accepts it as `at-least` or `exactly-at`, every foreign wire carries it in a side channel (a Postgres `SET`, a Kafka header, an HTTP header), and `wait(cut)` parks on the slowest frontier. This is the generalization of LSN tokens (Postgres `pg_last_wal_replay_lsn`), Spanner's read timestamps and Materialize's timestamps to heterogeneous engines.

**Span grows codec-extracted keys**, so a secondary index over foreign records (`by relation`, `by path prefix`, `by ref name`) is a declaration, not a fold the user writes. **Obligation grows foreign evidence**: an obligation may be discharged by a projection's signed frontier statement ("indexed by search at ≥ p", "replicated to region B at ≥ p"), which is how backups, compliance holds and replication SLAs become checkable.

**Protocol tables for foreign wires.** The command-grammar DSL must be able to host PG wire v3, the Kafka protocol, RESP, 9P, git's pack protocol and the S3 REST surface as protocol tables, so a foreign wire served directly by fn2 (RESP for the cache, S3 for objects, 9P for files) is a port of the same grammar machinery as NNTP, with the same "clients cannot crash us" boundary.

So the eight become: Log (domains with lineage, fork, codec-declared record kinds, erasure), Decide (plus sequenced multi-domain commit), Projection (versioned interpretation, versioned retention, hosted executors, Decide-only writes, declared loss), Name (validated aliases), Binding (unchanged; it carries alias and statement conflicts), Span (codec keys), Frontier (portable cut tokens), Obligation (foreign evidence). Plus one DSL capability: foreign protocol tables.

## 4. The one capability that would make it epic: the stack-wide cut

No existing system lets one token name a consistent state of a whole heterogeneous stack. fn2 can: a **cut** is a down-closed vector of commit positions, and because every database, queue, bucket, index, cache and filesystem in the stack is a projection of those domains, the cut is simultaneously (a) a read-your-writes token every engine honours, (b) a wait condition ("block until search, the replica and the cache have all reached what I wrote"), (c) a time-travel coordinate ("every system as of the cut signed on 1 October"), and (d) a **fork point**: `fork(stack, at = cut)` produces a copy-on-write branch of every domain in the stack, on which every projection is rebuilt lazily or reopened from its nearest snapshot Name, giving a full staging environment, a migration rehearsal, a bug reproduction or an agent's sandbox containing the database, queue, files and index at one instant, in seconds, with no dump.

A builder starts in four steps. First, specify the cut token: its encoding, down-closure check, and the `at-least`/`exactly-at` semantics per projection, with the refusal words `gap` (retention), `version-unavailable` and `projection-behind`. Second, specify the hosted-executor contract and build two executors against it: a Postgres executor on Neon's split (a custom storage manager that asks for page@position; WAL redo as the projection's interpretation) and a Tantivy executor (segments as snapshot Names, a frontier statement per commit). Third, add `fork(domain, at)` as a lineage record in the journal with prefix sharing in the ordered-run engine (keys carry their domain lineage; a child reads through to the parent below the fork point). Fourth, the demonstration: one command writes a row, a document and an object at position p; three reads at the returned cut agree; `fork` at that cut; a destructive migration on the branch; main unaffected; the branch's checkpoint claim names its parent and fork position. That demonstration is the "Postgres + search + S3 with one commit and a git branch of all three" that no vendor sells.

## 5. What it must never do

fn2 must never become the query engine of the systems it carries: no SQL planner, no Lucene scorer and no merge algorithm inside the kernel, because the engines stay the engines and fn2 owns only order, durability, identity, frontiers and obligations. It must never let a projection, a cache or a foreign executor become an authority, or answer a cut it has not reached as though it had, because the whole value of the substrate is that every lag and every loss is a stated position. And it must never manufacture a global consensus or a global total order where a per-domain order suffices, because that is the blockchain's mistake and the reason every substrate before it became the bottleneck of everything built on it.
