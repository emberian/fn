# fn2 from the physical world and off-planet: the store-and-forward node of a solar-system network (council draft, 2026-10-28)

Vantage: the physical world, where links follow orbits, bodies break, clocks disagree, and BP is the point ("BP is product", ember, 2026-10-09). Systems and papers cited from memory; nothing fetched.

## 1. The world from this vantage

A messenger substrate assumes two parties, a server and a live link; the physical world breaks all three. From here, fn2 is **the node a physical thing carries so that its history, its promises and its identity survive disconnection, delay, and the destruction of the thing**. Every rover, robot, buoy, drone, meter, cubesat, field laptop and relay has one. Between them the wire is BPv7 bundles (RFC 9171, secured by BPSec, RFC 9172), carried over whatever exists: TCPCLv4 when there is internet, LTP (RFC 5326) over a radio pass, LoRa at a few hundred bytes a packet, a USB stick in a pocket. Cerf's DTN architecture (RFC 4838) gave the network layer, never the node's account of what it *is*: its world, obligations and time. fn2 is that account.

**A Tuesday, sol 1,412.** Earth-Mars one-way light time is 14 minutes. A rover's navcam frames went by as transient items; three are promoted when a Decide commits "target T selected" naming them by content. At 15:40 the rover gets an eight-minute UHF pass to an orbiter. Its node exports **journal segments**: self-verifying runs of its log, each with a predecessor link, Merkle inclusion material and the body's signature. It hands them to the orbiter's node as bundles. The orbiter's node accepts *custody*, which is an fn2 obligation, `delivered(DSN-ground) ⊕ custody-accepted(next-hop) ⊕ waived(operator)`. The rover's own custody obligation is discharged by the orbiter's signed custody signal, so the rover may now reclaim the bytes. At its Deep Space Network contact the orbiter forwards and is discharged in turn. At JPL the ground node verifies every segment against the chain it already holds, folds the new suffix into its projection of the rover, and publishes that projection with an honest strength: `at-least(p)`, rover position *p*, as of the rover's clock at 15:40 plus a declared uncertainty. Tomorrow's command sequence goes up the same way, as a signed stream. Each command is an obligation on the rover whose discharge evidence is the rover's own committed execution record. A command lost in the relay stays outstanding. It is never silently assumed.

**The same Tuesday, in a forest in Borneo.** A field biologist on day five of seven offline runs an fn2 node on a laptop with a solar panel. Six camera traps and a LoRa mesh of soil sensors (Meshtastic-shaped, managed flooding) publish into it. The sensors' readings arrive as transient items. A per-plot fold (a Kalman filter over soil moisture) commits its state every hour, together with its *covariance*, which is the declared loss. Her observations are durable and signed; clips are content-named payloads with outboard BLAKE3 trees. A colleague's phone passing through camp takes custody of a segment bundle and the clips' Names, reaches a town, and delivers; each hop is discharged by the next hop's signature. **If the laptop had gone into a river on day six, her world would still exist**: the phone carried its last checkpoint.

**The same Tuesday, in a warehouse in Rotterdam.** Forty robots each journal their decisions and task claims (obligations with fencing incarnations); perception is transient on the same subscriptions. Robot 17's controller dies; a replacement body **reincarnates robot 17's world** from the peers' witnessed segments, incarnation +1. Its old claims are fenced and re-offered, the loss typed `body-replaced`, never silence.

**What is durable, live, fast, and what crosses.**
- *Durable*: decisions, commands, observations, custody and obligations, checkpoints, calibration versions, contact plans, time-domain rate events.
- *Live*: perception, telemetry at full rate, position, presence on the mesh, link quality.
- *Fast*: control loops (motor PID at 1 kHz, attitude control, collision avoidance). These are outside fn2 entirely, as PLANES §2.4 rules, and they commit consequences and checkpoints.
- *Crossing node boundaries*: only self-verifying things. Journal segments, Names with range proofs, custody signals (signed obligation discharges), signed checkpoint claims, and witness co-signatures.

## 2. Ten things it would build or subsume

1. **The bundle agent's store and custody engine** (ION's SDR heap and bundle store; dtn7-rs; µD3TN). BP storage becomes the journal plus Names, and custody becomes an Obligation released by signed evidence with the same recovery states as everything else. Custody was dropped from BPv7's base (it is being re-specified as Bundle-in-Bundle Encapsulation); fn2 gives it a crash model, typed refusals and an audit trail.
2. **Contact Graph Routing as a projection** (ION's CGR, the CCSDS Schedule-Aware Bundle Routing standard). The contact plan becomes a durable stream of predicted contacts, routes become a fold over it, and each forwarding choice is a Decide whose rationale is in the journal. Operators switch because "why did bundle B go via orbiter O" becomes a query at a position.
3. **The robot's journal** (rosbag2/MCAP recordings, plus DDS durability QoS VOLATILE/TRANSIENT_LOCAL/TRANSIENT/PERSISTENT). One stream carries perception as transient items and decisions as durable ones, with durability per item rather than per topic (the interweaving addendum). A bag is a copy; this is the robot's authoritative, signed, replayable history.
4. **Spacecraft and rover command sequencing** (JPL's uplink pipeline; time-tagged sequences with command-loss timers; onboard autonomy such as AEGIS target selection and AutoNav). Sequences become signed streams, each command an obligation discharged by execution evidence, and onboard autonomous choices become Decide records beside ground-commanded ones. The ground view becomes a projection with a stated strength. "Did command 4471 execute, under which sequence revision" becomes a frontier read, not telemetry archaeology.
5. **Disconnected mesh and sneakernet sync** (Briar's Bluetooth/Wi-Fi/removable-media sync, Meshtastic's LoRa flooding, Secure Scuttlebutt's feeds and pubs). Every carrier, whether a phone, a USB stick or a LoRa hop, is a contact that takes custody of segments. Pubs become witnesses that co-sign checkpoints. A forked SSB feed kills the identity forever; fn2 records equivocation as a Binding conflict and survives it.
6. **Field science capture** (ODK Collect and KoboToolbox forms, iNaturalist observations, Argo floats reporting over Iridium, camera-trap pipelines like Wildlife Insights). Observations become signed durable events with sensor identity, calibration version and time interval. Media becomes Names whose range proofs verify on arrival, and Wild Earth's field builders get BP custody for the week they are offline. Provenance becomes the chain, and a lost device stops meaning a lost week.
7. **State estimation as a fold with declared loss** (Kalman's 1960 filter, EKF/UKF, particle filters, factor-graph SLAM such as GTSAM). An estimator is a Fold whose summary is lossy by design, and the covariance (or the marginalization error) is the declared loss. The estimate at any commit position is reproducible from the journal and the filter's declared revision. "What did the robot believe at 14:02:07, and why" stops depending on whether logging was on.
8. **Grid edge and energy markets** (OpenADR demand-response events, Green Button meter data, IEEE 2030.5 for DER). A demand-response call becomes an obligation on a battery or a heat pump, discharged by signed meter evidence over the declared interval. Settlement becomes a Decide over those discharges, and a meter's register is a signed fold. Disputes become chain verification instead of reconciling two vendors' CSVs.
9. **Physical custody in supply chains** (GS1 EPCIS 2.0 events, bills of lading, cold-chain loggers, chain-of-custody forms for evidence and samples). A physical handoff is a custody obligation discharged by a co-signed receipt from both parties' nodes, and the cold-chain logger's temperatures are a fold whose excursions open obligations. EPCIS records claims nobody is accountable for; here discharge needs the receiver's signature, and a refusal to sign is recorded.
10. **The flight recorder that cannot be lost** (aviation FDR/CVR, ACARS, fleet telemetry, satellite housekeeping, drone flight logs alongside FAA Remote ID broadcasts). Every body continuously ships witnessed checkpoints to peers, so the black box is the network. After a crash, the reconstruction is the last witnessed cut plus the custody still in flight, with the gap typed. Nobody has to find the box on the ocean floor to know what was decided up to the last contact.

## 3. What the kernel must contain

The pruned eight (Log, Decide, Fold, Name; Binding, Span, Frontier, Obligation) are right about *one node with a disk and a reliable clock that is usually reachable*. This vantage removes all three conditions, and six things go missing.

**3.1 World and Body, with Incarnation as a first-class interface.** The kernel today assumes that a commit domain and the machine writing it are the same thing. They are not. A **World** is a lineage: a history identity, a world key (or threshold key), and a chain of incarnations. A **Body** is a writer: a hardware identity, a body key, an incarnation number, and a fenced lease on the world's head. Reincarnation is the protocol that continues a lineage on a new body. It requires authorization by the world's recovery policy (a quorum of witnesses or ground, a threshold signature), a selected predecessor cut, and an incremented incarnation that fences every earlier body. STORAGE §4.4 already names lineage continuation; this vantage makes it the central operation. Record kinds: `incarnation-begin(world, body, predecessor-cut, authority-evidence)`, `incarnation-fenced`, `body-retired(cause)`; a stale body's writes past the cut are equivocation in a Binding on `(world, predecessor-head)`, never merged.

**3.2 Segment: the Log as an exportable, self-verifying unit of transfer.** In the pruned kernel, Log positions are local prefixes. Store-and-forward needs a **Segment**: a contiguous run of a world's journal with its predecessor head, Merkle inclusion against a signed checkpoint, the body signature, and the Names it references (by identity, with the payload shipped or deferred). Laws: segments compose by concatenation exactly when predecessor heads match. A receiver may hold a segment it cannot yet attach (a gap behind it) and records that as `pending-predecessor`, never as a rejection. A segment is to fn2 what a bundle is to BP: the bundle carries a segment, and the bundle's identity (source EID, creation timestamp, sequence) is the transport's while the segment's Name is the node's.

**3.3 Obligation that crosses domains.** The pruned Obligation is discharged by evidence *in its own domain's log*. Custody is a linear resource that moves: A's obligation is discharged by B's signed acceptance, and the acceptance creates B's obligation. So the kernel needs **evidence formulas over foreign signed facts** (an atomic predicate "a valid custody signal from world W, incarnation ≥ i, naming segment S"), and a **transfer law**: across the system there is at most one outstanding custodian per custody unit, except during a declared handoff window in which both hold it, which is the safe direction. RFC 5050's custody-signal reason codes (depleted storage, redundant reception, no known route, lifetime expired, destination unintelligible) become the named refusal and discharge words. The end-to-end theorem CLIENTS §3.6 already names, `fn-sys-release-is-grounded-end-to-end`, is exactly this law stated over F_dtn.

**3.4 Contact: a predicted Frontier.** Frontiers are monotone facts about what has happened. A DTN node also schedules against what *will* happen: the next pass, conjunction (Mars is unreachable for about two weeks every 26 months), a ferry's itinerary, a USB stick's expected return. The kernel needs a **Contact** record (`peer, window in a time domain, rate, confidence, source`), a contact-plan Fold, and outbound queues whose admission is charged against predicted capacity. A missed contact is an event, `contact-missed`, that re-plans and never loses data. The scheduler's service classes gain contact-driven work. Wall time still never selects a machine: the contact window's opening is a time-domain crossing (UNIVERSAL §6.4) delivered as an observation.

**3.5 Time as intervals in per-body domains.** PLANES §4.6 and UNIVERSAL §6.4 give time domains with rate events. Off-planet that is necessary but too small, for three reasons. First, every timestamp is **an interval `(domain, earliest, latest)`**, Spanner TrueTime-shaped: a rover's clock has drift since its last correlation with ground, and a cubesat without GNSS has more. Second, deadlines have **two firing senses**: "certainly passed" (latest < now's earliest) and "possibly passed". An obligation declares which one releases it, so custody never expires on a "possibly". Third, domains nest: body proper time, the planetary frame (Coordinated Lunar Time, directed in 2024; lunar clocks gain about 58.7 µs/day), and the DTN epoch (RFC 9171: ms since 2000-01-01, or zero plus a Bundle Age block for clockless nodes). Dilation is a rate event, ground correlation an anchor event; a clockless node's domain is "age since birth", converted at the first better clock.

**3.6 Fold with declared loss.** STORAGE decision 1 allows discarding history only through an observation-preserving simulation. Physical folds are *lossy by contract*: a Kalman filter keeps a mean and covariance, not the measurements; a downsampler keeps one reading in sixty; a sketch keeps quantiles. The kernel needs a **lossy fold declaration**: the interpretation, the summary, and a *loss statement* that is itself state (covariance, error bound, sample rate, quantile error) and travels with every read. The law: exact folds keep the summary equation, and lossy folds keep it for the summary state while their adequacy is a bound, never an equality. A reader that needs exactness gets `unavailable(estimated)`, typed.

**3.7 Attested observation and sensor identity.** A physical fact never enters as a fact. It enters as an **observation**: `(sensor identity, calibration revision, time interval, value, body, incarnation)`, signed by the body, with the calibration itself a durable versioned stream. This is the "observations valid under the revision they happened in" clause from ember's versioned-executable-world essay, applied to instruments. A recalibration is a migration of every projection that read the sensor, never a rewrite of what was observed.

**3.8 Accounting in joules and link-bytes.** EXEC rule 5 extends to **energy**, **link budget per contact**, and **storage under custody** (refuse custody by name, `depleted-storage`, never accept and drop): more accounts, same rule.

**3.9 A consumer protocol with no round trips.** The `poll/wait/ack` table (UNIVERSAL §6.2) assumes a session. Under light-minute delay each request is a self-contained bundle carrying the cut it assumes, each reply carries the cut it answered at, and rebase is normal, not an error: Honda-Yoshida-Carbone asynchronous session types as a requirement, not "later".

**What the eight become.** Log becomes Log plus Segment (exportable, attachable, pending-predecessor). Decide gains import as a command kind. Fold gains declared loss. Name is unchanged, and it is the hero, because verification on arrival is everything when the sender may no longer exist. Binding gains world-level equivocation. Span is unchanged. Frontier gains the predicted Frontier (Contact) and interval time. Obligation gains foreign evidence and the transfer law. Two named interfaces are added, **Incarnation** (World and Body) and **Contact**. Observation enters as a record kind with a required shape.

## 4. The one capability that would make it epic: a world that outlives its body

No existing system lets a physical node's *whole world* (history, outstanding promises, identity) survive the hardware's destruction and continue, verifiably, on a replacement body. ION keeps bundles, SSB keeps feeds, a flight recorder keeps a tape; none keeps a world. fn2 already has signed Merkle checkpoints, independent witnesses (A-INDEPENDENT-FRONTIER), fenced obligations and lineage continuation; it has to make them one protocol.

**How a builder starts.**
1. *Split keys.* Each world has a world key, held as a threshold share among its declared recovery set (ground, two fleet peers, the operator), and each body has a body key that signs segments under the world's current incarnation.
2. *Witness continuously.* At every checkpoint the body offers the checkpoint claim, and opportunistically its segments, to its witness set as custody bundles. A witness atomically verifies extension and persists its frontier before co-signing (STORAGE §4.4 verbatim). The body's **witnessed frontier**, the latest cut held by a quorum, is a Frontier the body knows about itself, and it is the worst-case loss bound published on the body's status: "if I die now, you lose at most the history after p_w".
3. *Make outstanding obligations portable.* Custody held, claims held and command obligations held live in the journal, so a witnessed cut carries them. On reincarnation each one is either resumed by the new body (custody of bundles whose payloads a witness also holds), released as `body-replaced` to its creditor (claims re-offered, commands reported unexecuted), or marked `unknown-after(p_w)` when the evidence died with the body.
4. *Run the reincarnation protocol as one Decide on the recovery quorum.* The new body presents `incarnation-begin(world, new body key, chosen cut ≤ max witnessed, evidence)`. The quorum threshold-signs it. The incarnation increments, and every peer's fence for that world rises with it. Bundles addressed to the world's endpoint ID route to the new body as soon as the contact plan learns its location, because the EID names the world, not the body.
5. *Make the zombie case a fact.* If the old body was only out of contact (in a dust storm, under a solar conjunction, or in a robot's pocket), its post-cut segments arrive later as equivocation against the new incarnation's head. They are recorded in the world's Binding, kept as evidence, and fenced from authority. An operator can then *graft* them as observations, never as decisions.
6. *Simulate it first.* The deterministic simulator gains a body-destruction fault: kill a body at every write, fence and install boundary *and* at every point of a witness exchange. Reincarnate from every quorum subset; check promises preserved to `p_w`, later loss typed, never two incarnations with authority.

It buys the sentence no mission manager has been able to say: **"the rover is gone; its world is not"**: transport of a world along the equivalence "same lineage, later incarnation".

## 5. What it must never do

fn2 must never make safety depend on a round trip, a timeout or the reachability of a peer: every protocol it speaks has to stay correct when the answer arrives an hour, a conjunction or a year later, or never. It must never treat a sensor reading, a clock or a position as truth. They enter as attested observations with an interval and a calibration revision, and a physical claim that was never signed by a body under a live incarnation is not a fact. It must never sit inside a control loop or let any body's wall clock select work: motors, attitude and collision avoidance stay in the fast plane, and they speak to fn2 only through commits, checkpoints and output-gated consequences, so a stalled disk on a rover delays a record and never a brake.
