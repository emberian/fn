# fn2 as the sovereign node: the one process you run on every box on a hostile internet (council draft, 2026-10-28)

## 1. The world from this vantage

Today a person's digital life is about forty processes run by about eight companies. The homelab answer (Mailcow, Nextcloud, Synapse, Vaultwarden, restic, Tailscale, all under Docker Compose) gives you the forty processes on your own box: forty attack surfaces, forty upgrade treadmills, and no common answer to "who is allowed to do what, and what happened". Urbit saw that the answer is one event-sourced personal computer with one identity. It then bought a scarce, purchasable namespace, an interpreter nobody else could audit, and a system whose shape forbids you from leaving your star. fn2 from this vantage is Urbit's sentence with ordinary parts. It is one deterministic executor over one journal per commit domain. Every service is a convention over the same eight interfaces, and every byte that enters is charged to a principal before work is done on it.

**A Tuesday.** Maya runs the node on four boxes: a 4-vCPU rented box in Falkenstein (the always-on one), her laptop, her phone, and a Pi 5 in her village's community garden on a solar panel and a LoRa-plus-4G link that is up perhaps six hours a day. Her community, the garden co-op of forty people, runs one more node on a donated homelab box in someone's closet.

- *07:10.* The laptop wakes from a three-day sleep. It does not "sync". It presents its Frontier for each of Maya's domains: a vector cut of positions, plus a have-range set for payloads. The box answers with the missing suffix as portable signed events and content-named blocks. Catching up costs work proportional to the difference, the way Syncthing's block exchange does, but with the event history and not just file states. The laptop verifies the box's latest checkpoint claim against the co-signatures the Pi and two friends' nodes gave it.
- *07:15.* The phone sits behind carrier-grade NAT and is never reachable. It holds a BP custody relationship with the box. Overnight the box bundled Maya's inbox delta, chat mentions and three files into bundles with custody transfer. The phone pulled them over its outbound TCPCL connection and acknowledged custody. Each acknowledgement releases a forwarding Obligation on the box. Nothing was polled every two seconds.
- *09:00.* Mail arrives. An SMTP session from a stranger enters the same bounded machine discipline as NNTP: grammar-driven parsing in 4 KiB quanta, inside the core. Until it authenticates (DKIM evaluated against a Name-cached key, or a delegated sending grant), it is charged to the anonymous principal slot. A message from a sender holding a capability Maya issued ("anyone I have emailed may write to me, 20 messages a day") is charged to that grant. The inbox is an `addr` Span plus a Fold. IMAP and JMAP are projections, and so is the web reader.
- *11:30.* Someone points 40,000 connections from a /16 at the web reader and an SMTP listener, half of them slowloris. Every one of them lands in the anonymous principal's calendar slot, and their connection-seconds are a metered resource. When the anonymous budget runs out, refusal is an ordinary continuation that costs a fixed reply. Maya's chat wake still arrives within the scheduler's L-visit bound. Her phone's bundle pull, the co-op's feed and the Pi's telemetry all keep their reserved shares.
- *14:20.* The provider live-migrates the rented box, and the disk image comes back from a snapshot 40 minutes old. A Postgres or SQLite node would serve that past without a word. The fn2 node's recovery finds an intact local root and comparison shows nothing wrong with it. Then the first peer it contacts presents a checkpoint claim for the same history-id and incarnation with a larger event count and a co-signature from the Pi. The node classifies itself `rolled-back (witnessed)`. It refuses to publish or accept anything that would fork the witnessed history, fetches the missing 40 minutes of events and payloads from the laptop and the co-op node by Name, re-verifies, and resumes.
- *18:00.* Maya loses her phone on a bus. From the laptop she issues a key-event: rotate the phone's device key out. The rotation is authorized by two of her three device keys, under a pre-rotation commitment the KERI way. Every grant the phone held is revoked with it, because revocation is a Frontier threshold on that key's event log. The co-op node and her friends' nodes learn of it through the next feed exchange, and they witness it too, so an attacker holding the phone cannot present an older key state to anyone who has seen a newer checkpoint.
- *Sunday.* Maya moves her world off the rented box to a box in her flat. `transport` hands the new node the logs, payloads, key-event log, grants, obligations and witness set. The new node verifies the whole world against the last witnessed checkpoint, either by replay or by a summary certificate. A succession event, signed under Maya's key-event log, names the new node as the history's host. The old box receives a Move record and becomes a read-only redirecting mirror until its custody Obligations discharge. Then it is released, and the provider holds nothing that can claim to be her.

**What is on the wire:** NNTP (articles and statements, TAKETHIS feeds), SMTP in and out, BP bundles over TCPCL, LTP, a USB stick or LoRa; the consumer protocol (wait, poll, ack); HTTP for the web reader and JMAP; TLS with post-quantum key exchange everywhere. **Durable:** journals, content-named payloads, key-event logs, grants, obligations, checkpoint claims and co-signatures. **Live:** presence, typing, "the laptop is online", the Pi's sensor stream (transient items, promoted once an hour as a summary). **Fast:** nothing in the node; a game or voice relay runs beside it as a principal. **Across node boundaries:** portable signed objects, statements, checkpoint claims, co-signatures, custody receipts, key-events and grants, never pages and never process state.

## 2. Ten things it would build or subsume

1. **Mail (Postfix, Dovecot, rspamd, Mailcow/Mail-in-a-Box).** SMTP becomes one more command grammar inside the core, the mailbox becomes an `addr` Span with a read-state Fold, and IMAP and JMAP become projections. Self-hosters switch because the parts that ruin self-hosted mail today (Maildir and index corruption, unbounded queue growth, spam handling as heuristics after admission) become metering before admission plus grants that let known correspondents in by capability.
2. **News and forums (INN, Discourse, phpBB).** fn's home ground: groups are streams, threads are a References Span, moderation is Decide, and the web reader lives inside the node. Discourse users get a forum that peers, that ordinary newsreaders can read, and that survives the host because every co-op member's node carries the feed.
3. **Chat (Matrix Synapse/Conduit, Zulip, IRC bouncers).** A room is a Log stream that carries durable and transient items interwoven (the Third Room amendment), and seats wait on Frontiers. Federation is feed replication of signed events plus a Binding for equivocation, not Matrix's state-resolution DAG, so the class of "state reset" incidents that Matrix operators know is impossible by construction, and one 1 GB Synapse install becomes a few megabytes of rows.
4. **Files and sync (Syncthing, Nextcloud, Tahoe-LAFS).** Files are Names with BLAKE3/Bao outboard trees, directories are Folds over a file-event Log, and device sync is have-range Frontier exchange. Placement across friends' nodes is Tahoe's k-of-n held as custody Obligations. Syncthing users gain history and verifiable restores; Tahoe users gain a system a non-operator can run.
5. **Identity (Keybase sigchains, KERI, did:plc, Nostr keys, Sign-in-with-Google).** A principal is a key-event log: pre-rotated next-key digests, device keys under a threshold, witnesses that co-sign key state, and delegations as grants. Bluesky's PLC directory and Nostr's "lose the nsec, lose yourself" are the two failures it removes: there is no central directory to capture, and recovery is a threshold of your own devices and named friends.
6. **Backups (restic, Borg, Tarsnap, Backblaze).** A backup target is a peer that holds pins under custody Obligations and answers random range challenges against the Name tree, so a proof of retrievability is an ordinary audited operation and not an act of faith. Restore is the same `transport` as migration, so restores get exercised every time anyone moves.
7. **Overlay networking and presence (Tailscale with its coordination server, Headscale, Yggdrasil, ZeroTier).** Signed address records live as ephemeral keys with leases: "the laptop is at these endpoints until 10:40". The device mesh is the principal's own key-event log, and BP carries what the overlay cannot reach. Tailscale users lose the coordination server they never controlled; Yggdrasil users gain identity that is a person and not a node public key.
8. **Social feeds (Secure Scuttlebutt, Nostr relays, Mastodon/ActivityPub).** An author's feed is a Log with a `(signer, previous-head)` Binding, exactly SSB's append-only feed. Relays and pubs are peers with metered admission, and follows are Spans. SSB users get partial replication and deletion-by-retention that SSB's whole-feed model never had; Mastodon admins stop paying for every remote instance's firehose, because inbound federation is charged to the sending server's principal.
9. **Transparency witnessing (Certificate Transparency, Sigsum, Trillian, the Go checksum database, Omniwitness).** Every node co-signs the checkpoint claims of the nodes it peers with, after checking a consistency proof from the last claim it signed. A split view (two claims at one size with different roots) becomes a Binding conflict that is gossiped as evidence. Witnessing becomes something the small web does for itself.
10. **App hosting (Sandstorm grains, Cloudron, YunoHost, Urbit agents).** A grain is a principal with its own commit domain, budget and grants, and Sandstorm's Powerbox becomes the grant-issuing Decide. Apps reach the network only through capabilities the user handed them. Sandstorm users get back the model they loved, with the event history as the backup, the move and the audit log.

## 3. What the kernel must contain

The pruned kernel (Log, Decide, Fold, Name; Binding, Span, Frontier, Obligation) describes *what is computed*. It puts metering, authority and the fault protocol "outside the algebra" (UNIVERSAL §9.1). From this vantage that is backwards. On a hostile internet the first question about any event is *who pays for it and on whose authority*, and a node that cannot answer it inside the algebra cannot prove "clients can't crash us". Four additions are needed: two irreducibles and two protocols. The eight then change in place.

**New irreducible: Meter (principal, account, reservation, settlement).** Principals form a tree: node, then community, person, device, grain or agent, then the anonymous slot. Every resource (pool bytes, journal bytes, payload bytes, connection-seconds, calendar visits, fold-maintenance steps, outbound bandwidth, custody held for others) is a dimension of an account vector. The laws: *reserve before work* (no transition starts without a reservation that covers its cost certificate); *conservation* (charges sum to resources consumed, with shared costs split by a declared rule); *no laundering* (authentication transfers attribution forward and never erases a charge, and disconnecting erases nothing); *protected shares* (no principal can consume another's reserved capacity or scheduling minimum, EXEC rule 5, now a theorem over the Meter and not a property of the scheduler alone); *liability* (a vouch or an invitation puts a bounded Obligation on the voucher's account). Budgets are Folds, but admission is Decide, which reads the Meter.

**New irreducible: Grant (capability with attenuation and revocation).** A grant is a durable record `(issuer, holder key, scope, caveats, budget slice, expiry in a named time domain, revocation handle)`. Scope is a predicate over Spans: these streams, these keys, this projection. Caveats attenuate like Macaroons or Biscuit and delegate like UCAN. Laws: delegation only narrows; a chain is valid iff every link is valid at the decision's position; revocation is a Frontier on the issuer's key-event log or the grant's handle, so "revoked" is a threshold read and never a race. Every Decide's first precondition is a grant chain, and the decision record cites it, so an audit is a Span query. Robigalia's capabilities, Sandstorm's Powerbox, the agent inbox that should not need the operator socket (#14), and akapug's "roles are narrowable delegated rights" are all this one record kind.

**New protocol: Witness (co-signed checkpoints, freshness, split-view evidence).** STORAGE has the typed signed checkpoint claim and names `A-INDEPENDENT-FRONTIER` as an *assumption*. Here it becomes a served protocol. Record kinds: `cosignature(witness, origin history-id, incarnation, count, root, witness-time)`, `consistency-proof request/response`, `split-view evidence`. Laws: a witness signs only after verifying consistency from the last claim it signed for that origin; a node never serves a history position more than a declared distance past its last witnessed checkpoint without saying so; two claims for one `(history-id, incarnation, count)` with different roots are a Binding conflict that every peer accepts as evidence. Recovery gains two states beside `intact / torn tail / damaged / unknown`: `rolled-back (witnessed)` and `repairing (from peers)`. TigerBeetle repairs a corrupt block from replicas. fn2 repairs from custody peers by Name, because a person's other devices and friends are their replica set.

**New protocol: Lineage (key-events, succession, escrow, move).** Record kinds: `key-event(inception | rotation | device-add | device-remove | recovery)` with KERI pre-rotation and thresholds; `succession(history, from-host, to-host)`; `move(old location, new location)`; `heartbeat(operator)`; `escrow(authority, successors, threshold, trigger)`. The dead-man's switch is an Obligation: the operator's heartbeat must be discharged before a deadline in the node's time domain, and if it lapses, an escrowed grant activates for a threshold of named successors, giving them the community's administrative authority (never the person's private correspondence unless sealed for them). The co-op's node survives its admin moving away or dying. No existing system answers that problem.

**The eight, changed:**
- **Log**: every event envelope carries its author key, its payer principal and its grant reference. Then attribution is replayable, and a history can be audited for who caused what.
- **Decide**: authority comes first. A command's precondition is (grant chain valid at head) ∧ (Meter reservation available) ∧ the domain predicate, and the outcome records all three.
- **Fold**: carries a per-event cost certificate charged to the event's payer; maintenance of a projection someone asked for is charged to them, never to the node.
- **Name**: gains the range challenge as a first-class read, `prove(name, range, nonce)`, so custody is checkable.
- **Binding**: becomes the one equivocation detector for every signed chain: feeds, key-events, checkpoint claims, Message-IDs.
- **Span**: reads are pulled back along the reader's grant Span, so visibility is the pullback and not a filter applied afterwards. Private groups, sealed mail and per-grain isolation are one rule.
- **Frontier**: gains *witnessed frontiers*: a position co-signed by k of the declared witnesses, and a read strength `at-least-witnessed(p, k)` next to `exactly-at` and `at-least`.
- **Obligation**: gains holder transfer with receipt (custody moves between nodes during a move), deadline-triggered activation (escrow), and challenge-backed discharge (backup custody).

**Laws the kernel must state:** *hostile-input totality* (every grammar is a bounded machine with no well-formedness premise, EXEC §4); *time is a charged resource* (connection-seconds and parked waits are metered, which is the slowloris answer); *clock hostility* (deadlines that gate authority, such as escrow and grant expiry, are evaluated against a time domain anchored by witness co-signatures carrying time, Roughtime-style, never the box's wall clock alone); *device domains* (each device is its own commit domain, a person's world is a cut across them, and cross-device merges are declared mergeable Folds or Decides at a home domain, never implicit last-writer-wins).

**Admission without CAPTCHAs** is composed from the above, never bolted on. The anonymous slot has a small protected share. Escaping it takes one of: a grant (an invitation is a grant with a vouch liability on the inviter, the Lobsters invite tree with teeth); a Privacy-Pass-style unlinkable token issued by a peer that already admitted you; a payment receipt; or, as the last resort, a memory-hard proof of work priced by the anonymous slot's current saturation. Abuse by an invitee shrinks the inviter's budget through the liability Obligation, so a community's admission policy is its Meter.

## 4. The one capability that would make it epic: the portable witnessed lineage

No existing system gives a person a history that *no host can rewrite, no key loss can end, and no provider can keep*. Certificate Transparency gives the first to CAs and not to people. KERI gives the second to identifiers and not to data. ActivityPub's `Move` and Bluesky's account migration gesture at the third and depend on the old host's cooperation. fn2 can offer all three as one object, the **lineage**: a key-event log, a chain of checkpoint claims over every commit domain the principal owns, and the co-signatures of a witness set the principal chose (their own devices, their friends' nodes, their community's node). The property: *any party that has seen a witnessed checkpoint of the lineage will detect any rollback, fork or impersonation of it, and any holder of a threshold of the lineage's current keys can re-home the entire world, data, grants and obligations, onto any node, with the old host's cooperation optional.*

To start building:
1. Define the checkpoint claim's origin as `(lineage-id, domain-id, incarnation)` and the inception key-event's Name as `lineage-id`.
2. Implement the witness co-sign exchange as a consumer-protocol verb pair over the existing peering connection, with the Sigsum/Omniwitness consistency-proof format adapted to the Merkle event log.
3. Add the `rolled-back (witnessed)` recovery classification and the freshness rule to the simulator's RECOVERY checker, with a scenario that restores a 40-minute-old disk image under one peer.
4. Define `transport` as: a portable export (logs as signed event runs, payloads by Name, key-event log, grants, obligations with their holders), then import as replay or a certified summary checked against the newest witnessed claim, then `succession` committed under the lineage's keys, then `move` delivered to the old host as best effort, with custody Obligations transferred by receipt.
5. The top-level non-vacuity witnesses: an actual rolled-back image that is detected, an actual move without the old host's cooperation that is accepted by a peer that knew the old host, and an actual stolen-device key presented after rotation that is refused.

## 5. What it must never do

It must never let anything a client sends (its bytes, its rate, its slowness, its claimed identity) consume a resource that is not charged to that client's principal, or delay any other principal past its reserved share. It must never hold an authority the person cannot revoke or take with them: no provider-held key, no central directory, no phone-home, no namespace someone else sells. It must never silently serve a shorter or forked history, or call itself durable, witnessed or restorable beyond what evidence on hand shows.
