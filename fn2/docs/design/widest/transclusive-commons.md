# fn2 as the transclusive, attributed, versioned commons (widest-role council, 2026-10-28)

Vantage: Xanadu and the Memex, finally built. fn2 as the commons of documents, data, code, claims and science. Brief: `briefs/WIDEST-ROLE-20261028.md`. Read against FN2-ORIENTATION, EXECUTION-MODEL §1-3, STORAGE-DESIGN §1, PLANES-AND-GAMES §2 and §5.1, the interweaving addendum, UNIVERSAL-CONSTRUCTIONS §0 and §9, and CLIENTS §1-2. Nothing was fetched; systems and papers are cited from memory by name.

## 1. The world from this vantage

Every serious attempt at a shared record built the same three things and lost one of them. Nelson's Xanadu (1960 onward; Udanax Green's *istream* and *vstream*) had permanent content and links that survived edits, but never shipped. The Web shipped, but references are locations, so they rot: Zittrain, Albert and Lessig (2014) found that half the URLs cited in US Supreme Court opinions no longer pointed to what was cited. Git, IPFS and Software Heritage have permanent content, but their references are dead ends. A hash names bytes. It does not say what came next, who relies on them, or whether they are still true. Wikipedia and Wikidata have living, attributed history, but it lives inside one institution's database. Science has neither permanence nor liveness. Data, code and environment drift apart, retractions do not reach the papers that cite them, and "reproducible" is a word in a methods section.

From this vantage fn2 is the substrate that holds all three at once. **Every document is a stream of versions.** A version is a commit position in a Log. Its content is a tree of content-named payloads (Name), the permascroll Xanadu called the istream. A version's text is an arrangement of spans into that permascroll, the vstream. **Every reference is an anchor**: (stream, version position, span under a declared addressing scheme). An anchor never breaks for two reasons. Its target version is retained by an obligation the referrer holds. And every edit event declares how it transports positions, so the anchor can be carried along the history to any later version, with the result typed as *unchanged, moved, edited, split, deleted* or *withdrawn*. **Every link is a signed claim** stored in its author's stream and indexed at both ends. **Every statement of fact** (authorship, license, "this figure was computed from that dataset", "this result reproduced", "this paper is retracted") **is an attested, supersedable claim**, never a global truth. A reader's view is a fold over the claims they choose to trust.

A Tuesday:
- **09:10, a lab in Nairobi.** A postdoc reruns the figure-3 pipeline. Its identity is (pipeline Name, environment Name, the Nix/Guix closure, input dataset Names). The output payload's Name matches the one the original authors claimed. Her node appends a signed `derivation-result` claim. The Binding keyed by the derivation now holds one output with two attestations. Had the output differed, the Binding would hold two outputs and `conflicted` would be true, and the irreproducibility would be a recorded, forwardable fact rather than a rumour. This is the Message-ID conflict machinery (UNIVERSAL §4.3), unchanged.
- **10:30, a drafting office.** A clerk commits an amending act. Its operative clauses ("in section 12(3), for 'thirty days' substitute 'forty-five days'") are edit events on the anchored spans of the principal act's stream. Every commentary, court opinion and agency guideline that transcludes section 12(3) is woken with a typed delta: *edited, effective 2027-01-01 in the valid-time domain*. The consolidated act "as in force on 2026-11-02" is an `exactly-at` read in a declared time domain. That is the job legislation.gov.uk and Akoma Ntoso point-in-time systems do with hand-built pipelines.
- **11:00, an encyclopedia editor.** She reverts vandalism. The revert is an edit event whose transport map is the inverse of the vandal's. Every infobox that transcluded the paragraph re-anchors exactly, with no fuzzy text matching.
- **14:00, a reviewer.** She is discharging an obligation the editor created at submission: `review(r1) ⊗ review(r2) ⊕ desk-reject(editor)`. Her comments are claims anchored to spans of version 3. When the authors post version 4, each comment arrives transported: still attached, moved to section 5, or "the passage you criticised was deleted".
- **16:45, a student with a Memex trail.** Bush's *As We May Think* (1945) imagined the trail as the unit of scholarship. Hers is a stream of anchors with annotations, published as a document and transcluded into a classmate's notes. Every hop shows its provenance chain and every source's current status.

**On the wire:** events (versions as edit batches), Names and range-verifiable payload slices (BLAKE3 outboard trees), signed claims, link announcements, frontier cuts. **Durable:** version streams, payloads, claims, obligations, derivation records. **Live:** presence and carets in co-editing, as transient items in the document's own stream (the addendum's interweaving), and promotion of a draft to a version at "save". **Fast:** nothing here needs a tick loop. Rendering and diffing are folds. **Across node boundaries:** Names, claims, link announcements and anchors, which are portable. Commit positions are not. A remote anchor names the version by (stream identity, version Name), not by the local position, because positions never mix (PLANES L5).

## 2. Ten things it would build or subsume

1. **The wiki: MediaWiki and Wikibase.** Page history is a journal, templates are transclusions by anchor rather than by name-and-hope, and Wikidata's (item, property) statements with ranks and references are a Binding of validated claims whose conflicts are data. Editors switch because blame, revert and template dependency become exact queries over one history, and the encyclopedia can be forked and federated without a dump-and-reimport.
2. **Citation and annotation: Zotero, Crossref cited-by, Hypothesis, W3C Web Annotation.** A citation is an anchor to a version, and an annotation is a claim anchored to a span. Re-anchoring after edits uses declared transport, not TextQuoteSelector's fuzzy matching. Researchers switch because citations stop rotting and cited-by becomes a complete, typed backlink index instead of a crawl.
3. **Reproducible computation: Nix binary-cache trust, Trustix, `guix challenge`, DVC, Code Ocean.** A derivation is a Name over (function, environment, inputs), its output is a Binding, and independent rebuilders append attestations. Trustix's "log of build results compared across builders" becomes a native Binding with peer convergence. Scientists switch because "reproduced by three labs" becomes a checkable fact attached to the figure, not a sentence.
4. **Peer review: OpenReview, ScholarOne, Publons.** Submission creates an evidence-formula obligation. Reviews, rebuttals and decisions are claims anchored to the reviewed version, and credit for reviewing is the discharge record. Editors switch because the queue is a work queue with leases and the review history survives the journal that ran it.
5. **Preprints and the scientific record: arXiv versions, Crossref Crossmark, Retraction Watch.** A paper is a version stream, and a retraction or erratum is a superseding claim that wakes every transcluder through the backlink Span. Retracted work is still cited thousands of times because nobody downstream is told. Here somebody is told, by construction.
6. **Law and standards: legislation.gov.uk, Akoma Ntoso/LegalDocML, the US Code's OLRC, KeyCite and Shepard's, IETF RFCs with errata.** Amendments are edit events on anchored spans, consolidations are folds, point-in-time is an exactly-at read in a valid-time domain, and a citator's "negative treatment" is a typed backlink claim. Lawyers switch because "is this still good law" is a frontier read, not a subscription to a vendor.
7. **Archives: the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine, Perma.cc, Software Heritage.** A capture is a projection with declared loss (PLANES L2, L4): a WARC records what it fetched, and its loss declaration records what it could not (scripts, embeds, later states). Software Heritage's SWHIDs are Names under a second algorithm, bound to BLAKE3 Names by validated equivalence claims. Archivists switch because the archive stops being a parallel universe and becomes the retention obligation that keeps everyone else's anchors alive.
8. **The code commons: Unison, crates.io/npm, Software Heritage's graph.** As in Unison, definitions are Names, human names are a Binding (renames are free, conflicts are visible), and a registry is a fold over signed publish claims with dependents as a backlink Span. Maintainers switch because "who depends on this exact function, and did my change break their pinned behaviour" is a span query plus a derivation rerun.
9. **Versioned data: Dolt, Datomic, Quilt, CSV-on-GitHub.** A table is a fold over row events, and prolly-tree pages (Dolt, Noms) are Names, so diff, merge and branch are structural. A dataset release is a version a derivation can name. Data teams switch because the dataset, the query that produced the paper's numbers and the paper itself share one identity space.
10. **Claims and the semantic web: RDF/SPARQL, nanopublications (Groth, Gibson and Velterop 2010), Solid pods, Perkeep's permanodes.** Every triple is a signed claim with issuer, evidence and supersession, as nanopublications wanted and Perkeep's "permanode plus signed mutation claims" built. A knowledge base is a fold under a declared trust policy. Ontologists switch because provenance and disagreement are first-class, so they stop shipping "the" graph and ship views.

## 3. What the kernel must contain

The pruned eight are the right bones, but four are too small for the commons, and two record kinds are missing.

**Name must become plural and structural.** A Name today is one algorithm over a payload. The commons needs (a) *algorithm-tagged* Names (multihash: BLAKE3, SHA-256, git SHA-1 for SWHIDs, IPFS CIDv1); (b) **name-equivalence as a Binding**, `Name_a ≡ Name_b`, validated by rehashing and portable as a claim; and (c) **Names of structured values**: trees with a declared shape (IPLD's data model, Dolt's prolly trees, Nix store paths), so that a version, a table and a derivation have Names whose children are Names. The catamorphism framing of UNIVERSAL §4.2 already allows this. It has to become a DSL form, `defshape`.

**Log must make streams cheap, forkable and many.** Every document, page and dataset is a stream, so there are billions. Stream creation is an event, not configuration. **Fork** is a first-class relation: a stream whose prefix *is* another stream's prefix up to position p, shared and not copied. This is the versioned executable world's `fork`, and git's branch done with identity. **Merge** is a Decide that names both parents. A version's identity across nodes is the Name of (parent version Names, edit batch), a Merkle clock per document (UNIVERSAL §4.4: head-sets across open peering).

**The missing primitive: Anchor, with Transport.** UNIVERSAL's *Span* is a relation (an index). The commons also needs the Xanadu span: a **position region inside a version**, plus its law of motion. Concretely:
- An **addressing scheme** per content type, declared in a DSL form `defaddressing`: byte ranges, Unicode code-point ranges, structural paths (NLS's hierarchical addresses and Eugene Eric Kim's purple numbers), table (row-key, column), AST node identity for code, time ranges for media.
- A **transport map per edit-event kind**: a monotone partial map from positions in the version before to positions in the version after. A range maps through the Galois pair: lower adjoint for "the largest surviving sub-range", upper adjoint for "the smallest covering range". It comes with a typed outcome: `unchanged | moved | edited | split | deleted`.
- **The transport law:** transport is functorial along the Log, `T(e₁·e₂) = T(e₂) ∘ T(e₁)`, and identity on the empty word. The summary equation (a Fold's action law) therefore gives fast-forward over many versions for free. A composed transport can be memoized at checkpoints, the same way the outboard tree memoizes the hash catamorphism. This is ember's "transport along equivalences" made literal: anchors are transported along the edit history, and declared equivalences (whitespace normalization, reformatting, a rename in Unison) are edit kinds whose transport is total and outcome-`unchanged`.
- Operational-transform and CRDT systems (Jupiter, Google Wave, Peritext, Automerge's cursors) each have private versions of this. fn2 makes it a declared, checkable, reusable law, because one Decide order exists per document (UNIVERSAL §9.3's "no CRDT metadata where a server order exists").

**Claim must become a kernel record kind, not an application payload.** The record is `(issuer, subject: Anchor | Name | stream, predicate Name, object, evidence: [Anchor | Name | claim], valid-time interval, supersedes: [claim])`, signed, with the issuer's previous-head chain. Its laws:
- Supersession is a relation, not deletion: the superseded claim stays.
- The current view is a **Fold parameterized by a trust policy** (which issuers, which predicates, how to rank conflicts). The policy has its own Name, so "the view" is reproducible.
- Disagreement between issuers is a Binding conflict, never an overwrite.
- Valid time is separate from commit position, making the record bitemporal as in XTDB and Datomic's as-of/since. This extends PLANES L6 time domains to *declared* historical time, which law and science both need.

**Obligation must reach across nodes and travel along references.** Three extensions:
1. **Evidence predicates over remote claims and over anchors**: `exists anchor in derived-version citing source-span` discharges an attribution obligation, and `review-claim(issuer ∈ assigned-reviewers, subject = v3)` discharges a review slot.
2. **Inherited obligations**: a license claim on a Name (CC-BY, CC-BY-SA, a "transcopyright" grant in Nelson's sense, minus the micropayment) attaches an obligation to every transclusion of that Name. Deriving a version that transcludes it creates `attributed(span) ⊗ share-alike(license)` in the deriving stream, checked by Decide at commit. Permission comes from publication, as Xanadu rule 7 has it. Payment is replaced by an attribution the system enforces and can always display.
3. **Retention by referrers**: every transclusion and pinned citation holds a retention obligation on the target version's payload spans, discharged only by the referrer's own supersession, an archive's custody claim (BP custody, already product), or an explicit declared-loss event. "Links never break" is the RECLAIM checker's safety property applied to the commons.

**Span must be federated, with completeness.** Backlinks are the inverse Span of link claims. Across nodes they are assembled from link announcements carried by peering. Each node's backlink index publishes a Frontier of the form "complete through these peers' cuts", so "who cites this?" answers `at-least` with a stated coverage instead of Google Scholar's silent incompleteness (UNIVERSAL §2.4's two read strengths, applied to the world).

**Two new record kinds:**
- **`derivation`**: `(function Name, environment Name, input Names, parameters) → output Name`, attested by an executor. Its Binding is keyed by the derivation Name, and re-execution is a protocol on that record. It is the projection declaration (`defprojection`) promoted to a portable, content-named, re-runnable fact, so that a Fold *itself* has a Name and its result is memoized across the world.
- **`declared-loss`**: a projection's or archive's statement of what it does not carry (fields dropped, formats lossy, crawl depth, embargoed spans, erased payloads with their Names retained). This makes PLANES L2 and L4 records rather than prose. Erasure for legal reasons or the right to be forgotten is a withdrawal claim plus a declared loss: the Name and the fact of withdrawal stay, the bytes go, and every anchor into them resolves to `withdrawn(reason, claim)`.

**DSL forms the commons needs:** `defshape` (structured Names), `defaddressing` and `defedit` (each edit kind with its transport proof obligation: monotone, composes), `defclaim-kind` (predicate, subject sort, evidence formula, who may issue), `deftrust-policy` (a Fold over claims), `defderivation`, `deflicense` (the inherited obligation it creates).

## 4. The one capability that would make it epic: consequence propagation

No existing system can tell you, when a fact changes, everything that was built on it. fn2 can, because it would hold all three ingredients in one identity space: anchors with transport, backlink Spans with completeness frontiers, and derivation records. The capability is a **standing dependency query**. Any party (a reader, an author, a journal, a court, a CI system) registers interest in the *consequence cone* of anything it relies on, and is woken with a typed account whenever something upstream moves: the cited span was edited, the dataset got an erratum, the theorem's lemma was retracted, the statute was amended with effect from a date, the figure's derivation stopped reproducing, the ontology term was superseded.

The cone is the transitive composite of backlink Spans (citations, transclusions, derivation inputs, claim evidence), evaluated incrementally like DBSP or differential dataflow over the Log. Each edge carries its transported anchor, so the wake says not just "something changed" but "the three sentences you quoted were reworded, here is the delta, your quotation is now stale". Through derivation records, a change to an input Name marks every downstream output `unverified-under-new-input` and can schedule re-execution as a work-queue obligation.

**What a builder starts with:**
1. The anchor record and `defedit` for one addressing scheme (UTF-8 code-point ranges) with transport, tested by simulation against random edit histories: transport composed over a history equals transport over the squashed history.
2. Link claims plus the local backlink Span.
3. A cone query as a Span composite with a Frontier.
4. The wake path already designed in INBOX §4: edges, a hint wake, and the final frontier check.

The first demonstration is a two-node scene. A paper on node A transcludes a paragraph and a figure from a preprint on node B. B posts version 2, rewording the paragraph and retracting the figure's dataset. A's author is woken with `edited(delta)` and `input-retracted(derivation, claim)`, while A's published version keeps rendering the original, retained by obligation and marked superseded upstream. Retraction Watch, citators, Dependabot, `guix challenge` and Wikipedia's "citation needed" bots are all partial, private, polling approximations of this one query.

## 5. What it must never do

fn2 must never decide what is true: it records who claimed what, on what evidence, superseding what, and every "current view" is a named, reproducible fold under a trust policy someone chose. It must never let a reference change meaning silently: every anchor resolves to its original bytes or to a typed outcome (moved, edited, deleted, withdrawn, lost-with-declaration), and no projection, archive or erasure may present a substitute as the original. It must never make citation, transclusion or linking depend on the target's permission or payment, and never let attribution be stripped in transit, because the commons is held together by publication granting the right to reference and by provenance travelling with every byte.
