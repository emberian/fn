# Addendum to PLANES-AND-GAMES: durability interwoven per item, the Third Room lesson (the coordinator, 2026-10-28)

Ember, after reading the study: "Third Room ... was trying to build a vrchat / mmo engine / type of metaverse thing on top of Matrix. and hybrid transient/durable things where transience and durability interweave with each other per intended user experience as opposed to be fixed rigid aspects of the system architecture."

## What Third Room was (thirdroom2023-readme-matrix-virtual-worlds.md in ~/paperbin; HN 33013700, 2022-09-28, Robert Long and Matthew Hodgson in the thread)
Three developers at Element, February 2022 to 2023. A Matrix room is a world. The durable half is Matrix: the glTF scene and avatars in the homeserver's media repository, identity, membership, permissions, text chat and end-to-end encryption in the room's event DAG, a virtual-worlds spec (MSC3815) for the room state. The transient half is WebRTC: spatial voice and the game networking (positions, poses) over data channels, negotiated through the Matrix group-VoIP signalling (MSC3401), full mesh at first, a cascading SFU planned. The engine (Manifold) is bitECS plus three.js with WASM scripting, "no infrastructure beyond a normal Matrix homeserver" plus TURN. Element stopped funding it in 2023.

The architecture is two systems glued by signalling: durable facts in one (the DAG, with Matrix's ordering, identity and auth), transient facts in another (WebRTC, with its own session identity, no ordering across peers, nothing surviving a reconnect), and the client doing the gluing by hand. An object you drag across the room is positions over WebRTC until someone decides it is placed and writes a room state event. That decision is a UX decision, and it had to cross two systems with different identities, different orderings and no shared position. That seam is what killed the experience, as much as the funding.

## What the study got right, and where it is still rigid
The study's four planes and six laws stand: fn2 is the only place a durable fact exists, the fast plane is never inside it, loss is typed, positions never mix. Its live plane is inside the same executor on the same subscription edges, which is exactly the answer to Third Room's two-system seam. But it puts the durability class on the STREAM (§4.1: "the stream-id's kind tag gains a class: durable | live | ephemeral; every 5.1.1 stream is durable"). That is a fixed tier by another name. Ember's point is that the choice belongs to the item and the experience, and the same stream carries both.

## The amendment (replaces the study's §4.1 first bullet and decision 2's shape)
1. **Durability is a property of the item, chosen at publish.** Every item published to a stream carries a class, `durable | transient`, chosen by its author within what the stream admits. A stream declares the classes it admits and its default (a room admits both, default durable; a telemetry stream admits transient only; the journal of a commit domain admits durable only). The class is authorized like any other write: a principal's budget for transient bytes and rate is separate from its journal budget (EXECUTION-MODEL rule 5).
2. **One stream, one order, both classes.** A subscriber of a stream sees its durable and transient items in one delivered order. Durable items carry their commit position; transient items carry `(epoch, seq)` and the happened-after commit position at publication (the study's stamp), and never move the durable cursor (law L5). So a room's chat line, its dragged object's trajectory, a wave gesture and the placed object's final position arrive to a client as one sequence, from one subscription, under one identity and one auth, with the loss of the transient ones typed (`live-overrun`, `epoch-reset`) and the durable ones never lost.
3. **Promotion is an ordinary commit that references transient items.** "Placed" is a durable event whose payload may carry the trajectory's digest or the trajectory itself; "pen-up" commits the stroke that was transient while drawn; a moderation policy may commit a transient emote as evidence; a fast executor's checkpoint commits what its transient positions amounted to. Nothing is promoted by the node on its own; the experience says when a transient fact starts to matter. Demotion is the lease on an ephemeral key (presence expires; a position stream's items age out of the ring).
4. **Projections may fold either class.** "Who is here" folds transient presence; "what was built" folds durable placements only; a projection declares which classes it reads, and a transient-fed projection is itself rebuildable from nothing plus a declared loss (law L2).
5. **The fast plane publishes into the same streams.** A tick executor publishes transient positions at its chosen rate into the room's stream (admitted class transient, charged to the executor's principal) and durable consequences as transactions; clients need no second connection, which is the single-system answer to Third Room's WebRTC-plus-Matrix seam. Media (voice, video) stays outside, as Discord's SFU shows; its control state and the room's membership are fn2's.

What this costs in 5.1.1: the class moves from the stream kind to the item envelope (one field), the stream kind gains an admitted-class set and a default, the ring and the ephemeral key table are as the study specified, and the consumer wire's items already carry their class in the study's §4.1. The reference model and the two simulator scenarios are unchanged in substance. Nothing is served transient in 5.1.1 unless ember rules otherwise.

## The decision for ember
Adopt the amendment: durability as a per-item class within a stream that declares what it admits, one order for both classes, promotion by an ordinary referencing commit, projections choosing their classes, the fast plane publishing into the same streams. Recommended yes; it is the study's decision 2 with the rigidity removed, and it is what a Third Room on fn2 would need to be one system.
