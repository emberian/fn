## 1. What fn2 is, and why it exists

fn is ember's NNTP server written in ACL2 (a theorem prover whose logic is a pure first-order Lisp with executable definitions), with native extensions through SBCL. It served a small public node (6.6.0 since 2026-09-28) and was meant to become "the thing a world is kept in": the storage and delivery substrate under ember's other systems, in particular **minidregg** (a formal-first reimplementation of a coordination fabric) and **helm** (a fleet-coordination tool whose rooms, posts and claims need a durable, ordered, multi-reader log with wake-on-commit). The target consumers are therefore not human newsreaders but programs: an agent fleet of about thirty seats posting several thousand rows a day and sleeping until woken; a processing transaction (Mini) that reads a report, computes, appends a result exactly once, and acknowledges.

fn ("fn1") grew to about 2,000 ACL2 books with hand-written machine arms, three places that tested the same thing in three ways, a landing cycle per change, and designs that outran the code. On 2026-10-27 ember froze it and started **fn2** as a rebuild "bottom-up, semantics-first, with metaprogramming that expresses the whole thing succinctly." The laws that bind it, in ember's words from the design record: the derived path is the only path (nothing served is a hand arm; a feature that cannot be declared is a missing word in a generator, never a hand body); statement-first (every keystone enters as an owed theorem with executable teeth before proof work); assert, never absorb (an impossible state stops the core with a named reason; nothing is silently handled); every book certifies under a minute on the quiet box; performance is published, never a gate; names say what the wire does (no "ledger" vocabulary over a protocol fact).

### 2.2 The execution model

One `fn2-core` holds every child table. An executor (`fn2-turn`) walks a calendar of principal slots and service slots; each visit runs one machine row for one bounded run (4 KiB of bytes, 32 steps, 4 descriptors by default), which may issue operations (Receive, Send, storage reads and writes, a fence, Accept, Close) to an outbox; an adapter (the simulator, or the native host loop) completes them and posts results; a grant service wakes rows parked on resource kinds. Machines are rows of their kind's table; a parked row's FIFO links and wait reason live in a directory indexed by global id (since P1), so a park or a grant never writes another kind's table. The resource order makes every acquisition ascending in rank; the no-cycle argument is a proved theorem; the keystone that the real core's wait projection is always ranked is owed with teeth.

Layer B's durability: a block-device crash model stated as an allowed-outcome predicate (torn writes, misdirected writes, lying fences, at-rest corruption under a profile of assumption bits), an abstract log model, a commit batch of up to sixteen members sealed into 4 KiB journal frames with a BLAKE3 Merkle structure, recovery that classifies the arena as intact / torn-tail / damaged / unknown and installs only the first two, and a typed-edge allocator. Outcomes to clients are exactly three: accepted (after publish), refused (before the first journal write), uncertain (after it and before durability is known), and the uncertain word reaches the NNTP client as "441 ... do not repost (uncertain)".

Layer C: keyed ordered schemas with declared merge rules, folds (group counts, the consumer cursor), writes staged into a private successor overlay that publication exposes atomically, overlays sealed into runs by a sealer machine, range reads and a whole-schema scan with staleness re-checked at every step, a published frontier position, and (landing today) the identity records of the cross-peer identity ruling: evidence and history as union-inserted records, a candidate index and a summary fold, a decision that is two exact lookups, and a reply decided from the facts the decision returns (selection, evidence status, coverage), never from its verdict alone.

Layer D: the connection machine (grammar-driven, 4 KiB per run, pipelining by construction, fragmentation invariance stated and tested at every split point), the upload machine (a streaming dot-unstuffing feed that hashes as it goes), the transaction machine (identity claim, C's decision, the batch member, the three outcomes), the query cursor (landed today, the first kind written directly in the new form), the ledger and the scheduler over principals.

Layer E: the consumer port (ten verbs: register, rebase, unregister, read, wait, ack, position, status, fetch, append; bound to the account; three carriers: the owner's local socket with fn1's codes translated, a framed `XFNC` request on the authenticated NNTP connection, plain NNTP where append is POST and fetch is ARTICLE), the inbox as a selection over streams, Grant then Meter as every verb's first preconditions, nine keystones owed with teeth, Mini's twelve crash cuts and helm's rooms as fixtures passing in the REPL against a reference model, and a peering/BP design in wire words (a peer is a principal with a grant; an outbound feed is a node-owned consumer; a bundle is a carrier; custody is the forward pin released by the application receipt). Nothing of E serves yet.

Layer H: an independent Python model of NNTP (written from the RFCs, used as the oracle for the grammar's fuzzer and the served replies), a harness that runs families of fixtures against the simulated node and the real socket, and a nightly on the laptop whose one-line summary opens with the served surface.

## 9. Doubts and questions (the real request)

**9.1 Is the generator now the project's trusted computing base, and is that acceptable?** Every served arm is emitted by `machine-arms.lisp` (about 900 lines of ACL2 macro code) from declarations. Its correctness is checked by: run-time certificates on each generated function, two probe kinds whose generated and hand forms agreed, the Lean interpreter agreeing with the generated functions on 963 recorded calls, and a mutation discipline. It is not proved. Is this the right place for the trust to sit, and is the Lean measure plus PW3 (generator correctness per arm, stated, unproved) the right way to discharge it? What would you want to see before calling a generated kind trustworthy?

**9.2 Proof debt.** 873 owed rows, perhaps ten proved. The project's assurance claim today is "every keystone has executable positive and negative witnesses, and the system refuses to absorb." Is a project whose theorems are owed an honest "high assurance" project, or a well-tested one with a theorem-shaped TODO list? We have no concrete plan to discharge owed rows beyond "the generator should make most of them true by construction." Which rows would you insist be proved first, and which can stay as teeth forever?

**9.3 ACL2 proof cost as a scaling law.** The cursor book went 1,339 → 501 → 90 seconds through generator changes alone; the executor sits at 73 s and resisted the same move; the old-form kinds pay 50-800 s until ported. The per-child/per-kind quadratic is fixed; what remains is per-function guard proofs over stobj-let bodies. Is "every book under a minute" the right target, or should the generator aim for proofs by construction (no per-arm theorem at all, a single generic theorem over the step language's interpreter), which is what the Lean side already has? Is ACL2 the right tool for the frame at all, or only for the instances (the current division of labour, `LEAN-METATHEORY.md` §1)?

**9.4 The Lean pathway.** Today: ACL2 is the executable truth; Lean reads it back through a 758-function shallow embedding with 35 native primitives (eight proved against ACL2's definitions) and measures agreement on recorded runs. Proposed (§9 of that document, ruled yes by main today as decisions 6 and 7): prove the generator correct per arm in Lean, then prove a typed refinement, then lower to C from Lean with memory safety and bounded termination, replacing ATC's C-from-ACL2. The risk I see: two semantics and a bridge (the reader) that is checked by agreement, not proved; a bug in the reader is invisible exactly where both sides agree. Is the independent expression reader plus mutation discipline enough? Would you instead make Lean the primary semantics and generate ACL2 from it, or abandon ACL2's role in the frame entirely once the Lean interpreter exists?

**9.5 The cross-peer identity rulings.** Fourteen rulings (IDENTITY-ACROSS-PEERS-20261030.md): the Message-ID is a name whose binding to content is per node; a peer's acceptance is evidence here; a conflicting body commits its evidence and refuses the article in the same transaction; the reply is decided from selection, evidence status and coverage, never a verdict alone; a bounded number of candidate contents per name; local acceptance serialized by a claim per Message-ID with a stated stability property (claim stability: for an immutable offer, while its claim is held and before its own publication, every validated decision agrees). Please attack claim stability and the lost-acceptance race (an ack whose condition fails now leaves its slot unchanged instead of refusing the whole stage, `:unless-when :unchanged`). Is "evidence never decides what is served" the right invariant for a system that also wants peers to converge?

**9.6 The three outcomes and the uncertain word.** A client may receive "441 ... uncertain; do not repost" when the journal write was issued and durability is unknown; accepted is reported only after publication. For a program consumer this is exactly right (Mini's cuts c2/c3 depend on it); for an NNTP client it is unusual. The retry identity on NNTP is the Message-ID (a generated one is a new article); on the consumer port it is (principal, op id) with a retry table and an expiry. Do you see a hole in the exactly-once story across a crash between the journal write and publication, given recovery may replay a durable-but-unpublished member?

**9.7 The resource order and the ledger.** A global acquisition order of 24 kinds, bundles that acquire ascending, protected reserves for close/cancel/fence/wake/recovery/system, and a ledger with per-principal limits and reserves where committed = Σ max(hold, reserve) ≤ capacity. Deadlock freedom is proved for the rank argument; the keystone that the real core's wait graph is ranked is owed. Is this machinery proportionate? It adds a rank and a dimension to every new resource (E's three holding dimensions, the peer credit, each renumbered the order today). Is there a simpler discipline with the same guarantee?

**9.8 The executor's quantum.** 4 KiB per run, 32 steps, one outbox; B measured that device-speed hashing would need 262k yields per second. "Performance is published, never a gate" is the law, and ember separately wants warm work per action around a millisecond and never slower over time. Are we building a design that cannot reach that, and would know only after M2?

**9.9 The consumer port's choices.** The remote carrier is a length-framed request on the authenticated NNTP connection rather than a second TLS port (Astra disagreed: no head-of-line blocking behind a parked wait). Wait-is-read (K4) with a separate no-lost-wake property (K4b). The inbox as a selection over streams with per-edge starts, mentions as one edge on the principal's address stream, a withdrawal as a positioned entry in the target's streams. Retry by (principal, op id) with the semantic result stored or referenced. Which of these would you change before the first consumer talks to it?

**9.10 Peering and BP.** A peer is a principal with a grant, rechecked at every offer and transfer; an outbound feed is a node-owned consumer that installs delivery work and acknowledges; CHECK/IHAVE suppress only on local acceptance history; TAKETHIS's temporary failure defaults to RFC 4644's 400-and-close with fn1's 436 as a named compatibility profile; BP has one store, the bundle as a carrier and the forward pin as the obligation. Five record kinds were deleted because no verb wrote them. Is "a peer is a principal" the right identity model, and is a feed-as-consumer going to meet INN-style peers' expectations?

**9.11 The process itself.** Six Opus seats and a coordinator, each seat turning over about once a day, hundreds of millions of tokens a day, a dozen seam messages per landing. The parallelism lesson (§3) was learned by measurement today; the fix was "spine only" and "no design ahead of use." What is the right number of concurrent seats for a project whose critical path is one generator step at a time? Is main the bottleneck, and should main be reading more code and routing less? Is statement-first-then-build-same-day the right cadence, or is the Astra read a ritual?

**9.12 The measure of progress.** We count verbs leaving the interim table and the two targets' agreement with an independent model. Is that the right number, or does it bias toward the NNTP surface when the product is the consumer port? What would you measure?

**9.13 What we have not thought about.** Authority and credentials are a layer-E-2 quest with only a pinned access rule today; operator reconfiguration (policy set) is design; multi-domain and re-genesis (minidregg's re-genesis on a homelab box) are rulings without code; the public node migration from fn1 is unplanned. Where is the unexamined assumption that will cost a week?
