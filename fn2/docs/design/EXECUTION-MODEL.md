# fn2 execution model: the decision (round 3, 2026-10-28; present state 2026-10-31, rewritten whole)

Settled between the coordinator (Fable) and Codex (Astra) in three rounds: `EXECUTION-MODEL-ROUND1.md` (the coordinator's opening: "the batch is the step"), `EXECUTION-MODEL-ROUND1-ASTRA.md` (the rebuttal and counter-model), `EXECUTION-MODEL-ROUND2.md` (concessions and seven open items), `EXECUTION-MODEL-ROUND2-ASTRA.md` (the specification). This file is the decision; the rounds hold the arguments and the detail. Where the two disagreed, the winning argument is named. Ember's words that bind it are in `FN2-ORIENTATION.md` and `EMBER-ARCHITECTURE-WORDS.md`; ember's open question of 10-28 ("is **this** why we're fundamentally bottlenecked ... tigerstyle") is answered by §2's fourth bullet and rules 1, 3 and 6. The machine declarations that realize rules 1-3 are MACHINE-FORM-20261030.md (binding); where the tree does not yet implement a rule, the rule says owed.

## 1. The model, in one paragraph
fn2 is **one semantic executor over a deterministic cooperative network of bounded, resumable machines**, implemented as mutation in place of a fixed struct-of-arrays state with one byte pool, with batching inside the implementation and bounded asynchronous adapters (network, storage, TLS, clock, entropy, control) outside it. The semantic boundary encloses parsing, framing, connection state, scheduling, admission, buffer ownership and resource accounting, authentication and authorization policy, the built-in web interface, as well as the store, indexes, persistence, recovery, consumers, peering and BP. The core manipulates connection capabilities and pool ranges, never descriptors or kernel objects. The reference semantics takes one transition at a time under a specified scheduler; the executable turn is a bounded loop of those transitions, later fused, with the obligation that it implements an allowed reference schedule. Partitioning into several executors stays possible (transferable handles, explicit message boundaries) and is not built.

## 2. What was decided against, and why
- **"Already-validated commands" as the core's input** (round 1 proposal): rejected. Validity depends on evolving shared state (identity reservations, admission), so two commands validated against the same old state could both acquire the same absent identity; and a parser outside the core is the largest hole in "clients cannot crash us". (Astra, round 1 §1.)
- **"The batch is the step"**: rejected as a slogan. Three batches exist and none defines another's lifetime: the harvest batch (completions observed), the execution turn (bounded work across runnable machines), the commit batch (records sharing a durability barrier). A 16 MiB BODY requires at least 256 payload quanta at 64 KiB, or 4,096 at the initial 4 KiB execution quantum, plus framing and partial-I/O work; a TAKETHIS window contains independent record lifecycles; XOVER traverses an indexed, pinned selection. (Astra, round 1 §1; corrected in the review.)
- **A pipeline of per-core executors** (round 1 model C): deferred. Crossposts, global identity, account budgets, configuration generations and retention are not partitioned by connection; the protocols would be built before a demonstrated need. The envelope projects a CPU ceiling near 67k POSTs/s under its assumed per-POST cost; the composed implementation must establish its actual ceiling (ENVELOPE §1).
- **The pure step as the cause of fn's slowness**: rejected as a diagnosis. The measured costs were conversions, reconstruction and work under the owner mutex; functional semantics does not require copying state, consing effect lists, or finishing a request before yielding. (Astra, round 1 opening; REPRESENTATION-HISTORY §§2-4.)

## 3. The ten rules
1. **One executor, one semantic state.** `fn2-core`: a top-level stobj assembled from registered children: a table per machine kind, principal accounts, scheduling state, pool metadata and the byte array, cache metadata, outstanding operations, bounded event/action storage, persistent roots and recovery state. Each table is a struct of arrays; a machine is a row `(kind, slot, generation)`. Nested stobjs are modular access, not the logical decomposition: under MACHINE-FORM's placement (P1-P4) an arm runs over its own kind's table and the children its declared operations take, never over the whole core; the row fields generic code writes in other rows (the FIFO links and wait reason) live in the machine directory; the assembly is a registry that grows by one wrapper and one rewrite per kind. Kinds in the tree: listener, echo, scribe, connection, upload, transaction, commit batch, recovery, sealer; owed: cursor, wait, TLS admission, maintenance (checkpoint, cleaner). P1-P4 are owed (G1 and the placement probe are built). An abstract interface sits over the foundation where it simplifies invariants; its logical views (maps, sequences, octet lists) are never materialized on the served path.
2. **Three functions.** `fn2-transition` (one enabled transition: consume one observation, run one bounded continuation, or one scheduler/service transition), `fn2-reference-turn` (select and execute transitions under the scheduler until budgets are exhausted or nothing is eligible), `fn2-turn` (the host-called concrete loop, initially a plain bounded loop over concrete transitions, fused later). Never a second server to obtain vectorization.
3. **Bounded everything, simultaneously.** Every machine run has a work cap (initially 4 KiB parsed/rendered/hashed, 32 structural steps, 4 action descriptors; round 2 §B.3), every turn has `H = 128` observations, `K = 64` calendar visits, `Aturn = 256` descriptors; every queue has a capacity with a named exhaustion behaviour that is an ordinary continuation, never a crash and never an irreversible transition with nowhere to record its effect. Budget exhaustion leaves a resumable continuation. The caps are loop bounds; a cost certificate per run makes them a CPU bound.
4. **The scheduler is part of the semantics.** Intrusive FIFO runnable lists per principal; a fixed calendar (initially 80 entries: 64 principal slots interleaved with four protected service classes: commit/publication, timers and grants, subscription notification, maintenance/recovery); one bounded run per visit; newly runnable machines enter at the tail; repeated wakes do not advance a queued machine; all unauthenticated connections share one anonymous principal slot. Starvation bound: a continuously eligible principal is visited within `L` visits; with `m` runnable machines, each within `L·m`. Wall time never selects a machine. The executor waits on the outside only when no eligible internal work remains.
5. **Isolation, stated precisely.** One principal cannot consume another admitted principal's reserved capacity or minimum scheduling share; best-effort capacity can run out; more connections manufacture no entitlement. Reserve before issuing work; settle on completion; disconnecting erases no charge; authentication transfers attribution without laundering; shared costs (padding, flushes, cache occupancy, cleaning) have an explicit allocation rule; a system reserve covers recovery and control.
6. **One byte pool, core authority, host physical access.** The host allocates and registers the region at start; the core owns allocation policy, ownership metadata, generations, leases and accounting. A handle is `(slot, generation, offset, length)`. Receives and reads lend a writable range the core cannot inspect until completion returns it; sends and writes lend immutable bytes whose reuse waits for every external borrow to end (zero-copy send progress is not release). Persistent references name extents and blocks, never process addresses. Bounded copies within the representation (alignment, TLS, header insertion, packing) are allowed; the octet list exists only in the logic.
7. **Events and actions are a fixed vocabulary with reserved capacity** (round 2 §D): every operation reserves its slot, its action descriptor, its physical window and its completion storage before submission; single-shot accepts, receives, reads and sends in the first slice (a short send terminates the submission; the remainder is a new action); internal notifications (`Run`, `PageReady`, `TransactionOutcome`, `ResourceAvailable`, `SubscriptionDirty`) are state bits or reserved links, never a message queue. `Commit`, `AcceptPost`, `WakeConsumer`, `GrantBuffer`, `RefuseCommand` are not host primitives: they are core decisions.
8. **Durability is a protocol with recovery states, not a checksum loop** (round 2 §E). One ordered commit stream per storage domain; two batch slots (prepare beside in-flight; never publish past an unresolved predecessor); the commit machine `collect → seal → dependencies → log-write → fence → durable → install → publish → notify → draining`, with `uncertain` as a persistent state that only recovery resolves (a timeout is not a refusal; "refused, nothing stored" is not a safe outcome once persistence is ambiguous). Barriers per backend: the baseline completes all dependency and batch/log writes before a covering durability barrier; an alternative completes preceding writes before a sealing write with the specified preflush-plus-FUA contract, or establishes every dependency's durability separately; FUA on the final sector alone suffices only when every preceding dependency is already durable. One bounded transition installs the complete committed root/frontier; replies and wake fan-out proceed incrementally afterward and do not gate visibility. Recovery classifies `intact / torn tail / damaged / unknown` by evidence under the declared fault profile and never silently chooses a shorter history. Three named assumptions: `A-PERSISTENCE-FENCE`, `A-INTEGRITY-EVIDENCE`, `A-INDEPENDENT-FRONTIER` (rollback is detectable only with a surviving independent witness; four local superblocks are not one). Cleaning and checkpoints go through the same commit protocol; "never rewrite an acknowledged sector" means never overwrite a still-required sector.
9. **Persistent namespaces are storage abstractions, not resident arrays** (round 2 §G). Articles, groups, consumers and history are paged through bounded caches; resident capacity limits active execution state (connections, uploads, cursors, parked waits, subscription edges), never how many objects the store retains; data limits are the operator's profile (D27), with explicit exhaustion transitions. A full rebuild of the Message-ID index at open remains a repair path, not the only path. Structural capacities are fixed for process life; policy limits change live within them.
10. **Deterministic simulation is the primary gate from day one.** The same concrete core runs under a simulated host (the same primitive set) and the native host; the simulator records profile, seed, observation order, fault choices and revision; a tiny profile for exhaustive bounded schedules and death cuts, the concrete profile for composition; eleven checkers (TRACE, WORK, OWN, ACCOUNT, FAIR, HISTORY, IDENTITY, WIRE, CONSUMER, RECOVERY, RECLAIM; all but FAIR and CONSUMER exist, with DEATH, DEADLOCK and ENVELOPE beside them) and twenty acceptance scenarios (round 2 §F), the natives as the classical gate beside it. Liveness is checked only under a declared bounded-completion environment. Performance is reported beside the profile; the envelope's arithmetic is a hypothesis to measure, never a success criterion.

## 4. The refinement obligation (for when assurance comes; not a gate now)
`R(C,S) ∧ Inv(S) ∧ BoundaryCompatible(E) ⇒` for `(C', A, status) = fn2-turn(C, E)` there is a finite transition sequence σ following the specified scheduler and observation order with `reference-run(S, E, σ) = (S', Aref, statusref)`, `R(C', S')`, `Inv(S')`, `A = encode-actions(Aref)`, `status = statusref`, `Cost(fn2-turn) ≤ Qturn(profile)`, `emitted-actions ≤ Aturn`. Supporting obligations: initialization, transition preservation (ownership, conservation, dependencies, semantic invariants), frame, guard safety with **no "client bytes are well formed" premise**, output correspondence, progress (budget exhaustion leaves a continuation), fusion (same scheduled transitions or a proved commutation of independent ones), crash composition (every submitted-action prefix and death cut has a recovery state), and the byte-fill theorem (an input completion exposes exactly the range written under its loan). Theorems are request-indexed ("the refused operation contributes no committed mutation"), outcomes are per operation, schedule equivalence is over observations. Core theorems establish guard safety for arbitrary client octets, modeled work and memory bounds, typed failures, reply semantics, and exact identity decisions; protected-resource isolation is a trace property; service guarantees additionally require fairness and available dependencies; whole-process safety and end-to-end durability and identity require the relevant adapter, runtime and environment contracts under the named assumptions (round 1 §3; the review).

## 5. Code generation and the three DSLs
The machine and column declarations are the single source for everything their kinds generate (DSL-METATHEORY's law; the machine's arms are written in MACHINE-FORM's step language and their cost, writes, next phases, uses and refusal routing are derived, never restated by hand). They are the single source for (a) the ACL2 stobj realization, (b) a flat ATC-compatible representation and dispatcher (integer arrays, finite tags, explicit fuel, integer persistent references, the pool passed once with offsets), and (c) the correspondence obligations between them. ATC is a qualification task, not an established route: it expects its shallow C types, its tests require trust tags, and its theorem covers only its own lowering. The first extraction experiment uses real ingredients (two machine tables, the pool, a reserved action, completion matching, a budgeted dispatcher), not scalar arithmetic; it has not been run (owed, after 5.1.1 per RELEASE-PLAN §4.2). The three DSLs (records/columns; machines with state enums, fields, accounts, continuations and budgets; command grammars) are shared declarations developed bottom-up and exercised through the first composed protocol slice before their interfaces are stabilized: the byte and store layers cannot show that a grammar abstraction handles fragmented commands, article framing, discard and STARTTLS; the first real consumer does (the review, §2).

## 6. Where the two still differ, and the ruling
- **Message-ID index at open**: the coordinator first ruled rebuild-at-open as "measured cheap"; the review showed that figure is a derived estimate ENVELOPE §2.8 lists as unmeasured, and that the objection is architectural: rebuilding a resident table couples retained identity history to RAM, contradicting rule 9. Ruling, corrected: the first served storage design uses a persistent pageable identity index with bounded replay state and explicit backpressure when maintenance falls behind; full rebuild remains a bounded-memory repair path. The storage study decides the index's physical form.
- **Bounds table**: Astra's split of the envelope's table into execution capacity, admission policy, format geometry and fault assumptions (round 2 §G) is adopted whole; ENVELOPE-AND-HOST.md's §2 is to be rewritten in those four columns before any capacity is frozen. The five floor measurements in ENVELOPE §2.8 run before the capacities they inform are frozen, not before byte-pool, simulator or machine construction begins.
- **One OS thread**: not a religious requirement. Foreign work whose supported worst-case cost fits the quantum may run inline; otherwise it runs on bounded workers with reserved capacity that return results and never mutate semantic state (round 1 §2). One semantic executor is the rule; one thread is the default realization.

## 7. Ember's rulings on the four decisions (2026-10-28, verbatim in EMBER.md)
1. **Storage backend**: NOT decided by this document. "we need to be very careful and think importantly now about how we can achieve an efficient storage design, using lessons of postgres, tahoe-lafs, and other data layers." The storage layout, the data-file question and the durable unit are settled by the storage study (`fn2/STORAGE-DESIGN-ANGLES.md`, then an Astra review), which analyses append-only logs, LSM-over-grid, heap+WAL/MVCC, COW B-trees, content-addressed capability stores, event-log-plus-views and hybrids across twelve angles. The execution model's rules 6, 8 and 9 constrain whatever is chosen; they do not choose it.
2. **Copy-on-write filesystems and other platforms**: never refused. "its our job to make software that is capable of being correct. if someone wants to deploy it in a way that is fundamentally unsound, that's on them, not us. we shouldn't go out of our way to 'refuse' things though." fn2 documents the soundness requirements it places on a filesystem or device (atomicity, ordering, barrier semantics, direct-I/O alignment) and carries no refusal logic.
3. **Compile-time ceilings**: none. "No compile-time ceilings." Data limits are the operator profile's (D27); structural capacities bound active execution state only.
4. **Unit of state**: immutable log-structured is "a good idea but is it really the BEST idea? let's analyze a few angles on it." Decided with item 1 by the storage study.

## 8. The first slice
*Where it stands:* the greeting and the stateless commands are served on a real socket (M1b; the nntp S form judges 16,730 served replies with 0 disagreements); article input reaches the transaction machine, whose admission answers `:not-served` until D5 2/n; ARTICLE read-back, XOVER, the parked consumer, TAKETHIS windows and STARTTLS admission are owed; the composition below has not yet been exercised. REGROUND §5 sets the next milestone inside this slice: a consumer appends, reads, waits and acks on the laptop, and an NNTP reader sees the same article.

Serve the greeting, GROUP, ARTICLE/HEAD/BODY/STAT, XOVER, POST and TAKETHIS, a parked remote consumer wait/poll/ack, and STARTTLS admission, over the new store, under the simulator's twenty scenarios and the carried natives, on the laptop and on hbox. Deliberately exercise composition from the first week (round 1 §4): a streaming upload committing beside a cold 16 MiB BODY, a resumable XOVER, a TAKETHIS window, a parked consumer; a stalled reader, an unfinished cancellation, a principal exhausting its own quota, crashes between dependency writes and publication. The slice answers whether fn2 keeps unrelated work moving while preserving its bytes, promises and resource guarantees; the ownership, continuation, scheduling and persistence rules answer it, not the batch.
