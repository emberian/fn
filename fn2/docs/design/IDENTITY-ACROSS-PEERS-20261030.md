# Identity across peers: the rulings (main, 2026-10-31; present state. Ember overrules by editing this file.)

Sources: UC §4.1-4.5 (two identities; identity as a Binding), STORAGE decision 6 and correction 5, WIDEST §2 and §4, EMBER.md (keep the Message-ID octets; BP is product), identity.lisp and txn.lisp as built, fn1's specs/nntp.md (POST and transit words, D25) and specs/peering.md, and Astra's read IDENTITY-ASTRA-20261030.md (its ten witnesses for E4 stand as written there).

## 0. Still decided, not reopened
Two identities because two quotients: a Message-ID is a name the poster chose; a content identity is a digest of a NAMED subject (§1). Identity is a Binding: name → set of (content identity, evidence), merged by union, so validated evidence converges in any delivery order; a conflicting offer records evidence, never vanishes; resolution never deletes; the served choice is a declared local fold. Exact Message-ID octets are permanent (ember). First-arrival acceptance, local article numbers and which content a node serves do not converge, by design.

## 1. The rulings
1. **The identity subject.** The content identity is a digest over a named subject profile with a stated extraction rule. For NNTP it is the poster's authored source as D25 defines it (recovered by the injection inverse; exact octets where the inverse cannot safely recover), never the stored relay projection (relay-v1 stays a separate, named object); for signed native objects the signed source; for sealed streams the envelope plus ciphertext, where plaintext equality is unknowable and never guessed. The wire object and the authored object keep distinct names where they differ. fn2-txn-offer stops consuming the upload's raw digest as the identity (txn.lisp:352) and consumes the subject's.
2. **Namespace, origin, content, occurrence are four keys.** The Binding key is (identity namespace, exact external name); Netnews is one explicit global namespace (RFC 5536). An acceptance's origin is (lineage, commit domain, incarnation, position). A content identity is (subject profile, algorithm, digest). An occurrence has its own event identity. No name is ever prefixed by the receiving domain: that would defeat duplicate detection. The event envelope gains the origin fields now, with the revision stamp, while adding a field is cheap.
3. **Receipts are attributed and immutable; selection is a fold.** The engine stops storing an unqualified :accepted/:conflict under (name, content). It stores binding evidence (union), acceptance receipts "domain D accepted c as m at position p" (union of attributed facts), and derives the served selection as a declared fold over both plus resolution statements. "A accepted a" and "B accepted b" then coexist. The keystone becomes: equal evidence and equal interpretation inputs ⇒ equal interpretation; the current fn2-identity-binding-converges keeps its honest scope (one node's order-independence) and is re-documented as such.
4. **Local acceptance is the base contract; no protocol tie-break.** A disconnected node accepts locally (BP is product). The protocol never promises peers converge on served bytes; a node may declare a tie-break as its own fold, visible to readers, and application profiles may restrict writers or defer acceptance. A content-hash tie-break is forbidden as a protocol rule: it gives arbitrary bytes authority, invites grinding, and changes a name's referent as evidence arrives.
5. **A valid signature alone never seizes a name; carriage is not verification.** Authority is a chain: signature valid under a named key state + signer authorized for this namespace, name and operation + grant state established + an equivocation policy. D23's carrier allowlist admits an item and does not vouch for it. For unsigned NNTP: first admissible local arrival wins locally; the conflict is visible under an explicit dissemination contract, with no claim that an eclipsed node learns of it. Under DTN an object arriving before its enrollment, grant or chain predecessor is held, never promoted; later validation adds a fact and never rewrites the historical carriage verdict. "Validated" means deterministic in the fact and its named dependencies, never in current enrollment or policy.
6. **A conflict commits evidence; refusal of the article is a separate outcome.** The transaction's current path (txn.lisp:347, 378, 689: :conflict and known :evidence → :refused, release, nothing recorded) is a defect, the exact negative example of UC §4.3. In the new form fn2-txn gains an evidence-only committed transaction with an article-refusal reply: frame the object, reserve the permanent charge, commit the evidence without article membership or a retention promise, reply with what committed; the reply waits on the commit; a replay never double-charges; known evidence is idempotent; an invalid signature, an out-of-scope object and a valid competing binding are distinct evidence classes, never one "conflict". The article's refusal and the evidence commit's outcome (committed / uncertain / refused) are reported separately.
7. **No per-name ceiling in the structure; funded admission with explicit incomplete coverage.** The four-per-id constant (identity.lisp:22, 151) is deleted as a semantic bound: a set bounded per name is not closed under union. Evidence admission is charged and reserved (work, permanent storage, retention, liability as separate dimensions) against the offerer, sponsor or peer; a refused admission leaves coverage explicitly incomplete and says so in the typed read result; the index becomes an exact (namespace, name, content) lookup plus a per-name summary plus a pageable evidence cursor, so admission never walks every competitor. Evidence is counted, not just contents.
8. **The typed read result, not an HDR item, is the migration prerequisite.** Within a pinned view the selected identity's content is never silently substituted; availability and authorization may change under the read contract (a pin is not a retention promise). Lookup by Message-ID and by article number are distinct: a re-selection never makes an allocated number name different bytes; a supersede is a new article plus a withdrawal. The read result carries selection, evidence status (locally known candidates at this cut, never a global count), availability, coverage and authorization; the HDR syntax follows with the reader surface and respects the disclosure boundary (a count reveals a competitor exists).
9. **Permanence survives reclamation; six states.** Identity, conflict knowledge, evidence dependencies and retry meaning survive the body: accepted with body / accepted, body reclaimed / conflict evidence only / withdrawn / under a custody obligation / known by foreign receipt. A re-offer of reclaimed content is :duplicate, never a new publication; an authorized repair restores bytes without a new article; a node's reclamation is no global erasure; portable evidence (the signed envelope or a proof) is retained so a new peer can validate the binding after the body is gone; for unsigned bytes a node attests observation, not authorship. Compaction carries these obligations.
10. **Control is a typed event family, not one resolution kind.** Cancel withdraws a target under an authority and scope; supersede publishes new and withdraws old; approval binds an exact article to a context; identity-resolution selects among bindings. Two cancels are not inherently equivocation; chain evidence names the stream, not merely (signer, head); cancel-before-target, including a crash between them, is decided under the receiver's authority rule as fn1's peering spec already does; `control-not-filed` stays a filing refusal.
11. **Transit.** Legacy CHECK and IHAVE keep Message-ID-only suppression as a declared loss. A fully received TAKETHIS carrying a new admissible conflict takes ruling 6's path and answers 439 with the conflict word. XFNCATCHUP carries no evidence and is not extended to; fn2 peers get a distinct evidence stream (namespace, name, typed content identity, evidence identity, issuer, dependencies, coverage tied to a history and cut), designed in E4 against Astra's ten witnesses.
12. **Replication, transport, fork.** Replicating an ordered domain is not Binding union: history, obligations, decisions and writer authority travel with it, and non-merge choices stay in Decide. Transport preserves the namespace and the receipts and changes the incarnation; an old incarnation's late writes are retained as attributable observations, never current decisions. Fork keeps shared-prefix identities, makes branch-local occurrences new, and needs its own disposition for inherited obligations.
13. **Domain ownership for the initial port.** A node is one commit domain; all local NNTP identity and cross-post membership live in it (nntp.md:702's atomic cross-post holds within it); multi-domain cross-posting is a declared protocol later, never an implicit global write.
14. **Six facts about an object stay six.** Received, structurally valid, cryptographically verified, authority-qualified, locally admitted, currently selected. Early duplicate suppression never discards independent evidence arriving with a duplicate body; a retry, a repair and a re-observation are different operations over equal bytes; importing "A accepted this" grants nothing locally; a Merkle root proves commitment, never disclosure or completeness.

## 2. What this changes, by owner
- **C (the engine):** rulings 3 and 7: the record shape (evidence, attributed receipts, selection fold; no per-name ceiling; the summary and evidence cursor); the keystone re-scoped.
- **D (fn2-txn's migration into the new form):** rulings 1, 6, 8: the subject's digest; the evidence-commit path with its separate outcome; the typed read result. The migration preserves behaviour except where these rulings change it, named in the commit (the MACHINE-FORM §4 amendment).
- **B (the envelope, with B-S2):** ruling 2's origin fields beside the revision stamp.
- **E4 (peering), when it is designed:** rulings 5, 11, 12 and Astra's ten witnesses before any convergence claim.
