**Keep decision 3; reject the chapter’s proposed amendment as written.** It turns useful placement heuristics into guarantees, and its replacement release predicate loses protections already present in the format.

1. **Fatal — The proposed root set permits premature reuse.**  
   §H explicitly protects reservations, proposed successors, outstanding borrows, unresolved recovery candidates and old submitted I/O. None is reliably represented by `{installed root, checkpoints, obligations, leases}`. A prepared payload can be unreachable from all four while its write remains outstanding. Moreover, distinguish **discarding an object’s last required copy** from **releasing an obsolete placement**: relocation permits the latter while retention and obligations remain active. This ambiguity also needs fixing in my original four-conjunct wording. Define typed ownership/dependency edges; an identity reference is not necessarily a payload-retention edge.

2. **Material — “Reachability is minimal” is not established.**  
   The counterexamples establish independent protection obligations under particular interpretations, not a minimal formula or optimal collector. Obligations may already be represented beneath the installed root; conjuncts can be combined; relocation changes reachability while preserving promises. “Freed **iff** unreachable” additionally confuses safety with collection progress. Use **only if**, then state a separate progress property under available workspace, functioning storage and fair scheduling.

3. **Material — The clock theorem confuses three contracts.**  
   fn already permits time-based membership retention. Elapsed time cannot discharge an **indefinite evidence-gated custody obligation**, but that does not prohibit explicitly time-limited contracts. Nor must every holder observe a release before its individual record can disappear: a sufficient permanent summary or an epoch-admission rule can prevent resurrection. The defensible theorem is narrower: **no finite timeout alone implements indefinite custody or permanent duplicate rejection against arbitrarily delayed reoffers**. Tahoe’s renewable leases deliberately promise something else.

4. **Material — “Recorded facts, never a proxy” excludes sound implementations.**  
   A maintained reference count, conservative bitmap or retention frontier can safely summarize facts if its invariant proves the needed implication. Conversely, recording a timestamp does not make the clock trustworthy. KAFKA-8522 condemns the *wrong timestamp semantics*, not time or summaries: its repair records the appropriate deletion horizon and still uses time. Require **authoritative evidence or a proved conservative summary**, including explicit clock assumptions. (`kafka2026-jira-kafka-8522-tombstones-survive-forever.txt`.)

5. **Material — The cleaning equation is correct; its attribution and “floor” are not.**  
   For victim capacity \(E\), copying \(uE\) survivors yields \((1-u)E\) net space: copy writes/net space = \(u/(1-u)\). Sprite’s modeled total read-plus-write cost is **\(2/(1-u)\)** when reading whole victims, with a special cost of 1 for wholly dead segments—not a measured \(1/(1-u)\). The latter counts writes including replacement user data. This is conditional accounting, not an online lower bound: waiting, spare capacity, victim choice and allocation granularity change the problem. Leases, checkpoint delays, withdrawals and age variation also create survivors; the three listed causes are not exhaustive. One policy makes zero-copy expiry possible, not universally timely. (`rosenblum1992-design-implementation-log-structured-file-system.txt`, §3.4.)

6. **Material — The open-tail “floor” is a layout assumption.**  
   With separate chains and independently sealed `S`-sized units, batch padding is  
   \(\sum_i(\lceil x_i/S\rceil S-x_i)\), less than \(gS\), and sometimes zero. Mixed-class packing is another alternative to lingering or journal inlining; it trades padding against later cleaning. “No resident buffer per class” is a memory rule. Keep this as an amplification measurement for the chosen layout, not a universal lower bound.

7. **Material — Kafka and Postgres support grouping, not the claimed release consensus.**  
   Kafka’s segment retention does not establish fn’s independent obligations. KIP-405 has distinct local and overall retention controls, and **successful remote copying gates local deletion**; that is an important dependency protocol, not merely “class = age.” The follower error is correctly described but does not supply a custody contract. (`kafka2026-docs-kip-405-tiered-storage.txt`.) Postgres partition removal avoids per-row deletion, but survivors need separate treatment, permanent identities need another retained structure, and dropping a partition takes a parent-table lock. “Zero-cost expiry” should mean **zero survivor copying**, not zero work or interference. [Postgres partitioning](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/ddl-partitioning.html).

8. **Material — RocksDB’s two TTL mechanisms are conflated.**  
   FIFO TTL may still evict unexpired files under its size limit. `DBWithTTL` supplies the separate “at least TTL; removed during compaction” behavior. These are explicit eviction contracts, not placement hints accidentally becoming authority. They do not prove that timers can never express deadlines. Periodic compaction also needs scheduling/resource assumptions before it establishes an elapsed-time bound. (`rocksdb2026-docs-fifo-compaction-style.md`; `rocksdb2026-docs-time-to-live.md`.)

9. **Material — The remaining precedents need their actual safety conditions.**  
   Git’s prune grace **mitigates rather than eliminates** concurrent-writer corruption; “fn needs no grace” follows only after complete reservation/borrow registration and writer fencing. [Git GC](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-gc). Tahoe permits deletion after cancellation or expiry, with GC disabled by default (`tahoelafs2026-docs-architecture.rst`). Cassandra’s grace is coupled to repair and compaction coverage; it is not a timer-only deletion algorithm, and its replica resurrection differs from Kafka consumers missing tombstones. [Cassandra tombstones](https://cassandra.apache.org/doc/latest/cassandra/managing/operating/compaction/tombstones.html). ZFS and S3 are useful independent-hold precedents, but authorized hold removal does not inherently certify delivery or custody completion; S3 protects particular object versions. [ZFS holds](https://openzfs.github.io/openzfs-docs/man/master/8/zfs-hold.8.html), [S3 Object Lock](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/object-lock.html).

10. **Material — Tradeoff 1 is real, but the “free hint chain” does not exist.**  
    `F_SET_RW_HINT` is **inode-scoped**, not an extent-chain or per-write placement selector. Changing it around writes to one shared file does not establish distinct chain hints. FDP placement handles identify configured reclaim-unit resources; they are not arbitrary ordered lifetime classes. Require a backend-specific mapping, handle-budget policy and safe fallback on unsupported operations. Kang’s reported gains are workload-specific; SNIA explicitly notes that host copying can reduce device WAF while increasing host writes. Measure both. (`linux2026-docs-fcntl-f-set-rw-hint.txt`; `kang2014-multi-streamed-ssd.txt`; `snia2026-introduction-flexible-data-placement-whitepaper.txt`; `sabol2023-nvme-fdp-state-of-the-union-fms.txt`.)

11. **Material — Tradeoff 2 is real; its causal billing and backpressure rule are incomplete.**  
    Cleaning has joint causes: admissions, placement choices, victim timing, leases, retention changes and obligations. There is no unique “causing principal” without an accounting convention. Charge conservative reservations and reconcile measured work; do not pretend predicted copy bytes are exact debt. Per-principal admission control also needs a **global capacity guard**: stopping one obligation kind cannot prevent ordinary writes exhausting relocation space. SILK protects urgent flush/low-level work and preempts less urgent work; one cleaner bandwidth share is insufficient. “Never reads” should mean preserving a bounded read service share, not unlimited read admission. (`balmau2019-silk-preventing-latency-spikes-lsm.txt`; `rocksdb2026-docs-rate-limiter.md`.) FlexEngine’s deferral consumes bounded memory and relies on migration; Pisces does not provide retrospective cleaning attribution. (`wang2026-flexengine-lsm-multi-tenant-serverless.txt`; `shue2012-pisces-performance-isolation-fairness-multi-tenant-storage.txt`.)

12. **Fatal — Tradeoff 3 does not implement the promised erasure.**  
    Deleting a key from current metadata leaves it in journal history, old checkpoints, snapshots, backups and possibly memory. Per-object encryption therefore needs a **separately specified, nonrecoverable key lifecycle**; otherwise it relocates the erasure problem. Sealed streams give this neither for free nor under node control: their keys belong to clients. Key destruction must respect—or explicitly supersede—outstanding service obligations; making it a fifth reclamation conjunct does not enforce that. “Erasable” also implies nothing about expected lifetime. Costs include authenticated framing, nonces, range reads and key management, not merely 32 bytes and one cipher pass. Keep erasure as a separate decision with an explicit adversary and scope.

13. **Material — Tradeoff 4 is real, but the proposed quotation is fictitiously exact.**  
    If `bytes` means surviving bytes, those bytes are the immediate copy volume; multiplying again by \(u/(1-u)\) is wrong. If it means net bytes to recover, the formula needs identified victims. Class occupancy alone cannot determine `u`, future pinning, checkpoint delay or a maximum stranding duration. Report a snapshot estimate or bound with assumptions. A retention extension must also say what happens to bodies already reclaimed. The outage reports support maintenance visibility, but Joyent particularly demonstrates **lock dependency failure**, not merely insufficient debt accounting. (`joyent2015-docs-manta-postmortem.txt`.)

14. **Material — Tradeoff 5 is substantially covered, with two missing choices.**  
    Protected admission and named refusal already belong to the execution rules. The additions are sponsorship and shared-cost allocation: a crosspost or multiply pinned object has no unique natural owner. Reserve physical capacity globally and define how principals share its accounting, including transfer when one obligation ends. “Operator waiver is the only release” is false except as the escape from an otherwise permanently unsatisfiable obligation: normal grounded completion remains a release. A dead principal’s account deletion must not delete its obligations.

15. **Material — Tradeoff 6 names a useful state but can deadlock it.**  
    §F already reserves checkpoint allocations; §G already backpressures on arena exhaustion. The missing proof is that admission preserves enough **disk, journal capacity and working memory** to finish relocation, checkpoint construction, anchor promotion and release recording. Blocking *all commits* prevents those maintenance commits and possibly the acknowledgements that discharge obligations. Pause consuming admissions while admitting reserved progress-enabling transitions. Distinguish checkpoint-blocked capacity from genuinely all-live capacity and uncertainty quarantine: a checkpoint cannot solve the latter two.

16. **Material — Tradeoff 7 has its intuition backwards.**  
    Short-lived inline content may expire before journal reclamation and require **no copy**. Permanent content eventually needs copying if its journal storage is reclaimed. The relevant variables are payload lifetime relative to journal reuse, batching, shared metadata and extra block rounding—not “short inline nothing, permanent inline freely.” Keep the threshold tunable; do not adopt per-class `S` as a theoretically justified default.

17. **Material — Tradeoff 8 invents a bound F2FS never claims.**  
    F2FS puts ordinary user data in **warm**, with selected data—including cleaned survivors—in cold. Its survivor classification is a heuristic about future updates, not a theorem about remaining retention. A block can be copied indefinitely after reaching the longest class. Even before that, surviving to near a fixed expiry can mean *less* remaining life. Use current retention/obligation information where available; evaluate survivor segregation as a heuristic. Remove the bound by number of classes. (`lee2015-f2fs-flash-friendly-file-system.txt`, §2.4.)

18. **Material — The logic section understates the proof and misattributes its precedent.**  
    GoJournal supplies atomic journal operations, including allocator-bit updates, while leaving concurrency control to its client; it does not establish the quoted universal “free durable before reuse” protocol. FSCQ’s direct contribution is disjoint ownership of free and allocated objects. (`chajed2021-gojournal-verified-concurrent-journaling.txt`; `chen2015-fscq-crash-hoare-logic.txt`.) The closer checkpoint precedent is **F2FS’s pre-free sections**, reusable only after checkpointing (§2.5). A MOVE stutters only under an abstraction that hides physical placement; proving it still requires byte/identity preservation, borrow safety, crash ordering and resource invariants. Classes also affect observable admission if they control charging. Finally, DiskSec proves confidentiality across executions under its filesystem threat model—not forensic media erasure. Key secrecy/destruction does not become an ordinary integrity property merely by assuming a cipher. (`ileri2018-sfscq-proving-confidentiality-file-system.txt`.)

**Amended wording**

> **Retention placement:** Group payloads by expected physical lifetime using placement hints; provide one default class without requiring resident state per class. Class membership never authorizes release. Backend placement hints, inline thresholds and survivor classification are optional performance policies.
>
> Distinguish logical payload discard from physical placement retirement. Discard the last required payload copy only when all applicable retention requirements and independent durable obligations permit it and no promised read still requires it. A committed relocation may retire an old copy while preserving those requirements at its replacement.
>
> Reuse an allocation only through §H’s protocol, after protection by current state, recovery dependencies, prepared successors, reservations, borrows, outstanding I/O and unresolved recovery candidates has ended. Maintain a proved conservative ownership/dependency representation; do not rescan whole state on the served path.
>
> Admission preserves globally sufficient maintenance resources and applies explicit principal/sponsor accounting. Capacity pressure has named reasons and preserves reserved progress-enabling operations and bounded read service. Cleaning and checkpoint progress are observable. Erasure requires a separate confidentiality and key-lifecycle contract.

**Four additions**

- **Time semantics:** specify arrival time versus authored time, clock jumps/restarts, retention-policy versioning, and whether extensions affect existing objects. ACL2 decides expiry from the specified inputs.
- **Race witnesses:** attach an obligation between victim selection and release; acquire a BODY lease during relocation; crash during anchor promotion; complete an old write after restart. Show the exact transition that rejects unsafe reuse.
- **Space accounting:** distinguish live bytes, reclaimable bytes, bytes blocked by recovery/borrows, relocation workspace and permanent identity metadata. Distinguish measurable backlog from predicted future cleaning.
- **A falsifying experiment:** pre-age or prefill the proposed workload; an hour-two policy change does not exercise one-day expiry by itself. Run through repeated cleaning at the longest class, compare fixed-budget placement policies, and measure host writes and NAND writes separately where telemetry permits.
