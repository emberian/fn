1. **Material — Raft does not impose one outstanding operation per client.** The extended paper presents the last-serial/last-response scheme; the thesis §6.3 explicitly generalizes it to concurrent requests, retaining response pairs until the client acknowledges a sequence-number frontier. Session expiry is another mechanism. More importantly, bounding outstanding requests does **not** preserve answers to arbitrary ancient retries: it changes which retries remain supported. Deduplication belongs in snapshots, but Raft’s session protocol is not an eternal outcome oracle. [Ongaro’s thesis, §6.3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ongardie/dissertation/refs/heads/master/online.pdf).

2. **Material — FoundationDB is an analogy, not “exactly `Summary_k`.”** Its log is partitioned by tags; storage servers advance separate durable frontiers and pop corresponding mutations. They can fetch speculative mutations before log durability and discard them during recovery. This is not one globally checkpointed semantic prefix. The split nevertheless supports the journal/materialization architecture. Automatic idempotency also needs qualification: the documented feature remains experimental and does not resolve `transaction_timed_out` or `cluster_version_changed`; it is not arbitrary application-result replay. (`zhou2021-foundationdb-unbundled-transactional-kv-store.txt`, §2.4; [FDB write path](https://apple.github.io/foundationdb/ha-write-path.html), [idempotency documentation](https://apple.github.io/foundationdb/automatic-idempotency.html).)

3. **Material — KAFKA-8522 is misdescribed.** It concerns repeated cleaning updating the **oldest segment’s timestamp**, continually resetting tombstone retention—not simply an uncleanable final segment. The faulty timestamp approximation could retain tombstones too long **or too briefly**; the recorded fix version is 3.1.0. Separately, missing an expired tombstone can leave a downstream materialization stale; Kafka does not promise permanent refusal of subsequent writes to that key. Its time-based policy implements a different contract, not an inherently unsound one. (`kafka2026-jira-kafka-8522-tombstones-survive-forever.txt`; `kafka2026-docs-design.md`, “Log Compaction”.)

4. **Material — Datomic excision is a counterexample to preserving historical observations.** The request is transactional; the actual excision runs asynchronously during indexing, outside the ordinary history timeline, removing matching datoms from indexes **and the transaction log**. The permanent excision predicate records what might have been affected, not an exact inventory of erased identities. It supports “remember that forgetting occurred,” but does not establish fn’s exact-identity or observational-equivalence contract. (`datomic2026-docs-excision.txt`, “Requesting Excision,” “Querying Excision,” “How Excision Works”.)

5. **Material — Redis rewrite is not intrinsically too weak for retry and obligation state.** It preserves the stored dataset, including application-maintained deduplication records and obligations if those are represented as keys. Its limitation is that it does not automatically preserve discarded command history. “Current values and nothing else” also overlooks expiry metadata and structured values. Modern multipart AOF explicitly uses a base snapshot plus incremental files—a particularly direct example of the proposed representation. [Redis persistence](https://redis.io/docs/latest/management/persistence/), [expiration semantics](https://redis.io/docs/latest/commands/expire/).

6. **Material — PostgreSQL wraparound does not prove that latest-only folds have no maintenance debt.** Wraparound follows PostgreSQL’s finite transaction-ID representation and visibility rules; even unchanged tables require freezing. It is not an inevitable consequence of mutable authority or historical reads. Conversely, fn’s latest-only folds still require run compaction, checkpoint advancement and safe allocation reuse. The outage mechanism is correctly identified; the architectural inference is not. Its debt is observable through documented age counters, rather than intrinsically invisible. (`postgresql2026-docs-routine-vacuuming.txt`, §24.1.5.)

7. **Material — The CRDT paragraph conflates deleting tombstones with deleting causal evidence.** Bieniusa’s optimized OR-set eliminates explicit tombstones using causal context without waiting for global stability. That context still distinguishes previously observed additions from unseen additions. Moreover, an OR-set intentionally permits a fresh add after removal; fn’s permanent identity refusal is a different specification. The obstacle is unrestricted re-offering of externally chosen identities without a usable causal namespace—not merely “peers are not replicas.” (`bieniusa2012-optimized-conflict-free-replicated-set.txt`, §4.)

8. **Material — Myhill–Nerode supplies a semantic quotient, not a storage optimum or executable audit.** For a fixed deterministic transition/output contract, equivalence under every future input continuation is the right idea. Use future **requests and environment observations**, not just already-selected semantic events: summaries must preserve which requests succeed and which events can arise. The quotient can be infinite and its equivalence undecidable. Uniqueness concerns abstract states, not byte-minimal representations. “Determines the equivalence class” is a correctness characterization, not a way to check a proposed summary.

9. **Material — The membership bound is valid; the asserted byte cost is not established.** Exact membership requires both zero false positives **and zero false negatives**; otherwise “always absent” defeats the stated bound. For arbitrary size-\(n\) subsets, \(\log_2 {u\choose n}\) is the appropriate counting bound. Mean textual length does not establish entropy: shared domains, prefixes and structured identifiers admit lossless compression. Thus retain recoverable exact octets, not necessarily verbatim copies. A finite digest alone cannot provide unconditional equality, but that does not make raw-string storage optimal. (`pagh2005-optimal-bloom-filter-replacement.txt`, introduction.)

10. **Material — Neither the operation-table bound nor the authenticated-set conclusion follows as written.** Arbitrary independent historical outcomes require growing information, but not necessarily one literal entry per operation: structured identifiers and reconstructible outcomes can compress. A finite retry contract must additionally fence retired identities against fresh execution. Likewise, a bare commitment cannot answer membership, but an ordered Merkle tree is one construction, not a necessity; a conventional sparse Merkle proof has depth tied to key width, not automatically \(O(\log n)\). State the required proof interface before choosing its implementation.

11. **Material — The per-event “device floor” mixes four different quantities.** Four KiB is the proposed journal isolation unit; 4 ms is a measured append-plus-`fdatasync` median on one stack. Neither is a universal hardware lower bound. Persistent-memory store atomicity is distinct from cache-line writeback and persistence ordering. PLP can reduce latency without reducing journal padding; faster fences can even reduce incidental batching and **increase** padding per operation. Hardware, format and batching jointly determine cost. A weaker durability tier is also possible on identical hardware, but must change the promise explicitly. (`raad2020-persistency-semantics-intel-x86.txt`; `ENVELOPE-AND-HOST.md`, §§1.1, 1.5; my review §9.)

12. **Material — Tradeoff 1 is real, but its proposed dichotomy is false.** A new fold can sometimes be initialized exactly from the existing summary: if \(F_{\text{new}}(H)=g(Summary(H))\), no original events are needed. Other folds can bootstrap from a retained snapshot plus suffix; still others genuinely require unavailable history. Require an explicit initialization/migration function and coverage boundary. Neither “starts at installation” nor “starts at oldest retained event” is universally correct. An old fold value alone is not automatically sufficient migration input, and a large migration must remain resumable.

13. **Material — Tradeoff 2 is real, partly covered, and the default rebase recommendation is too strong.** `CLIENTS-AND-DATA-MODELS.md` already specifies non-pinning consumers, explicit unavailable gaps, rebase and evidence-gated obligations. What remains is the precise history-cut/bootstrap contract. Replacing missing events with state does not preserve an event-delivery promise merely because the gap is named. Offer explicitly different subscription contracts: retained delivery versus state bootstrap with gaps. Also, retaining missing metadata need not retain every payload or unrelated event in the prefix.

14. **Material — Tradeoff 3 is covered in principle; its implementation alternatives are wrong.** Execution rule 5 already requires allocation of shared costs, and my format already assigns operation outcomes a retry lifetime. A shared ordered-run engine can charge retained bytes and maintenance to principals without separate per-principal indexes. Quotas, charging and scheduling shares solve different problems; charging alone does not bound work or storage. Specify admission reservations, expiration work and named exhaustion while preserving the shared engine.

15. **Minor — Tradeoff 4 mostly restates the ruling and overstates the implementation consequence.** Latest-only mutable folds are already explicit in my schema table. Historical queries can use retained snapshots plus replay; they do not inherently require versioned entries or PostgreSQL-style vacuum. Conversely, replaying a suffix without a valid initial state cannot answer the historical question. Keep latest-only as the first served contract, while distinguishing optional replay coverage from supported pinned article metadata. (`STORAGE-DESIGN-ASTRA.md`, §3.C.)

16. **Minor — Tradeoff 5 is already covered and its headline is absolute beyond its evidence.** My ruling explicitly preserves both cursor operations’ retry identities despite coalescing their final value. Arbitrary distinct outcomes generally require information proportional to operations, but structured IDs/outcomes can share encodings, and fewer fold writes still reduce total storage traffic. Say: “Coalescing the materialized value does not, by itself, eliminate promised operation-result evidence.” That is the useful measurement instruction.

17. **Material — Tradeoff 6 is real; “distributed transaction” and “two-phase commit” are premature.** Two domains on one node can share a coordinator or atomic commit substrate. Independent commit domains require a cross-domain atomicity protocol if that promise is offered; 2PC is one option. The first-slice restriction is sensible, but write **“each atomic mutation belongs to one commit domain”**, not “folds never span domains”: a derived read-only aggregate can span domains with an explicitly weaker consistency contract.

18. **Fatal to the proposed amendment — tradeoff 7 does not preserve exact identity or collision quarantine.** I defend retaining exact octets under the present contract. After deleting \(m\), retaining only \(h(m)\) cannot distinguish a retry of \(m\) from a different \(m'\) with the same digest. There is no remaining evidence with which to detect that collision and quarantine it. Quarantining **every** digest match would also quarantine genuine retries. Collision resistance makes failure computationally unlikely; it does not implement the existing exact decision.

    I concede the alternative is coherent **as a changed contract**: probabilistic identity under a named cryptographic assumption, with changed duplicate/conflict outcomes. It is not already authorized by content hashing or operator placement policy. Nor does an unkeyed digest assure erasure: guessable Message-IDs remain testable. Remove this option from decision 1; consider it separately only if the identity contract is deliberately revised.

19. **Fatal to the stated invariant — the acknowledged frontier has the wrong inequality.** A batch can become durable before its reply is delivered. Recovery must preserve that batch despite its being unacknowledged. For ordinary crash recovery, the recovered prefix must **contain every acknowledged operation**, and may contain additional pending ones. Furthermore, durable materialized pages can lag behind acknowledgments when the journal supplies the missing suffix; arbitrary partially installed pages need not themselves describe one prefix. GoJournal explicitly models durability as a **lower bound**. Under fn’s broader damage model, insufficient evidence instead yields `unknown`. (`chajed2021-gojournal-verified-concurrent-journaling.txt`, Fig. 7; `chajed2019-perennial-crash-safety-concurrency.txt`, §2.)

20. **Material — Durable linearizability neither supplies duplicate suppression nor equates acknowledgment with linearization.** It preserves completed operations across crashes and permits some pending operations to survive. Retry attempts must additionally be identified as one logical operation by the API and deduplication protocol. The relevant execution order is persistence, installation/visibility, then potentially much later response delivery. Strict linearizability concerns the treatment of operations interrupted by crashes; it is not “ack point equals linearization point.” (`izraelevitz2016-durable-linearizability.txt`, definitions 3 and 5 and §4.)

21. **Material — DaisyNFS supports the decomposition, not the claimed cheap proof.** Its sequential reasoning depends on GoTxn’s verified transaction interface and restrictions on shared-state access. fn must establish an analogous abstraction across preparation, dependency writes, installation, recovery and reclamation; its abstract history and concrete durable representation are emphatically different objects. **That composition is the expensive foundation.** DaisyNFS reports roughly 20× proof/code for its transaction system versus 2× for sequential filesystem proofs.

    Summary soundness is another substantial obligation, but need not be proved directly as a giant `defun-sk`: an observation-preserving inductive simulation can establish continuation equivalence. Coalescing needs ordered-sequence equivalence, not necessarily commutation—`set(1); set(2)` can collapse to `set(2)` although the original operations do not commute. (`chajed2022-daisynfs-verifying-nfs-server.txt`; `chajed2019-perennial-crash-safety-concurrency.txt`.)

22. **Material — Keep the decision; withdraw the claims of convergence and optimality.** These systems establish useful precedents with different contracts, not a theorem selecting one authority architecture. Total order also does not itself prohibit parallel preparation or independent-key work; fn’s executor policy is a separate choice. The proposed random summary-cut experiment is useful falsification, not the measurement that settles universal equivalence. Add tiny exhaustive continuations and crash cuts, targeting expired retries, conflicting identities, outstanding obligations, fold migration and consumer gaps. Measure summary size and maintenance separately; mean identifier length is not an entropy baseline.

23. **Minor — Two supporting examples need less rhetoric.** Git shallow boundaries retain parent references in boundary commits, not an inventory of every omitted ancestor’s identity. Event sourcing’s deployment pain is not uniformly schema evolution: replaying interactions with external systems is a central problem in Fowler’s account. Neither example supports the chapter’s stronger retention claims. [Git shallow documentation](https://git-scm.com/docs/shallow); `fowler2005-docs-event-sourcing.txt`, “External Systems”.

**My amended wording for §4 item 1:**

> **Unit of state:** one ordered semantic event history per commit domain, represented by a retained prefix summary and suffix, `State = Fold(Summary_k, Events_{k+1..n})`. Keyed mutations use that same commit protocol and record principal, operation identity, expected prior version where relevant, mutation and stable outcome. Within the declared retry contract, retries return the original outcome without re-execution; retirement of outcome evidence requires an explicit rule preventing stale requests from becoming fresh operations. Coalescing preserves every promised result.
>
> Internal mutable folds are initially latest-only; supported historical selections retain their required evidence. Each fold names its interpretation, initialization and coverage. New or changed folds may use existing summaries only through a valid bootstrap or migration rule; unavailable history is never implied.
>
> Discarding semantic history requires an identified summary rule preserving the retained contract’s observations and future behavior, including identity decisions, acceptance/conflict bindings, obligations, retry outcomes, consumer behavior and supported historical selections. Initially retain losslessly recoverable exact Message-ID octets and their binding evidence. A consumer cut must satisfy its declared delivery or explicit gap/bootstrap contract.
>
> A storage checkpoint, a signed checkpoint claim and permission to discard history remain distinct. Accounting and exhaustion follow the execution model; one atomic mutation does not cross independent commit domains without an explicit protocol.

**Four additions worth making:**

- **RIFL is the missing direct precedent.** It treats durable RPC results, their association with mutated objects, migration and garbage collection as one protocol. That is closer to fn’s operation-outcome problem than “Raft has a client table.” [*Implementing Linearizability at Large Scale and Low Latency*, `rifl.pdf`](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/papers/rifl.pdf).

- **Operation identity needs request binding.** Specify the outcome when one principal reuses an op-id with a different mutation or expected version. Snapshotting only `(id → result)` can erase the evidence needed to distinguish a retry from contradictory reuse.

- **External effects need durable intent and independent acknowledgment.** Replaying a fold must not resend an effect as though it were new. The event history can atomically record an obligation/outbox entry; it cannot atomically commit an arbitrary recipient’s action. This connects decision 1 directly to the retention ledger. (`fowler2005-docs-event-sourcing.txt`.)

- **Failed persistence deserves a deployment example before wraparound does.** Rebello et al. found data-loss/corruption risks across PostgreSQL, LMDB, LevelDB, SQLite and Redis; retrying `fsync` can fail because dirty pages were already marked clean. This directly motivates fn’s `uncertain` state and recovery contract, independently of whether authority is a log or heap. (`rebello2020-can-applications-recover-fsync-failures.txt`.)
