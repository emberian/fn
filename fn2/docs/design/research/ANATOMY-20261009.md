## Reachability, by three graphs

1,352 books (556K lines) are in the execution graph (included, transitively, from a host build). Of the 565 that are not: 342 (110K lines) are in the qualification graph (included by a test or tool fixture); 68 (18K) name a claim or refinement and belong to the assurance graph; 15 (6K) are the paged checkpoint and held-row models the runtime reuses; 45 (11K) are parked programs (BPSec, substrate, transfer, 9P); 2 are reclamation obligations that must move, not vanish; 71 (15K) are retire candidates (executable rungs no claim names, legacy readers, D46-unhooked); 22 (4K) await an owner naming their claim. Absence from a served image is not a defect; with fn-core as the product it is often desirable. The rule (GPT6, 2026-10-09): every maintained book belongs to the execution, assurance or qualification closure of a named supported claim, or is explicitly parked. 92 stobjs are defined, 64 used.

## The three sins

1. **Conceptual multiplication in the definitions.** Every storage concept is a ladder of rungs, each reified as books with their own invariants, cursors, census and refinement: history 120 books (59 with executable code, spread one to 26 functions each); bp-node 60 books; catalog 28 plus a 4.5K-line logic book and a 3.3K-line paged book; msgid 12 books over three representations with exec twins. The owner is a sprawl rather than a ladder: 162 books, 1,144 executable functions, 14 subfamilies. 766 executable functions car/cdr-walk octet-typed data despite D27; a 3 MiB POST changes representation about seven times between socket and log (packed-submission split/join, body-chunks join, post-body-onto, a dozen injection helpers), each a copy under the owner mutex.
2. **The host decides.** 620 host functions (16.5K lines) branch on policy with no book deciding: lock order, cache hits, cold-wait queues, the connection cap. owner.lisp has 163 threading and mutex sites, io.lisp 65, with names like `verify-under-lock`, `read-held`, `accept-locked`. The books decide what the owner does; the host decides when, under which lock, on which thread; the 52 lock keys and the failed phase-model lanes follow from that. 781 I/O primitives (16K lines) where one socket, file, barrier, thread and TLS layer needs about 3K. 1,157 thin wrappers plus 1,803 interface declarations state one fact twice.
3. **The apparatus outgrew the organism.** (Counts are reconnaissance; the objective function is bytes copied, scanned, allocated and retained per operation, mutable objects with more than one owner, and distinct mechanisms establishing the same fact.) 301K lines of registers, 45K of checkers, 130K of tests and 55K of planning prose exist so many agents could work without colliding. The checkers are a third again the size of the host they check.

## The read path, host side (host/native/extent.lisp, 98 functions)

One mutex `*fnn-extent-lock*` and about twenty globals beside it (fds, paths, incarnations, bases, cache, cache tokens, stats, issued, file holds, cold workers, window mode/worker/token/cache, run buffer, lz-last, cache-span and its buffer, no-io, direct-next, lz buffer key, dlz, retired, pending, checkpoint id). The cache is a Lisp list trimmed with `subseq` (:758, :1338) and probed with `find-if` (:1471). Cold readers wait on a condition variable under the same mutex (:1009, :1225). The verdict path coerces octets to a list before calling the book (:1271, :1283). Every one of these is a host decision; the books see none of it.

## Certify economics

Train box step at the freeze: install 150 s, certify 138 s, emit 38 s, about 5.5 min; 29 books take over 10 s, none over 30 (slowest: article-stream-owner-bridge 13.6, nntp-auth-fold 13.3, peer-pull 13.2, pagestore-keystones 13.0). The cost is count, not any one book: 1,917 books, 565 of them certified for no image, and `green_check --strict` reporting 1,354 of 3,062 certificates stale at their closure key (CONVERGE-4). Transitive certify runs at over 2x the --lane wall on a cold cache; host-ld in every train would add about 7 min. A tree of under 500 books makes every one of these numbers ordinary.

## What survives untouched

None of these moves in the surgery.

## Surgery, in order (revised after GPT6's review)

1. Settle the runtime contract (RUNTIME-MODEL §1: identified actions and completions, one executor, three buffer ownership states with handles and generations, a charged work budget with continuations, the commit point) as a short book plus an extracted multi-instance exercise.
2. One complete POST → ARTICLE → crash → reopen path from fn-core over spans and leased buffers, completion-gated, published to a reader with a retirement protocol, reopened under the admission budget; it closes the page store's open refinement for that path and finishes the target semantics beside it.
3. Expand to the real workloads (history and index queries with their own semantic invariants, consumer positions with incarnations, the web reader, BP's durable sub-machine), each landing a complete behaviour that deletes the mechanism it replaces; generators generalized from their first two working instances.
4. The host shrinks as policy moves into machines: seams, primitives and the executor loop; validation of untrusted events and external handles stays at entry.
5. The durability chain changed carefully but not last: the durability protocol is settled in step 1 and exercised in step 2.
6. The apparatus with the coordination rewrite: registers regenerated into `build/`, one checker per contract, ratchets compared against the last qualified artifact.

Targets are planning hypotheses, stated so they can be missed: executable definitions about 60K lines; host about 10K; under 500 books; every maintained book in a named claim's execution, assurance or qualification closure.
