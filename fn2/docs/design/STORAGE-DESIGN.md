# fn2 storage design: the decision (2026-10-28; present state, rewritten whole)

Settled between the coordinator and Codex (Astra) on the Opus analysis `STORAGE-DESIGN-ANGLES.md` (seven designs, twelve angles) and Astra's review-and-format `STORAGE-DESIGN-ASTRA.md`. This file is the decision; those hold the arguments, the tables and the concrete format. It sits under `EXECUTION-MODEL.md` (rules 6, 8, 9 bind it) and ember's rulings: no platform is refused, no compile-time ceilings, the unit of state analysed before chosen.

## 1. The design, in one paragraph
An **ordered semantic event journal** is the one history: articles, withdrawals, cursor acknowledgements, obligations, evidence and configuration are typed operations in one commit stream, sealed in batches under the execution model's commit-batch machine. **Payloads are separated**: an authored source streams in as immutable blocks named by content (BLAKE3 range-proof material for portable verification), written directly to final storage with journal references, never through compaction. **One pageable ordered-run metadata engine** (sorted immutable runs with pageable directories and filters, TigerBeetle/RocksDB-shaped, reserved incremental compaction) serves every keyed structure: exact Message-ID identity (the exact octets retained in permanent identity metadata; a hash may reject, never decide), article metadata, overview keyed by (group, article-number) from its first materialization, mutable folds (cursors, accounts, pins, signer heads), obligations and location mappings. **Physical page integrity** is parent-held page hashes plus an explicit previous-batch digest; **semantic integrity** is a Merkle event log whose typed checkpoint claim (history identity and incarnation, event count and root, fold/schema identity, summary commitment, exact-identity commitment, checkpoint descriptor, signing key) is signed at each checkpoint. A **pinned version** is a number giving stable selection and identity; a reply acquires an **object lease** before promising to stream, and only a bounded window occupies the byte pool. **Reclamation** releases an allocation only when every retention requirement is discharged, every durable obligation released, no active lease needs it, and recovery no longer needs the old allocation; retention classes are placement hints, never release evidence. **Recovery** classifies intact / torn tail / damaged / unknown from evidence under the declared fault profile; under the broad profile an unexplained bad final batch is `unknown`, not `torn tail`; no local format detects complete rollback without an independent witness.

## 2. What was decided against, and why
- **A second mutable store beside the log** (Postgres-shaped heap for the hot state): rejected; mutable keyed objects are folds whose updates ride the same commit stream, so one durability protocol, one recovery, one accounting. Postgres's real contributions (WAL separating commit latency from writeback, out-of-line large values, visibility maps) are taken as lessons, not its MVCC/vacuum machinery.
- **Commit-order overview plus a later per-group reclusterer** (the Opus recommendation's layout): rejected; overview enters the ordered engine under (group, number) from the start, which removes a subsystem and the ~1,000-read XOVER on cold groups.
- **A resident Message-ID hash rebuilt at open**: already ruled out by EXECUTION-MODEL §6 (paged identity index; rebuild as repair); also `(tag, sequence)` is not exact identity once payloads are reclaimed.
- **"Pins cost zero"** and "only an honest flush is required": both too strong (see §3).
- **A COW metadata tree** (LMDB-shaped, but with object leases, parent-held hashes and the same journal): not rejected; kept as the **comparison implementation** (Astra's counter-design, STORAGE-DESIGN-ASTRA §4) because its advantages (one navigable structure, no merge policy, less replay at open) are real and its cost (distinct dirty metadata pages per batch including the allocator) is a measurement, not a verdict.

## 3. The eleven corrections to the analysis (STORAGE-DESIGN-ASTRA §1), all adopted
1. "The log is the only authority" is well-defined only with the summary equation `State = Fold(Summary_k, Events_{k+1..n})` and the obligation that every retained-contract observation of the full fold equals the summary fold; a storage checkpoint, a signed checkpoint claim, and permission to discard history are three different things (D13).
2. Recovery tables must not narrow the fault model: a newer root with a missing dependency is `unknown` under acknowledged-write loss, since "fence never succeeded" and "fence succeeded, then the dependency was lost" leave identical bytes; four superblocks import none of TigerBeetle's replicated evidence.
3. A Merkle log does not replace the explicit previous-batch digest; their subjects differ (semantic inclusion vs physical recovery).
4. `install` is a handle swap of an already-constructed successor state (execution rule 8); the second prepared batch is built against the first's proposed successor, never both against the old root (or account updates are lost).
5. Exact Message-ID octets, content identity, acceptance binding and conflict state are permanent identity metadata.
6. Resident fences, filters, manifests, bitmaps, tails and Merkle frontiers are themselves paged under rule 9; u64 counters need an extension scheme, "585,000 years" is not a ruling.
7. Pins: a version still needs the metadata to answer at that version; later reclamation turning a readable article into `unavailable` changes the connection-isolation claim and is adopted explicitly; three retention mechanisms stay distinct (durable obligations; logical version; physical leases); a BODY acquires its storage lease before its success status; "space held ≤ article size" needs relocation or sub-extent allocation.
8. Alternatives are compared with object leases, not database-wide snapshots; LSM levels can move tables by metadata; Postgres is not a strawman.
9. Numbers corrected: an isolated durable ack costs one 4 KiB journal unit (≈860 MB/day at 210,000/day) unless operations overlap or deliberately wait, so group commit's value is the measurement; the 64 MiB tail's "30 ms" excludes decoding, hashing, index reconstruction and dependency reads; the other seven corrections stand as written.
10. BLAKE3 range verification needs the reconstruction algorithm specified (chaining values vs root output, partial final blocks, small objects); content identity excludes allocation address and generation, so physical page hashes are separate from source verification.
11. The platform requirements are: an honest barrier, exclusive writer ownership, a declared write-damage radius, correct completion and borrow handling, valid addressing, durability of file growth and namespace changes where they occur; documented per backend, never refused; an I/O error after persistence became ambiguous is `uncertain`.

## 4. Ember's five decisions, amended by the scholar (STORAGE-DECISIONS-SCHOLAR.md; each kept, none replaced; each recommended yes)
Preface, from the chapters jointly: *Erasure is a sixth decision, not yet made: it needs its own adversary, scope and key lifecycle, and it interacts with the identity contract below.* *Every amended paragraph names what it does not establish (correct folding, query completeness, freshness, erasure, confidentiality by key absence); those negative clauses are part of the decision.* The original rulings and reasons are in STORAGE-DESIGN-ASTRA.md §2; the state of the art, the theoretical optima, the unconsidered tradeoffs and the proof principles behind each amendment are the scholar's chapters 1-5.

1. **Unit of state**: one ordered semantic event history per commit domain, a retained prefix summary plus suffix, `State = Fold(Summary_k, Events_{k+1..n})`; keyed mutations use the same commit protocol and record principal, operation identity, the request's binding (mutation and expected prior version), and stable outcome. Within the declared retry contract a retry returns the original outcome without re-execution and a contradictory reuse of an operation identity is refused by name; retiring outcome evidence requires an explicit rule that fences retired identities against fresh execution; unexpired operation identities are a per-principal reservation with a named exhaustion. Coalescing preserves every promised result, including every recorded outcome. Internal folds are latest-only; a historical fold question is answered from a retained snapshot plus replay, charged as maintenance. Each fold names its interpretation, initialization and coverage; a new or changed fold may use an existing summary only through a valid bootstrap or migration rule, and unavailable history is never implied. External effects are recorded as obligations or outbox entries released by evidence, never re-performed by replay. Discarding semantic history requires an identified summary rule, proved as an observation-preserving simulation against the retained contract's observations and future requests (identity decisions, acceptance and conflict bindings, obligations, retry outcomes within their contracts, consumer delivery contracts, supported historical selections); initially the summary retains losslessly recoverable exact Message-ID octets with their binding evidence, and obligation evidence. A consumer cut satisfies the consumer's declared contract: retained delivery under a charged, inactivity-bounded obligation, or state bootstrap with an explicit gap. A storage checkpoint, a signed checkpoint claim and permission to discard history remain three distinct acts. One atomic mutation belongs to one commit domain.

2. **Pinned versions**: a supported pin identifies a published logical version, history and incarnation, selection semantics and supported query scope. While valid it preserves selection and identity through physical reorganization; the engine retains sufficient evidence for those answers, which requires neither every intervening change nor any old physical root. Consumer query and view versions and durable scan positions are distinct from storage snapshot pins and create no implicit retention hold; a retaining consumer is a charged, inactivity-bounded durable hold. Profiles define pin expiration and rebase rules; expiration, protected holds and any promised historical-read window jointly determine which observations compaction must preserve, derived from the supported observations and closed over their dependencies; expiration takes effect before its evidence is discarded and never changes an already-started response. A cursor preserves logical scan progress through the last examined key and re-seeks safely after suspension; cached physical traversal state is optional, charged and bounded. Before promising payload success a reply acquires a logical object lease atomically against release; physical reads and sends hold generation-checked borrows until the adapter releases them; relocation preserves the leased content identity and publishes durable replacement placement before retiring old storage, and reuse additionally waits for all old borrows and recovery dependencies. Replies refine the reference semantics for explicit availability, rebase, authorization-change and fault outcomes, each with a stated disclosure; a valid lease prevents reclamation-induced loss of a promised payload under the declared platform assumptions.

3. **Retention placement**: group payloads by expected physical lifetime using placement hints; one default class, no resident state per class; class membership never authorizes release. Backend placement hints (mapped per backend to write-lifetime hints or placement handles, with a safe fallback), inline thresholds and survivor classification are optional, measured performance policies. Distinguish logical payload discard from physical placement retirement: discard the last required copy only when all applicable retention requirements and independent durable obligations permit it and no promised read still requires it; a committed relocation may retire an old copy while preserving those requirements at its replacement; reuse an allocation only through the allocator protocol, after protection by current state, recovery dependencies, prepared successors, reservations, borrows, outstanding I/O and unresolved recovery candidates has ended, using a proved conservative ownership and dependency representation never rescanned on the served path. Every release input is committed evidence or a proved conservative summary; time enters only through specified inputs (arrival versus authored time, clock jumps, policy versions). No finite timeout alone implements indefinite custody or permanent duplicate rejection; time-limited contracts are declared as such. Admission preserves globally sufficient maintenance resources and applies explicit principal and sponsor accounting (shared objects, a global physical reserve, obligations surviving account deletion, operator waiver only for unsatisfiable obligations); cleaning is reserved conservatively and reconciled against measured work, with SILK-shaped priorities and a bounded read service share under pressure. Capacity pressure has named reasons (checkpoint-blocked, all-live, uncertainty quarantine), pauses consuming admissions and admits reserved progress-enabling transitions. A live policy change reports a bounded estimate with its assumptions; cleaning and checkpoint progress, live bytes, reclaimable bytes, bytes blocked by recovery or borrows, relocation workspace and permanent identity metadata are separately observable. Erasure is a separate decision with its own adversary, scope and key lifecycle.

4. **Integrity**: parent-held physical hashes binding allocation context; explicit predecessor commit and seal links in every sealed batch, maintenance batches included; and a domain-separated append-only Merkle event log with a pageable frontier and proof material. A versioned typed checkpoint statement binds history and incarnation, an explicit event cut and root, fold/schema identity, summary and exact-identity commitments, an acyclic checkpoint descriptor and the authorized signing keys; for transparency interoperability it is logged as a statement about an already determined predecessor cut and authenticated by inclusion under the enclosing standard three-line checkpoint (origin naming the history; size; root), under a declared interoperability profile (tree hash, signature acceptance rule, encodings, counter bridge); neither the statement nor its descriptor references the enclosing root or seal. Checkpoint inclusion establishes neither correct folding nor query completeness, availability or freshness; portable source verification (BLAKE3 range proofs) is a separate contract. Local durability, signed coverage and policy-accepted witnessed coverage are distinct; shared checkpoints are published at a declared cadence with freshness, only covering checkpoints support inclusion receipts, and no checkpoint is minted per client. Witnesses atomically verify extension and persist their frontier before replying; the acceptance policy states its fault assumptions, the honest-quorum-intersection requirement between every pair of accepted quorums, key activation, overlap, revocation and distribution, and its transition rules. Recovery reconstructs candidates through anchors and journal before comparing independent frontier evidence; contradictory evidence prevents normal rollback; witness coverage is an explicit lower bound. Compatible restoration preserves lineage and never signs before the frontier is re-established; an irrecoverable discontinuity requires separately authorized lineage establishment under a new origin and never cancels prior promises, identities or obligations. Integrity material is retained according to declared recovery and proof-service obligations; any confidential-opening scheme states its disclosure and erasure limits, and deleting a salt or key does not erase previously disclosed associations or independently retained identity metadata.

5. **Sealed streams**: client-encrypted opaque content with an explicit public envelope (Message-ID, signer, stream, size, and application-selected public fields). The node authenticates the immutable envelope and ciphertext under the ordinary signed-record contract, holds no application decryption keys and indexes only deliberately public fields; its signature check establishes endorsement of bytes, not correct encryption. The supported client profile binds the canonical immutable pre-encryption context to the ciphertext (as associated data and under the outer signature, with the inner signature binding context where the application signs inside) and requires recipients to verify that binding; it specifies authenticated streaming chunks, nonce derivation, truncation detection and range reads, symmetric AEAD, key establishment and signatures separately, with confidentiality horizons and agility. Leakage is declared separately for nodes, relays and their collusion (envelope fields, lengths, commit order and arrival times, observable traffic, withdrawals and acknowledgements) as a simulatability claim under stated assumptions. Append and fetch authorization, current cryptographic membership and historical key possession are distinguished. Public signers are the initial profile; hidden-signer designs require a separate authorization and evidence contract. Key establishment, membership, rotation, padding and encrypted client indexes belong to the application protocol; storage order supports an MLS delivery service only with an explicit client integration protocol. Node acceptance and recipient acceptance are distinct, with undecryptable signed objects, conflicts and charges resolved by name. Server search over sealed plaintext does not exist; search over public fields does. Retention follows declared requirements and confidentiality horizons; encryption alone promises neither erasure nor permanent secrecy. Verification operates on ciphertext commitments without decryption; Tahoe's time-expiring leases do not replace evidence-gated release.

6. **Identity after erasure (ember, 2026-10-28, verbatim: "keep the Message-ID octets and let the operator otherwise deal with it").** Exact Message-ID octets are retained permanently in the identity metadata, with content identity, acceptance binding and conflict state, so the duplicate check and collision quarantine stay exact (D25, OBJ-001) and the exact-identity theorem keeps its subject. Erasure of an identity is an operator matter outside the node's contract: the node documents that identities persist after bodies are reclaimed; it builds no digest-only identity and no identity-aging window. The lookup structures are FILTERS-2026.md: Ribbon (or Binary Fuse) per sealed run, InfiniFilter for the active overlay, a measured perfect hash inside a run; the "forgotten-identity set" of keyed digests in that file is withdrawn.

## 5. What the builder has (STORAGE-DESIGN-ASTRA §3, normative in structure, provisional in values)
The concrete format: identities, order and encodings; the journal unit and batch seal; payload blocks and manifests; the ordered-run engine's page encoding, directories, filters, merge rules per schema, and installation; successor-root construction with two prepared batches; the allocator with history incarnation, writer epoch, physical allocation generation, logical version and checkpoint generation kept distinct so stale bytes never masquerade as current; root selection; and the exact on-disk invariants recovery checks for each of intact / torn tail / damaged / unknown, with the two scope qualifications (a constant-time open proves only what it replayed; rollback is undetectable without an independent witness).

## 6. The measurements that decide what the arguments cannot (STORAGE-DESIGN-ANGLES §14 as amended by Astra)
M1 shared-run engine vs the COW comparison under identical payload, integrity and retention semantics, counting journal padding, metadata, allocator, anchors and cleaning separately; M2 cold sparse groups past cache capacity, charging directory and overlap reads; M3 exact Message-ID lookup after bodies and journal placements are reclaimed; M4 stranded disk, leased object storage, borrowed pool memory and retained historical metadata as four quantities; M5 correlated thirty-consumer bursts vs isolated durable acks; M6 two prepared batches, an interrupted checkpoint, a newer singleton anchor, corrupted final-batch dependencies, an independent later receipt, stale reused blocks, a crash during recovery, with dependency verification measured apart from journal scanning; M7 the filesystem experiments, published as observed barrier and alignment behaviour, no refusal logic.

## 7. The decisive first-slice demonstration
A checkpoint or relocation occurs while two successive batches are prepared, a cold BODY is leased, a cursor ack commits, and expiry attempts to reuse space; the process is killed at every write, fence and install boundary; recovery preserves every established promise, identifies genuine ambiguity as `unknown`, and never reuses storage still required by the selected history or an outstanding borrow.
