## The essay's ideas, as they bear on fn2
A persistent semantic image with a checked language of evolution: two coordinates (program revision, execution history); the operations execute / edit / migrate / materialize / fork, each with explicit compatibility obligations; "revising a computation" (recompute from new code) vs "evolving an activity" (keep the past, change future behaviour) vs "migrating the state" (change representation, keep meaning); a representation change needs a simulation relation `τ(step_P(s)) ≈ step_P'(τ(s))`; a feature change needs instead a declaration of what survives (identity, pending requests, ownership, observations); entity identity ≠ revision identity ≠ presentation name (Unison); version-pinned activations (Julia world age); continuation migration as a stronger, explicit operation; observations remain valid as "this happened under revision r17" while becoming invalid as evidence about r18; exploratory branches use recorded effects, promotion into the live world is distinct; sources: *Operation-based versioning as a foundation for live executable models* (SoSyM, Oct 2024, read-after-write dependencies), Hazelnut Live (arXiv 1805.00155), Sketch-n-Sketch bidirectional evaluation (1809.04209), Theseus (OSDI 20), Kitsune (DSU), Julia world age (2010.07516), Chordata shortcut memoization (arXiv 2603.19560, March 2026), Blaze. Fetch these into ~/paperbin.

fn2 is such a world: the journal is the execution history; fold and projection versions, format migrations and machine-kind revisions are the program-revision axis; a projection rebuild is "revise the computation"; a fold version change with a declared migration is "migrate the state"; a new Decide function from a position onward is "evolve the activity"; the simulator's branches are the exploratory forks with recorded effects; promotion by referencing commit is the live-world promotion.
