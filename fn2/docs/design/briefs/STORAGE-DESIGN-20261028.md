## The designs to analyze (fetch what ~/paperbin lacks: Tahoe-LAFS paper and docs, Postgres storage internals (heap/MVCC/WAL/checkpoint/vacuum/TOAST/visibility map), LMDB (Chu), Bitcask, SQLite WAL, FoundationDB's storage, Kafka compacted topics; use Scry/Kagi/curl as the papers brief did; save into ~/paperbin with the existing naming)
1. **Append-only log with derived indexes** (fn1's shape; Bitcask; Kafka): the log is authority, indexes are rebuilt or checkpointed.
2. **Log-structured with an LSM over a grid** (TigerBeetle: fixed-size grid blocks, a manifest, compaction with pinned readers, checksums everywhere, one file).
3. **Heap pages plus WAL with MVCC** (Postgres: in-place pages, tuple versions, a visibility map, vacuum, TOAST for large values, checkpoints, full-page writes against torn pages).
4. **Copy-on-write B+tree with two root slots** (LMDB: single-writer, readers never block, no WAL, no compaction, free-page reuse; what it costs for large values and for write amplification).
5. **Content-addressed immutable objects with capabilities** (Tahoe-LAFS: immutable vs mutable files, verify/read caps, merkle trees, erasure-coded shares across servers, the "least authority" model). Relevant because the clients want signed records, delegated narrowed rights and peer replication; ask what of it applies to a single node and what to a peer group.
6. **Event log plus materialized views / compacted overlays** (Kafka compacted topics; event sourcing): the log is the truth, mutable keyed state (cursors, accounts) is a fold materialized and checkpointed.
7. **Hybrids** worth naming: log for articles + heap/B-tree for the small hot state; a per-group segment layout; SQLite-style WAL with checkpoint-into-main.

## The angles, each as a section with a table across the designs
A. Write amplification, read amplification, space amplification for the actual workload (2 KiB POSTs at 100-1,000/s; ARTICLE by number and by Message-ID; XOVER ranges; a 16 MiB body).
B. Crash recovery: cost at open (replay vs root switch), what the tougher fault model (corruption at rest, misdirected I/O, lost writes after fsync, reordering under direct I/O) does to each design, and how each detects damage (checksum placement, merkle roots, chained trailers).
C. Pinned-version reads under concurrent commits and under cleaning/compaction: who pins what, what cleaning must wait for, worst-case space held by a stalled reader.
D. Retention and expiry: retiring whole ranges of old articles; whether the design makes expiry a cheap range drop or a compaction; evidence-gated release (pins).
E. The small hot mutable state (cursors, accounts, subscriptions, limits): where it lives in each design and what one ack costs (a 2 KiB log record per ack is the wrong answer; say what the right one is).
F. Large values: tens-of-MiB articles streamed in and out without residency; TOAST-style out-of-line vs in-log extents vs separate blob files.
G. Verification and integrity: per-block checksums vs merkle trees vs chained trailers; what each lets recovery and a reader prove; cost per read.
H. Capabilities and signed records (from Tahoe and the clients): can the storage layer carry verify/read capabilities per object or per stream so narrowed rights are a property of the data rather than the server's ACL? What does that cost?
I. Replication and peers: what the design gives a peer that pulls a stream (offsets? content hashes? erasure shares?), and what BP/DTN carriage needs.
J. Filesystem and device soundness: what each design requires of the platform (write atomicity, ordering, fsync semantics, direct I/O alignment, COW filesystems' behaviour with in-place updates and with large files, ZFS recordsize), stated as requirements to document, never as refusals; and the robigalia case (raw block device, no filesystem).
K. Simplicity and verifiability: how many invariants, how many moving parts, what a refinement proof would have to say, and how the design maps onto the execution model's commit-batch machine and recovery states.
L. Namespaces without compile-time ceilings: how each design pages articles, groups, consumers and history through bounded caches (EXECUTION-MODEL rule 9) with explicit exhaustion.
